## 2. Statutory & Procedural Architecture: Why Section 36(b) (`15 U.S.C. § 80a-35(b)`) Bypasses FRCP Rule 23.1 Demand Futility and the PSLRA Automatic Discovery Stay to Pry Open Section 15(c) Board Valuation Decks

### 2.1 Statutory Extension of Section 36(b) to Business Development Companies
Under **Section 60 of the Investment Company Act of 1940 (`15 U.S.C. § 80a-59`)**, the provisions of Section 36 (`15 U.S.C. § 80a-35`) apply to a Business Development Company to the same extent as if it were a registered closed-end investment company. **Section 36(b) (`15 U.S.C. § 80a-35(b)`)** imposes an express federal statutory fiduciary duty upon the investment adviser:

$$\text{Duty}_{\text{36(b)}}: \quad \text{Investment Adviser} \longrightarrow \text{Fiduciary Duty with respect to the receipt of compensation for services}$$

Crucially, an action under Section 36(b) may be brought Either by the SEC or **by a security holder of the registered investment company or BDC on behalf of such company** against the investment adviser, any affiliated person of the investment adviser, or any enumerated officer/director/depositor/underwriter for breach of fiduciary duty in respect of compensation or payments of a material nature paid by the company to the adviser or its affiliates.

### 2.2 The Triple Procedural Asymmetry: Rule 23.1 Exemption, PSLRA Stay Inapplicability, and Section 15(c) Production
Standard shareholder litigation challenging asset markups in public corporations or REITs faces an almost insurmountable pre-discovery barrier under the PSLRA (`15 U.S.C. § 78u-4(b)(1)–(2)`)—requiring particularized scienter pleadings prior to any document production while `15 U.S.C. § 78u-4(b)(3)(B)` imposes an automatic stay of all discovery during the pendency of any motion to dismiss—and under **FRCP Rule 23.1**, which requires derivative plaintiffs to plead particularized facts demonstrating that a majority of the board of directors lacks independence to consider a pre-suit litigation demand.

Section 36(b) alters this game-theoretic equilibrium through three statutory overrides:

1. **Elimination of Rule 23.1 Demand Futility (*Daily Income Fund, Inc. v. Fox*):** In *Daily Income Fund, Inc. v. Fox*, 464 U.S. 523 (1984), the U.S. Supreme Court unanimously held that because a registered investment company itself possesses no statutory right of action to sue under Section 36(b) (the statute confers standing exclusively on the SEC and security holders), a shareholder's Section 36(b) suit is not a traditional derivative action enforcing a corporate right within the scope of FRCP Rule 23.1. Therefore, **a BDC shareholder is never required to make a pre-suit demand on the BDC's board of directors or plead demand futility**.
2. **Inapplicability of the PSLRA Automatic Discovery Stay:** The PSLRA automatic discovery stay (`15 U.S.C. § 78u-4(b)(3)(B)` and `15 U.S.C. § 77z-1(b)(1)`) is statutorily restricted to private actions arising under the Securities Act of 1933 or the Securities Exchange Act of 1934. Because Section 36(b) is codified exclusively under the Investment Company Act of 1940 (`15 U.S.C. § 80a-35(b)`), the PSLRA automatic stay does not attach as of right, and pleading fraud or scienter under FRCP Rule 9(b) is not an element of the cause of action.
3. **The Section 15(c) & Rule 2a-5 Discovery Lever under *Jones v. Harris* and *Gartenberg*:** Under *Jones v. Harris Associates L.P.*, 559 U.S. 335 (2010), adopting the Second Circuit's framework in *Gartenberg v. Merrill Lynch Asset Management, Inc.*, 694 F.2d 923 (2d Cir. 1982), an investment adviser breaches Section 36(b) when it charges a fee that is "so disproportionately large that it bears no reasonable relationship to the services rendered and could not have been the product of arm's-length bargaining." Under *Jones v. Harris*, 559 U.S. at 351–52, judicial deference to the independent directors' approval of the advisory contract under **Section 15(c) (`15 U.S.C. § 80a-15(c)`)** is strictly conditional:
   - Where the board's process was deficient or **where the investment adviser withheld material information or provided misleading valuation/exposure data during the annual Section 15(c) contract-renewal process**, courts must discount board approval and scrutinize the underlying fee mechanics directly.
   - Because **SEC Rule 2a-5 (`17 C.F.R. § 270.2a-5`)** permits the BDC board to designate the external investment adviser itself as the "valuation designee" for ASC 820 Level 3 illiquid loans (subject to Section 2(a)(41), `15 U.S.C. § 80a-2(a)(41)` good-faith fair valuation), an adviser that inflates Level 3 fair values, reclassifies distressed software loans into non-software categories, or capitalizes uncollectible paper PIK into the fee base simultaneously fabricates the denominators and numerators presented to the board during the Section 15(c) renewal review.

## 3. Closed-Form Cybernetic & Accounting Model

### 3.1 EQ-1: Cash-Fee-on-Paper-PIK Liquidity Drain & RIC Distribution Trap
Let a BDC elect tax treatment as a Regulated Investment Company (`RIC`) under Subchapter M of the Internal Revenue Code (`26 U.S.C. § 852(a)(1)`), which requires the BDC to distribute in cash at least $\delta_{\text{RIC}} = 0.90$ (`90.0%`) of its "investment company taxable income" (`ICTI`) each taxable year to avoid corporate-level income tax and the `4.0%` nondeductible excise tax under `26 U.S.C. § 4982`. Under Original Issue Discount (`OID`) accrual rules (`26 U.S.C. §§ 1272–1273`), non-cash Payment-in-Kind (`PIK`) interest capitalized into loan principal is included immediately in both GAAP Net Investment Income (`NII`) and taxable `ICTI`, yielding **zero** cash inflow ($\text{CashInflow}(\text{PIK}) = 0$).

Let $\gamma_{\text{inc}} \in [0.175, 0.200]$ denote the external adviser's contractual cash incentive fee rate on pre-incentive NII above the hurdle rate (`17.5%` for OBDC/OTF; `20.0%` for legacy 2-and-20 BDC structures), and let $\text{PIK}_t$ denote the dollar volume of non-cash PIK interest accrued in period $t$. For each `$1.00` of paper PIK interest accrued, the immediate **Cash Liquidity Drain** ($\text{CashDrain}_{\text{per\_PIK}}$) extracted from the BDC's cash reserves or revolving credit facility is:

$$\text{CashDrain}_{\text{per\_PIK}}(\gamma_{\text{inc}}, \delta_{\text{RIC}}) = \gamma_{\text{inc}} + \delta_{\text{RIC}} \cdot (1 - \gamma_{\text{inc}}) = \delta_{\text{RIC}} + \gamma_{\text{inc}} \cdot (1 - \delta_{\text{RIC}})$$

Plugging in the statutory RIC minimum $\delta_{\text{RIC}} = 0.90$:

1. **At the OBDC / OTF Incentive Fee Rate ($\gamma_{\text{inc}} = 0.175$):**
   $$\text{CashDrain}_{\text{per\_PIK}}(0.175, 0.90) = 0.175 + 0.90 \times (1 - 0.175) = 0.175 + 0.7425 = 0.9175 \quad (\text{91.75 cents per } \$1.00 \text{ PIK})$$
   Applied to OTF's Q1 2026 non-cash PIK interest of $\text{PIK}_{\text{OTF, Q1}} = \$43.0\text{M}$, the single-quarter cash incentive fee paid to the adviser on paper PIK is:
   $$\text{Fee}_{\text{Inc, PIK, Q1}} = \$43.0\text{M} \times 0.175 = \$7.525\text{M} \quad (\text{or } \$8.60\text{M at } \gamma_{\text{inc}} = 0.200)$$
   And the total single-quarter cash liquidity drain forced on OTF by `$43.0M` of paper PIK is:
   $$\text{TotalCashDrain}_{\text{OTF, Q1}} = \$43.0\text{M} \times 0.9175 = \$39.4525\text{M}$$

2. **At the Standard 20.0% BDC Incentive Fee Rate ($\gamma_{\text{inc}} = 0.200$):**
   $$\text{CashDrain}_{\text{per\_PIK}}(0.200, 0.90) = 0.200 + 0.90 \times (1 - 0.200) = 0.200 + 0.7200 = 0.9200 \quad (\text{92.00 cents per } \$1.00 \text{ PIK})$$

Furthermore, once `$1.00` of paper PIK is capitalized into loan principal and marked near par ($\mu_{\text{PIK}} \approx 1.0$), it enters the gross-assets denominator for all subsequent quarters in perpetuity, generating an ongoing annual cash base management fee ($\gamma_{\text{base}} = 0.015$, or `1.50%`) of $\$0.015 \cdot \mu_{\text{PIK}}$ per year until the loan defaults or is written down.

### 3.4 EQ-4: Section 36(b) Effective Cash Incentive Rate (`23.33%` / `26.67%`) & `$690.4M` 1-Year Statutory Disgorgement Cap
When a BDC's pre-incentive NII consists of $(1 - \alpha_{\text{PIK, NII}})$ cash NII and $\alpha_{\text{PIK, NII}}$ non-cash paper PIK NII, the adviser charges its contractual nominal incentive rate $\gamma_{\text{inc}}$ on the combined sum (`Cash NII + Paper PIK`), but collects `100%` of the fee in **hard cash**. The **Effective Cash Incentive Fee Rate** ($\gamma_{\text{eff\_cash}}$) extracted from actual cash NII is therefore:

$$\gamma_{\text{eff\_cash}}\left(\gamma_{\text{inc}}, \alpha_{\text{PIK, NII}}\right) = \frac{\gamma_{\text{inc}} \cdot \text{NII}_{\text{pre}}}{\text{NII}_{\text{cash}}} = \frac{\gamma_{\text{inc}}}{1 - \alpha_{\text{PIK, NII}}}$$

Evaluating OTF in Q1 2026 ($\text{NII}_{\text{pre, implied}} = \$172.0\text{M}$, $\text{PIK}_{\text{Q1}} = \$43.0\text{M}$, $\text{NII}_{\text{cash, Q1}} = \$129.0\text{M}$, $\alpha_{\text{PIK, NII}} = 25.0\%$):
- At $\gamma_{\text{inc}} = 17.5\%$:
  $$\gamma_{\text{eff\_cash}}(17.5\%, 25.0\%) = \frac{17.5\%}{1 - 0.25} = \frac{17.5\%}{0.75} = \frac{\$30.10\text{M}}{\$129.0\text{M}} = 23.33\%$$
- At $\gamma_{\text{inc}} = 20.0\%$:
  $$\gamma_{\text{eff\_cash}}(20.0\%, 25.0\%) = \frac{20.0\%}{1 - 0.25} = \frac{20.0\%}{0.75} = \frac{\$34.40\text{M}}{\$129.0\text{M}} = 26.67\%$$
