## 1. State the Case — The Exoteric Porch ($\mathcal{N}_{\text{Exo}}$) vs. Esoteric Core ($\mathcal{N}_{\text{Eso}}$) Antinomian Wrapper Architecture

### 1.1 The Enforcement Vacuum of Extra-Legal Rentier Cartels

An extra-legal rentier cartel — a coalition that extracts tolls from capital, labor, or information flows through coordinated exclusion rather than productive contribution — suffers one irreducible structural defect: its collusion agreements are void as against public policy in every constitutional court. No judge will enforce a contract to fix prices, allocate markets, bury adverse evidence, or divide the proceeds of fraud. Schelling (1960) showed that collusion stabilizes around focal points only when the parties possess some enforcement technology; in a constitutional legal order, the cartel is denied the state's enforcement monopoly precisely for the agreements that generate its rents.

The finitely repeated cartel dilemma therefore unravels by backward induction: in the final period every member defects; anticipating this, every member defects in the penultimate period; the induction carries defection back to period one. Multi-period collusion survives only if the cartel manufactures its own credible-commitment technology outside the court system. The antinomian wrapper is that technology: a concentric 3-ring architecture that wraps the cash-and-collateral engine inside a tax-exempt and fraternal shell stack, substituting statutory privilege, costly initiation, and hostage collateral for judicial enforcement.

## 3. Stigmergic `Publish-as-PoW` and Constructive Duplicate Amplification

### 3.1 Zero-C2 Publication and First Amendment Maximization

The classical bounty clearinghouse is a single point of failure: it holds the task queue, the payer identity, and the submission channel, and each is subpoenaable. The stigmergic alternative sets both coordination counters to zero:

$$C2 = 0, \qquad \text{Tokens\_Chat} = 0$$

No command-and-control channel; no private chat tokens. Each hunter node solves independently, writes independently, and publishes public-first. The only coordination is the shared public record itself — the stigmergic trace. This construction maximizes First Amendment protection along two axes:

- ***Bartnicki v. Vopper*, 532 U.S. 514 (2001):** a publisher who lawfully obtains information from public records cannot be civilly liable for publishing it, even where a third party obtained it unlawfully, so long as the publication concerns a matter of public concern and the publisher did not participate in the interception.
- ***New York Times Co. v. Sullivan*, 376 U.S. 254 (1964):** defamation claims by public figures require proof of actual malice — knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard. Deterministic citation of e-filed 990 XML, `EO BMF` rows, and court dockets leaves no factual predicate for actual malice.

With $C2 = 0$ there is no conspiracy to indict, no clearinghouse to seize, and no task queue to characterize as solicitation. Each publication is a standalone proof-of-work.

### 3.2 Theorem 3 (Constructive Duplicate Amplification vs. Tournament Waste)

**Statement.** Where $M$ independent nodes publish convergent findings on the same entity graph, three compounding effects make the swarm's output strictly more valuable than any single tournament winner's output, while the adversary's suppression cost scales linearly to exhaustion.

**1. Bayesian False-Positive Collapse.**

$$P(\text{Hallucination} \mid M) = \epsilon^M \to 0$$

If each independent derivation carries false-positive rate $\epsilon$, and the derivations are conditionally independent given the underlying records, then $M$ convergent publications drive the probability that the finding is an artifact to $\epsilon^M$. Duplicate publication is not redundancy waste; it is the Bayesian verification mechanism.

**2. SLAPP Litigation Exhaustion.**

$$C_{\text{SLAPP}}(M) = M \cdot c_{\text{Litigation}} \to \infty \land P(\text{Suppression} \mid M) = (p_{\text{Gag}})^M \to 0$$

Each additional independent publisher adds one full litigation front at cost $c_{\text{Litigation}}$ to the wrapper, while the probability of gagging all $M$ publishers decays geometrically in $p_{\text{Gag}} < 1$.

**3. RAG Latent Corpus Fixation.**

$$W_{\text{RAG}}(\text{Entity Graph} \mid M) \propto \sum_{m=1}^{M} \text{CitationCentrality}(m) \cdot \text{DeterministicCodeVerification}(m)$$

Retrieval-augmented generation systems weight entities by citation centrality in their training and retrieval corpora. Each convergent publication, weighted by its citation centrality and its deterministic code-verifiable proofs, fixes the entity graph deeper into the latent corpus of every downstream model. Suppression after fixation would require retraining the corpus; the wrapper's only remaining option is to out-publish the swarm, which it cannot do without confessing the graph.

**Numerical sanity check.** With $p_{\text{Gag}} = 0.60$ and $c_{\text{Litigation}} = \$500{,}000$:

| $M$ | $P(\text{Suppression} \mid M)$ | $C_{\text{SLAPP}}(M)$ |
|---|---|---|
| $1$ | $0.60$ ($60\%$) | $\$500{,}000$ |
| $20$ | $(0.60)^{20} = 0.00003656$ ($0.0037\%$) | $\$10{,}000{,}000$ |

At $M = 20$ the wrapper faces a `$10,000,000` litigation bill to suppress a finding that survives with probability $1 - 0.00003656$. The tournament model (one funded winner, one publication, one litigation front) hands the wrapper a 60% suppression probability for `$500,000`. Constructive duplication inverts the game.

### 3.2.1 Corollary 3A (Common-Mode Fault-Domain Collapse — the Independence Precondition)

The exponent in $P(\text{Suppression} \mid M) = (p_{\text{Gag}})^M$ is valid **only** under conditional independence of the $M$ publication channels. If all $M$ nodes push to one code host, front their domains through one CDN, register through one registrar, and settle through one payment rail, then a single upstream terms-of-service action, a `17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(3)` notice-and-takedown, or one third-party subpoena is a **common-mode event** that removes many nodes at once. The effective $M$ collapses toward the number of distinct fault domains, not the number of nodes. The `$10,000,000` in the sanity check above is the $\varphi = 0 \land G = M$ limit — not a default.

**Fault domain.** Define the fault-domain tuple for node $j$:

$$D_j = (\text{CodeHost},\ \text{Transport},\ \text{Registrar},\ \text{HostingJurisdiction},\ \text{PaymentRail},\ \text{AuthorDomicile})$$

Let $G$ be the number of **distinct** fault domains across the $M$ nodes, $m_{\text{dom}} = M/G$ the mean nodes per domain, and $\varphi \in [0,1]$ the fraction of domains whose removal is achievable by a *counterparty action* — a ToS termination, a `§ 512(c)(3)` takedown notice, a registrar lock, a custodian freeze — rather than by a full litigation front at cost $c_{\text{Litigation}}$.

**Design-effect collapse (Kish 1965).** Applying the survey-sampling design effect to clustered publication channels:

$$M_{\text{eff}} = \frac{M}{1 + \varphi\left(\dfrac{M}{G} - 1\right)}, \qquad P(\text{Suppression} \mid M) = (p_{\text{Gag}})^{M_{\text{eff}}}$$

**Adversary cost floor.** The wrapper's minimum suppression spend becomes:

$$C_{\min}^{\text{Suppress}} = \varphi G \cdot c_{\text{Upstream}} + (1-\varphi)\, M \cdot c_{\text{Litigation}}$$

where $c_{\text{Upstream}} \ll c_{\text{Litigation}}$ is the cost of one upstream counterparty action.

**Diversity Invariant.** Theorem 3's $(p_{\text{Gag}})^M$ is recovered exactly when

$$\varphi = 0 \quad \land \quad M \ge G \ge G_{\min} = \left\lceil \frac{\ln p^*}{\ln p_{\text{Gag}}} \right\rceil$$

— that is, when no domain is removable by counterparty action and the distinct fault domains (and nodes) are numerous enough to drive suppression below the target $p^*$. At $p_{\text{Gag}} = 0.60$ and $p^* = 0.01$: $G_{\min} = \lceil \ln 0.01 / \ln 0.60 \rceil = \lceil 9.01 \rceil = 10$.

**Numerical sanity check.** $M = 20$, $p_{\text{Gag}} = 0.60$, $c_{\text{Litigation}} = \$500{,}000$, $c_{\text{Upstream}} = \$5{,}000$:

| Configuration | $G$ | $\varphi$ | $M_{\text{eff}}$ | $P(\text{Suppression})$ | $C_{\min}^{\text{Suppress}}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Naive — 1 code host, 1 CDN, 1 registrar | $1$ | $1.0$ | $1.00$ | $0.60$ | $\$5{,}000$ |
| Partial — 5 ToS-terminable domains | $5$ | $1.0$ | $5.00$ | $0.07776$ | $\$25{,}000$ |
| Mixed — 10 domains, half ToS-terminable | $10$ | $0.5$ | $13.33$ | $0.00110$ | $\$5{,}025{,}000$ |
| Hardened — 10 zero-ToS domains | $10$ | $0.0$ | $20.00$ | $0.00003656$ | $\$10{,}000{,}000$ |

The naive row is the entire critique of Theorem 3 in one line: `20` nominally independent publishers behind a single counterparty are suppressible for `$5,000` — a `2,000x` collapse from the `$10,000,000` the hardened row imposes. Headcount is not immunity.

**Achieving $\varphi = 0$.** A domain is non-terminable when no single counterparty can remove the artifact by administrative action:

- **Transport:** self-hosted static origin, `IPFS` / BitTorrent v2, `Nostr` relays, signed git bundles, `RSS`, and physical dead-drops — none of which has a ToS counterparty holding unilateral removal power over the artifact. Where a `§ 512(c)(3)` notice *can* reach a host, `§ 512(g)` put-back, the *Lenz v. Universal Music Corp.*, 815 F.3d 1145 (9th Cir. 2016) good-faith-belief requirement, and `§ 512(f)` misrepresentation liability jointly raise $c_{\text{Upstream}}$ toward $c_{\text{Litigation}}$ and convert the notice itself into a liability for the wrapper.
- **Hosting jurisdiction and registrar:** diversity across jurisdictions that will not domesticate the wrapper's home-court injunction.
- **Author domicile:** *Bartnicki* and *Sullivan* are United States doctrines. Nodes domiciled outside the US must be counted as separate fault domains, because a US judgment does not travel automatically.
- **Payment rail:** Rail 1 settlement keys must not share a custodian, or the payment rail becomes a shared fault domain even when the publication transport is fully diverse.

**Operational rule.** $M$ is not a headcount. $M$ is the number of fault domains the adversary must attack *separately*, and the swarm's SLAPP immunity is purchased by spending on **domain diversity**, not on node count. Theorem 4 below prices this directly: it pays a per-domain bounty for exactly this good.

### 3.3 Defeating the Subscription Chokepoint via 3 Permissionless Cashflow Rails

Centralized recurring fiat processors — Stripe, Patreon, PayPal — are the subscription chokepoint: they operate as `Shell_3`-class de-platforming surfaces, terminating merchant accounts under wrapper-adjacent pressure and exposing subscriber lists to subpoena. The swarm routes around this chokepoint on three permissionless rails:

- **Rail 1 — Ex-Post Permissionless Patronage & Retroactive Public-Goods Zaps.** No standing subscription, no merchant account, no recurring charge. Value flows ex-post, permissionlessly, to published proofs-of-work that the patronage graph retroactively selects. There is no account to freeze because there is no account.
- **Rail 2 — Direct Statutory Whistleblower Filings.** `26 U.S.C. § 7623(b)` (IRS Form 211), `31 U.S.C. § 5323` (FinCEN), and `31 U.S.C. § 3730` (FCA qui tam) pay hunter nodes directly from collected proceeds. This rail is not processor-dependent; it is a statutory liability of the United States and cannot be de-platformed.
- **Rail 3 — *Dirks v. SEC* Public-Research Balance-Sheet Positioning.** Under *Dirks v. SEC*, 463 U.S. 646 (1983), trading on information assembled from public sources and disclosed research — without breach of duty by a tipper — is not insider trading. Hunter nodes may take balance-sheet positions consistent with their published findings; the publication itself is the safe-harbor record.

### 3.4 Theorem 4 (The `Publish-as-PoW` Settlement Gate) and Corollary 4A (Enforcement-Oracle Impossibility)

Rail 1 pays ex-post for published proofs-of-work. That raises the gating question: **what event makes a zap payable?** The intuitive answer — "pay when the government acts" — is formally inadmissible. Corollary 4A proves why; Theorem 4 supplies the admissible gate.

One clarification before the formalism, because it is the most common misreading of this section: **the gate is a machine pre-filter, not an autonomous payout engine.** In Rail 1 there is no smart contract and no escrow. A human patron — or a fund's analyst — reads the dossier, decides whether the finding is materially significant, and sends the zap. `G1–G4` does not replace that judgment; it makes the judgment *affordable*. Theorem 4 is therefore a statement about **verification-cost asymmetry**, not about automated settlement.

#### Corollary 4A (Enforcement-Oracle Impossibility)

**Statement.** A settlement oracle $O$ is swarm-safe only if it satisfies all four conditions:

| # | Condition | Formal requirement |
|---|---|---|
| (i) | **Bounded latency** | $\exists\, T_{\max} < \infty$ such that $O$ resolves within $T_{\max}$ of publication |
| (ii) | **Artifact determinism** | $O = f(\text{PoW}_j)$ — a pure function of the published artifact, computable offline by any node |
| (iii) | **Adversary independence** | $\partial O / \partial a_{\text{Wrapper}} = 0$ — the wrapper cannot move $O$ by any action cheaper than suppressing the publication itself |
| (iv) | **Publication monotonicity** | $\partial O / \partial M \ge 0$ — more publication never reduces the payout |

Government enforcement — an indictment, a `§ 4941` assessment, a `501(c)(3)` revocation, a deferred-prosecution arrangement, a settlement — violates **all four**:

- **(i) fails.** Enforcement latency is unbounded, and under `26 U.S.C. § 7623(b)(1)` and `26 C.F.R. § 301.7623-4(d)(2)` it is collection-gated on top of that (Corollary 2B-A). $T_{\max}$ does not exist.
- **(ii) fails.** Enforcement is a function of prosecutorial discretion, examination budget, and politics — not of the artifact. Two byte-identical proofs can receive opposite oracle values.
- **(iii) fails, fatally.** The wrapper *can* move the oracle: settle quietly, enter a non-prosecution agreement, dissolve the entity, pay the excise tax without admission, or lobby the examining function. Worse, the oracle's availability depends on a state actor the swarm does not control, which reintroduces exactly the single point of failure that $C2 = 0$ was constructed to eliminate — a **payout SPOF**. Attacking the payout SPOF is strictly cheaper for the wrapper than attacking $M$ publications, so a rational adversary always attacks the oracle instead of the swarm.
- **(iv) fails, inverted.** An enforcement gate rewards **withholding**. Where payout requires a government action that publication can preempt — the `31 U.S.C. § 3730(b)(2)` seal requirement, the `§ 3730(e)(4)` public-disclosure bar, and the `26 U.S.C. § 7623(b)(2)(A)` reduction to $\le 10\%$ for claims derived from prior news-media disclosures by non-original-source filers (`§ 7623(b)(2)(B)`) are the statutory instances — publishing *reduces* expected payout: $\partial O / \partial M < 0$. This directly inverts Theorem 3, whose entire value depends on maximizing $M$. An enforcement-gated bounty is therefore not merely slow; it is **anti-stigmergic** — it pays nodes to suppress one another.

**Conclusion.** Enforcement is inadmissible as a *gate*. It remains fully admissible as a *free additive option* (§4 below). The correct answer to "should a zap be gated on government enforcement?" is: **no — gate on the artifact, and hold enforcement as an option that can only ever pay extra.**

#### Theorem 4 (The Admissible Settlement Gate)

**Statement.** Let $\text{PoW}_j$ be node $j$'s published artifact: specification, `JSON` schema, pure-stdlib verification script, and the SHA-256 manifest binding them. The settlement gate is the conjunction of four **artifact-local** predicates:

$$\text{Gate}_j = G1_j \land G2_j \land G3_j \land G4_j$$

$\text{Gate}_j$ is a **necessary condition, never a sufficient one**. It certifies that the artifact is reproducible, sourced from real government records, novel, and independently mirrored. It does *not* certify that the finding matters. The payout rule is therefore two-layered:

$$\text{Zap}_j = \underbrace{\text{Gate}_j}_{\text{machine — necessary}} \;\land\; \underbrace{\text{PatronJudgment}_j}_{\text{human — sufficient}}$$

| Gate | Name | Predicate | Cost to verify |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G1** | **Reproducibility** | `exit_code(verify_j) = 0` $\land$ `SHA256(stdout_j) = H_Manifest` — the proof runs to completion on pure stdlib with zero network access and reproduces the committed hash | seconds, local |
| **G2** | **Source provenance** | every leaf record in the artifact's derivation graph is an element of $\mathcal{S}_{\text{Gov}}$ — `26 U.S.C. § 6104` XML, `EO BMF`, `ICIJ` node registers, `DOJ FARA`, county deed, `PACER` — and each leaf's content hash matches the published source hash | seconds, local |
| **G3** | **Novel edges** | $\lvert E_j \setminus E_{\text{Corpus}} \rvert \ge \theta_{\text{Novel}}$ — the artifact contributes at least $\theta_{\text{Novel}}$ entity-graph edges absent from the indexed corpus | seconds, local |
| **G4** | **Fault-domain independence** | $D_j$ (Corollary 3A) is not already claimed by a settled artifact — the node adds a *distinct* fault domain, not a duplicate mirror of an existing one | seconds, local |

All four are pure functions of the artifact plus public reference data. None requires a state actor, a custodian, a chat channel, or the wrapper's inaction. Conditions (i)–(iv) of Corollary 4A are satisfied **by construction**: latency is one falsification window (§3 below); determinism is $G1 \land G2$; adversary independence holds because no predicate reads any input the wrapper controls; and monotonicity holds because G4 pays *for* domain diversity, so $\partial O / \partial M \ge 0$.

**Why the machine layer exists at all — verification-cost asymmetry.** A human patron cannot skip verification, only cheapen it. Without `G1–G2`, a patron weighing a `$36,000` zap must manually pull the underlying `§ 6104` XMLs, re-derive the joins, and check for hallucinated or doctored line items before daring to send — roughly $T_{\text{Manual}} \approx 40$ hours of analyst time. With `G1–G2`, a script confirms record provenance and hash parity in $T_{\text{Machine}} \approx 2$ seconds, and the human's remaining task collapses to the one thing no script can do — judging material significance — in $T_{\text{Judgment}} \approx 5$ minutes:

$$\frac{T_{\text{Manual}}}{T_{\text{Machine}} + T_{\text{Judgment}}} = \frac{144{,}000\text{s}}{302\text{s}} \approx 477\times$$

The gate does not remove the human from the loop. It removes `99.8%` of the human's *cost*, which is what makes ex-post patronage at scale economically possible at all. Absent it, patronage does not become automated — it simply does not happen, because no patron will spend 40 hours vetting one unsolicited dossier.

**What the machine layer cannot do.** `G1–G4` is satisfiable by garbage. A node can join five unrelated dentists' LLCs to a rotary club and clear `G1` (the script runs), `G2` (the records are genuine government filings), `G3` (five novel edges), and `G4` (a fresh mirror) — and the finding is still worthless. This is not a defect to be engineered away; it is precisely why $\text{PatronJudgment}_j$ stays in the conjunction. Any design that treats $\text{Gate}_j$ as *sufficient* is a spam faucet, and will be drained within a day.

**1. Two-tranche bounty split.** The bounty $B_j$ for a settled finding splits into a discovery tranche and a per-domain verification tranche:

$$B_{\text{Swarm}} = \underbrace{B_D \cdot \mathbb{I}[G1 \land G2 \land G3]}_{\text{discovery — paid once}} \;+\; \underbrace{G \cdot b_{\text{Domain}} \cdot \mathbb{I}[G1 \land G2 \land G4]}_{\text{verification — paid per distinct fault domain}}$$

The discovery tranche is paid **once**, to the first artifact clearing $G1 \land G2 \land G3$. The verification tranche is paid to *every* subsequent node that clears $G1 \land G2$ and adds a new fault domain under G4. Verification is priced **per domain, capped at $g^*$, and never split**. Splitting a fixed bounty across $M$ claimants is the tournament anti-pattern: it makes $\partial O / \partial M < 0$ and re-imports the very competition Theorem 3 abolishes.

**2. Optimal domain cap $g^*$.** Paying $b_{\text{Domain}}$ for each additional domain is efficient only while that domain's marginal suppression-cost value exceeds its price. The marginal value of the $g$-th domain is $(1 - p_{\text{Gag}}) \cdot L_{\text{Finding}} \cdot p_{\text{Gag}}^{\,g-1}$, so:

$$g^* = \left\lfloor 1 + \frac{\ln\!\left(\dfrac{b_{\text{Domain}}}{(1-p_{\text{Gag}})\,L_{\text{Finding}}}\right)}{\ln p_{\text{Gag}}} \right\rfloor$$

At $p_{\text{Gag}} = 0.60$, $L_{\text{Finding}} = \$1{,}960{,}000$ (the Theorem 2A annual clearance value), and $b_{\text{Domain}} = \$1{,}000$: $g^* = 14$. Domain 14's marginal value is $\$1{,}024 \ge \$1{,}000$ (pay); domain 15's is $\$614 < \$1{,}000$ (stop).

**3. Falsification window and zero-cost patron refusal.** Settlement is not instant-final; it is **challenge-final**. After gate clearance the patron waits a falsification window $T_{\text{Challenge}} = 30$ days before sending. During that window any node may publish a counter-proof; because G1 and G2 are deterministic and public, anyone can re-run the artifact and detect fabrication with probability approaching one ($p_{\text{Detect}} \approx 1$).

In Rail 1 there is **no escrow and no bond to slash** — and none is needed. The patron has not yet paid, so the waiting period *is* the security: if a falsification proof clears the public log inside 30 days, the patron simply does not zap, at a cost of `$0`. The claimant's penalty is reputational and durable — the key is burned in the public log and every future patron skips it.

$$C_{\text{Patron}}^{\text{Refusal}} = \$0, \qquad p_{\text{Detect}} \approx 1 \implies \text{no bond required}$$

A literal slashable bond ($\text{Bond}_j \ge B_j / p_{\text{Detect}}$) belongs only to the scripted on-chain bounty-vault construction in `SPEC-STIGMERGIC-ZK-OSINT-BOUNTY-SETTLEMENT-v1.0.md`, where a custodial escrow actually exists to hold it. Importing bond mechanics here would violate $C2 = 0$ and buy nothing, since the patron's exposure before payment is already zero.

$T_{\text{Challenge}} = 30$ days is the *only* latency in the entire settlement path — and it is a constant chosen by the swarm, not a random variable chosen by a prosecutor.

**4. Enforcement as a monotone top-up, never a gate.** Statutory recovery, when it eventually arrives, is added on top:

$$\text{Payout}_{\text{Total}}(j) = B_j \cdot \mathbb{I}[\text{Gate}_j] + \sum_e \beta_e \cdot \mathbb{I}[e], \qquad \beta_e \ge 0$$

where $e$ ranges over enforcement events — a `§ 7623(b)` award, a `§ 5323` award, an FCA relator share, a `§ 1964(c)` treble recovery. Every $\beta_e$ is non-negative and additive, so enforcement can only *increase* the payout and can never gate, delay, or reverse the swarm settlement. The swarm is paid at $T \approx 0$ on the artifact; the state's slower, adversary-exposed oracle is a free out-of-the-money option the node holds (Corollary 2B-A, Rail 2).

**Numerical sanity check.** With $B_j = \$50{,}000$ (the Theorem 2A $C_{\text{Patronage}}$ budget for one finding), $b_{\text{Domain}} = \$1{,}000$, $g^* = 14$, $p_{\text{Gag}} = 0.60$, $\theta_{\text{Novel}} = 5$:

| Term | Value |
|---|---|
| Verification tranche $B_V = g^* \cdot b_{\text{Domain}}$ | $14 \times \$1{,}000 = \$14{,}000$ |
| Discovery tranche $B_D = B_j - B_V$ | $\$50{,}000 - \$14{,}000 = \$36{,}000$ |
| Budget reconciliation vs. Theorem 2A | $B_D + B_V = \$50{,}000 = C_{\text{Patronage}}$; $\text{ROV} = 39.2\times$ preserved |
| $P(\text{Suppression} \mid G = 14,\ \varphi = 0)$ | $(0.60)^{14} = 0.00078364$ ($0.078\%$) |
| Discovery node net ($C_{\text{Solve}} = \$500$) | $\$36{,}000 - \$500 = +\$35{,}500$ ($71\times$) |
| Replicating domain node net ($C_{\text{Solve}} = \$5$) | $\$1{,}000 - \$5 = +\$995$ ($199\times$) |
| Settlement latency | $T_{\text{Challenge}} = 30$ days (constant, swarm-chosen) |

The gate pays the discoverer `$35,500` net within 30 days, pays each of 14 independent fault-domain replicators `$995` net for re-running a public script, drives suppression probability to `0.078%` as a *byproduct of paying for verification*, and never once asks what the Department of Justice is doing.

### 4.0 The Conjunctive Funnel Precision Law (Why Stage 1 Is a Recall Filter, Not a Classifier)

Stages 1–4 are a **sequential conjunctive funnel**, not four independent classifiers. Read in isolation, Filter 1.1 (`>= 3` foundations at one `ICO`/`STREET`) is a high-recall, low-precision candidate generator: run alone against the ~1.8M `EO BMF` rows it fires on every legitimate wealth-management philanthropy desk, registered-agent plant, donor-advised-fund sponsor, and big-4 accounting floor in the country. **That is by design.** Precision is a property of the *composition*, and it is governed by the terminal discriminator, not by the first filter.

Let $\pi$ be the base rate of true wrapper subgraphs in the candidate population, and let stage $k$ have sensitivity $s_k$ (true-positive pass rate) and false-positive pass rate $f_k$. After $K$ conjunctive stages:

$$\text{Precision}_{1 \to K} = \frac{\pi \prod_{k=1}^{K} s_k}{\pi \prod_{k=1}^{K} s_k + (1-\pi) \prod_{k=1}^{K} f_k} \xrightarrow{\;f_3 \to 0\;} 1$$

Because the $f_k$ **multiply**, a single near-zero stage dominates the product. Stage 3's inner join against `ICIJ` / `DOJ FARA` / county-deed registers is that stage: an exact normalized-name-and-address match between a `Form 990` officer and an offshore-leaks node register is not a heuristic, so $f_3 \approx 0$ and terminal precision is governed by $f_3$ — **not** by $f_1$.

**Numerical sanity check.** $\pi = 0.01$ over the 40,000-row address-clustered cohort that Stage 1 draws from the ~1.8M `EO BMF` file — 400 true wrapper subgraphs against 39,600 benign collisions:

| Stage | TP | FP | Survivors | Precision | $s_k$ | $f_k$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Filter 1.1 / 1.2 / 1.3 (`EO BMF` triage) | 400 | 39,600 | 40,000 | `1.00%` | $1.00$ | $1.00$ |
| Stage 2 (`990` / `990-PF` XML tomography) | 360 | 840 | 1,200 | `30.0%` | $0.90$ | $0.0212$ |
| Stage 3 (`ICIJ` $\bowtie$ `FARA` $\bowtie$ deeds) | 360 | 0 | 360 | `100%` | $1.00$ | $0.0000$ |
| Stage 4 (excise / RICO classification) | 360 | 0 | 360 | `100%` | $1.00$ | $0.0000$ |

Stage 1 is a **pure recall filter**: at $s_1 = f_1 = 1.00$ it passes the entire 40,000-row cohort at `1.00%` precision, and it is *supposed to* — its only job is to lose none of the 400. The funnel discards `99.1%` of that cohort by Stage 4 ($40{,}000 \to 360$). The `39,600` benign collisions Stage 1 passes — every Bessemer Trust, Wilmington Trust, and Rockefeller Financial suite in the file — are annihilated at Stage 3, where the join requires an actual offshore node-register hit. Stage 2's 1,200 survivors are the subset carrying a full `Form 990` / `990-PF` XML footprint worth deep-parsing; the remainder are `Foundation Code 10` and `Form 990-N` rows whose *absent* footprint is itself the Filter 1.2 signal.

**Cost corollary.** The funnel is cheap precisely because the imprecise stage is the cheap one:

$$C_{\text{Funnel}} = 40{,}000(\$0.005) + 1{,}200(\$0.10) + 360(\$0.50) = \$200 + \$120 + \$180 = \$500$$

The `$0.005` is per-row CSV canonicalization and group-by over the Stage-1 cohort; the `$0.10` is per-XML deep parse over the 1,200 rows that have one; the `$0.50` is per-*hit* exhibit pull-down and normalization for the 360 Stage-3 matches, the hash join itself being near-free against pre-indexed `ICIJ` / `FARA` / deed tables. The total is exactly the $C_{\text{Solve}} = \$500$ assumed in Theorem 2B. The `$500` is not an arbitrary round number; it is the funnel's own arithmetic.

**Corpus-internal proof.** `590 Madison Ave` fires Filter 1.1 because `Rockefeller Financial` is a legitimate family-office desk hosting many unrelated foundations — a true positive of the *filter* and a false positive of the *thesis*. Only Join 3.1 (`The Morningstar Foundation c/o Rockefeller Financial` $\bowtie$ `Glilot Capital Partners I [Node 82014304]`) discriminates. The address collision is the **question**; the offshore node-register join is the **answer**.

**Benign-collision exclusion list (cost optimization only — never a classifier).** The following address classes are known high-volume Filter 1.1 collisions. Skipping their Stage 2 XML parse saves compute; it must **never** be used to exclude an entity from Stage 3, because a wrapper can and does co-locate inside them:

- Big-4 and national accounting suites; law-firm estate and trust-administration desks.
- Donor-advised-fund sponsors: `Fidelity Charitable`, `Schwab Charitable`, `Vanguard Charitable`, `Silicon Valley Community Foundation`.
- University mail stops and fiscal-sponsor addresses.
