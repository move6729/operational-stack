## Section 2: Transformer Prefill vs. Decode Roofline Physics & Falsifying the "Speculative Decoding" Excuse

### 2.1 Why Speculative Decoding, Medusa Heads, and Draft Models Cannot Lower Prefill FLOPs

The standard provider rebuttal—"we run aggressive speculative decoding / Medusa heads / draft models, so our marginal cost is lower than your roofline"—fails on the physics of the two transformer phases:

- **Decode** is **memory-bandwidth bound** ($\mathcal{O}(P_{\text{active}})$ per token): each generated token must re-stream all active weights from HBM3e. Speculative decoding, Medusa heads, and EAGLE-style draft models genuinely accelerate this phase by trading cheap draft FLOPs for verifier bandwidth relief.
- **Prefill** processes all $N_{\text{tok}}$ prompt tokens in parallel and is strictly **compute-bound (FLOP-bound)** on GPU tensor cores:

$$\text{FLOPs}_{\text{Prefill}}(N_{\text{tok}}, P_{\text{active}}) = 2 \cdot N_{\text{tok}} \cdot P_{\text{active}} + 4 \cdot n_{\text{layers}} \cdot d_{\text{attn}} \cdot N_{\text{tok}}^2 \ge 2 \cdot N_{\text{tok}} \cdot P_{\text{active}}$$

(EQ-1)

No draft model, Medusa head, or speculative verifier can lower the $\ge 2 \cdot N_{\text{tok}} \cdot P_{\text{active}}$ lower bound: every prompt token must be fully forward-propagated through every active parameter of the model **at least once**. Speculative decoding is a *decode*-side trick; it does not touch the prefill FLOP floor. Any price below this floor is by construction subsidized from another pocket.
