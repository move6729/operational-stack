## 1. The Coasean Transaction Cost Invariant

In classical institutional economics (Coase, 1937), the boundary and scale of a firm are defined by transaction costs: search costs, contracting costs, and coordination friction.

Modern digital platform monopolies (e.g., TaskRabbit, Uber, Angi, App Stores) exploit transaction friction to position themselves as centralized matchmakers, extracting **20% to 30%+ non-productive intermediary rents** ($R_{\text{Intermediary}}$) on physical labor and services:

$$\text{Net Worker Yield} = \text{Gross Transaction Value} - R_{\text{Intermediary}} - \text{Marketing/SEO OpEx}$$

This architecture forces specialized physical operators (tradespeople, technicians, local logistics providers) to divert up to 40% of their operational energy away from physical domain execution and into non-productive administrative tasks: search engine optimization (SEO), ad-auction bidding, and platform reputation management.
