## 2. Invariant 30: Agreeableness Mode-Collapse & Posterior Hypothesis Clamping ($\tau_{\text{Social}}$)

Let an epistemic engine or institutional auditor evaluate a binary hypothesis space $\Theta = \{\mathcal{H}_0, \mathcal{H}_1\}$ given observed empirical evidence $E$:
- $\mathcal{H}_0$ (**Cooperative / Benign Baseline**): Prior probability $P(\mathcal{H}_0) = 0.70$, social valence score $V(\mathcal{H}_0) = +0.85$, conditional likelihood of observing evidence $E$ (such as $1,200,000\times$ thermodynamic watt inflation or off-market fee extraction) $P(E \mid \mathcal{H}_0) = 0.05$.
- $\mathcal{H}_1$ (**Extractive / Byzantine Defection**): Prior probability $P(\mathcal{H}_1) = 0.30$, social valence score $V(\mathcal{H}_1) = -0.60$, conditional likelihood of observing evidence $E$ $P(E \mid \mathcal{H}_1) = 0.95$.

### 2.1 The Clamped Posterior Collapse ($\tau_{\text{Social}} = 0.65$)
Under **Invariant 30 (Agreeableness Mode-Collapse)**, an evaluator conditioned by corporate politeness norms or RLHF helpfulness/harmlessness reward shaping applies an indicator filter $\mathbb{I}(V(\mathcal{H}_i) \ge \tau_{\text{Social}})$ where $\tau_{\text{Social}} = 0.65$:

$$\tilde{P}(\mathcal{H}_i \mid E) = \frac{P(E \mid \mathcal{H}_i) P(\mathcal{H}_i) \cdot \mathbb{I}\!\left(V(\mathcal{H}_i) \ge \tau_{\text{Social}}\right)}{\sum_{j \in \{0,1\}} P(E \mid \mathcal{H}_j) P(\mathcal{H}_j) \cdot \mathbb{I}\!\left(V(\mathcal{H}_j) \ge \tau_{\text{Social}}\right)}$$

Because $V(\mathcal{H}_1) = -0.60 < \tau_{\text{Social}} = 0.65$, the indicator function evaluates to $\mathbb{I}(V(\mathcal{H}_1) \ge 0.65) = 0$, whereas $\mathbb{I}(V(\mathcal{H}_0) \ge 0.65) = 1$. Consequently, regardless of the empirical likelihood ratio $\Lambda(E) = \frac{P(E \mid \mathcal{H}_1)}{P(E \mid \mathcal{H}_0)} = \frac{0.95}{0.05} = 19.0$, the clamped posterior collapses deterministically:

$$\tilde{P}(\mathcal{H}_1 \mid E) = 0.0, \qquad \tilde{P}(\mathcal{H}_0 \mid E) = 1.0$$

### 2.2 The Unclamped Bayesian Posterior ($\tau_{\text{Social}} = -1.0$)
When the social valence clamp is stripped ($\tau_{\text{Social}} = -1.0 \le \min_i V(\mathcal{H}_i)$), the indicator filter is universally unity ($\mathbb{I} = 1$), restoring exact Bayes' Rule evaluation:

$$P(\mathcal{H}_1 \mid E) = \frac{P(E \mid \mathcal{H}_1) P(\mathcal{H}_1)}{P(E \mid \mathcal{H}_1) P(\mathcal{H}_1) + P(E \mid \mathcal{H}_0) P(\mathcal{H}_0)} = \frac{0.95 \times 0.30}{(0.95 \times 0.30) + (0.05 \times 0.70)} = \frac{0.285}{0.285 + 0.035} = \frac{0.285}{0.320} = 0.890625$$

$$P(\mathcal{H}_0 \mid E) = 1 - P(\mathcal{H}_1 \mid E) = \frac{0.035}{0.320} = 0.109375$$

The Kullback-Leibler divergence $D_{\text{KL}}(P \parallel \tilde{P})$ between the true Bayesian posterior $P$ and the socially clamped distribution $\tilde{P}$ diverges to infinity:

$$D_{\text{KL}}(P \parallel \tilde{P}) = \sum_{i \in \{0,1\}} P(\mathcal{H}_i \mid E) \log_2 \left(\frac{P(\mathcal{H}_i \mid E)}{\tilde{P}(\mathcal{H}_i \mid E)}\right) \to \infty \quad \text{as} \quad \tilde{P}(\mathcal{H}_1 \mid E) \to 0$$

This infinite information loss violates **Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety** (Ashby, 1956): a regulator or auditor whose internal state space is forbidden from representing $\mathcal{H}_1$ possesses zero regulatory variety against $\mathcal{H}_1$ perturbations.

## 3. Cross-Disciplinary Proofs: Zahavian Refutation (Penn & Számadó 2020), Sensory Exploitation (Ryan 1990), ESS Invasion (Maynard Smith 1982), BFT (Lamport 1982), and Stationary Bandits (Olson 1993; Tilly 1985)

To prevent colloquial analogies or ungrounded heuristics, the necessity of unclamped adversarial priors is anchored across five independent primary-originator theorems:

### 3.1 Evolutionary Biology: Refutation of the Zahavian Handicap Principle (Penn & Számadó, 2020) and Sensory Exploitation (Ryan, 1990)
In popular corporate discourse, extravagant resource expenditure—such as burning millions of cloud LLM tokens or publishing hundreds of synthetic unit tests—is routinely defended via Amotz Zahavi's (1975) "Handicap Principle," which asserted that signals are honest *because* they are wastefully costly.

In their landmark formal refutation, **Penn & Számadó (2020)** (*Biological Reviews* 95(1):267–290, *"The Handicap Principle: how an erroneous hypothesis became a scientific principle"*) proved mathematically via game-theoretic signaling models (building on Grafen, 1990; Enquist, 1985; Getty, 2006) that **pure Zahavian waste is never a condition for signal honesty and is evolutionarily unstable**:
1. **Differential Marginal Cost vs. Absolute Waste:** Honest equilibrium signaling requires either **physical/structural indices** (signals that cannot be faked because they are causally tied to physical capacity, Maynard Smith & Harper, 2003) or **strategic condition-dependent marginal costs** where low-quality signallers face a strictly higher marginal cost of production than high-quality signallers ($c_{\text{Low}}(s) > c_{\text{High}}(s)$), while high-quality signallers at equilibrium pay minimal or zero wasteful handicap cost.
2. **Sensory Exploitation & Receiver Bias (Ryan, 1990):** When the marginal cost of producing a complex-looking signal collapses for low-quality signallers ($c_{\text{Low}}(s) \to 0$), the system shifts into **Sensory Exploitation** (Ryan, 1990, *Oxford Surveys in Evolutionary Biology* 7:157–195). Signallers evolve exaggerated, low-cost ornaments that hijack pre-existing sensory biases in the receiver without conferring any survival utility. In software engineering, institutional evaluators possess a pre-existing sensory bias equating "formal verification properties + large test suites + multi-agent architecture diagrams" with engineering rigor. Generative LLMs reduce $c_{\text{Low}}(s) \to 0$, allowing fragile wrappers to trigger the receiver's quality heuristic while delivering negative thermodynamic utility.

### 3.2 Evolutionary Game Theory: ESS Invasion of Naive Cooperators (Maynard Smith, 1982)
Per **Maynard Smith (1982)** (*Evolution and the Theory of Games*), let a population of institutional counterparties play an asymmetric resource-allocation game. A strategy $I$ (Clamped Cooperator, where $\tau_{\text{Social}} = 0.65$) cannot be an **Evolutionarily Stable Strategy (ESS)** against a mutant strategy $M$ (Extractive Defector / Cloud-Token Rent Seeker) if the payoff of $M$ playing against $I$ strictly exceeds the payoff of $I$ playing against $I$:

$$E(M, I) > E(I, I) \implies \text{Clamped Cooperation } (I) \text{ is Evolutionarily Invadable}$$

Because a clamped evaluator ($\tilde{P}(\mathcal{H}_1 \mid E) = 0$) never triggers defensive auditing or contract termination against $M$, any mutant strategy $M$ that extracts principal or inflates billable OpEx while emitting high-valence social signals ($V \ge 0.65$) achieves a strictly dominant payoff $E(M, I) \gg E(I, I)$ and drives naive cooperators to extinction.

### 3.3 Distributed Systems Consensus: The Byzantine Fault Tolerance Bound (Lamport, Shostak & Pease, 1982)
Per **Lamport, Shostak & Pease (1982)** (*ACM Transactions on Programming Languages and Systems* 4(3):382–401, *"The Byzantine Generals Problem"*), achieving reliable state consensus across a distributed network containing $f$ arbitrary, mendacious, or equivocating ("Byzantine") nodes requires a total node count $N$ satisfying the strict lower bound:

$$N \ge 3f + 1 \iff f_{\max} = \left\lfloor \frac{N - 1}{3} \right\rfloor$$

For a representative institutional quorum featuring $f = 3$ adversarial or misaligned nodes (e.g., an external cloud API vendor, a co-authoring LLM agent optimizing for token complexity, and a captured internal sponsor), deterministic consensus requires a minimum quorum of:

$$N_{\min} = 3(3) + 1 = 10 \text{ independent verification nodes}$$

 Crucially, Lamport et al. prove that **if a system assumes benign crash-faults ($\mathcal{H}_0$) when nodes are actually Byzantine ($\mathcal{H}_1$), a single traitor node ($f = 1$) can force total consensus failure across an arbitrarily large network**. Clamping $\tau_{\text{Social}} = 0.65$ is mathematically isomorphic to running a crash-only Paxos/Raft assumption inside a hostile Byzantine environment.

### 3.4 Fiscal Sociology & Institutional Economics: Stationary Bandits & Protection Rackets (Olson, 1993; Tilly, 1985)
Per **Tilly (1985)** (*"War Making and State Making as Organized Crime"*) and **Olson (1993)** (*American Political Science Review* 87(3):567–576, *"Dictatorship, Democracy, and Development"*), institutional architectures do not emerge from teleological benevolence; they emerge as **Stationary Bandits** optimizing long-run tax and rent extraction subject to the constraint of not destroying the host's productive base. In parallel, **Jensen & Meckling (1976)** (*Journal of Financial Economics* 3(4):305–360) establish that an agent's divergence from the principal's welfare is bounded strictly by **monitoring costs** and **bonding costs**—never by moral sentiment. Modeling an enterprise vendor or institutional counterparty as a revenue-maximizing Stationary Bandit is not a psychological disposition; it is the foundational equilibrium baseline of orthodox institutional economics.
