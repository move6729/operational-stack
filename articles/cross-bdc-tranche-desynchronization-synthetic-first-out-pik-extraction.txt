### I. The Split-the-Baby Alchemy: How a 70.9-Cent Impaired Loan Becomes a 100.0-Cent Fee Engine

Suppose a private-credit club holds $\$403.3\text{ million}$ in First Lien Senior Secured term loans to a commercial janitorial roll-up whose enterprise value has deteriorated to $\$286.1\text{ million}$. In an un-engineered credit market, the arithmetic is unforgiving: an enterprise worth $\$286.1\text{ million}$ divided across $\$403.3\text{ million}$ of pari passu first-lien debt yields a blended recovery of $70.94\text{ cents}$ on the dollar. If the lenders mark their books to that economic reality, two consequences follow immediately under the Investment Company Act of 1940 (`15 U.S.C. § 80a-1 et seq.`) and the Internal Revenue Code (`26 U.S.C. § 852`):

1. **Immediate Base Management Fee Compression:** Under standard Business Development Company (BDC) advisory contracts charging $1.50\%$ of gross assets, writing down $\$403.3\text{ million}$ of par loans to $\$286.1\text{ million}$ permanently erases $\$117.2\text{ million}$ of fee-bearing asset base.
2. **Non-Accrual Placement and Incentive Fee Collapse:** Because the borrower lacks the free cash flow to service cash interest on $\$403.3\text{ million}$ of principal, placing the unitary facility on non-accrual halts the recognition of Net Investment Income (`NII`), eliminating the $17.5\%$ to $20.0\%$ quarterly cash incentive fees paid to the external investment adviser.

Now observe what happens when the club executes an out-of-court Liability Management Exercise (`LME`)—colloquially known across restructuring desks as **"Splitting the Baby."** Rather than marking the unitary $\$403.3\text{ million}$ First Lien facility at $70.94\text{ cents}$ and placing it on non-accrual, the lenders amend the credit agreement to bifurcate their own claims into two synthetic tranches inside the exact same collateral box:
- A **$\$278.3\text{ million}$ Synthetic "First-Out" First Lien Tranche**, converted to pay up to $9.22\%$ Payment-in-Kind (`PIK`) interest—capitalizing new principal onto the loan balance every quarter instead of requiring cash; and
- A **$\$125.0\text{ million}$ Synthetic "Second-Out" Stub**, which absorbs $100\%$ of the enterprise value impairment and is written down into the single digits or low teens.

In the SEC Form 10-Q XBRL Schedules of Investments filed by Ares Capital Corporation (`ARCC`, CIK `0001287750`, Period `2026-09-30`, Accession `0001628280-26-067383`) and FS KKR Capital Corp. (`FSK`, CIK `0001422183`, Period `2026-06-30`, Accession `0001628280-26-053783`), this exact structure is not a theoretical exercise. It is the live, audited carrying value of **Kellermeyer Bergensons Services, LLC and KBS TopCo, LLC (`KBS`)**.

Across `ARCC` and `FSK`, the combined $\$278.3\text{ million}$ First-Out PIK tranches sit at a blended fair-value mark of **$98.71\text{ cents}$ on the dollar** (`ARCC` marks its $\$43.0\text{ million}$ First-Out $9.22\%$ PIK tranche and its $\$5.7\text{ million}$ $5.25\%$ PIK / $9.14\%$ total-rate tranche at **$100.0\text{ cents}$**; `FSK` marks its $\$229.6\text{ million}$ First-Out `SOFR + 5.3% PIK` tranche at **$98.43\text{ cents}$**, or $\$226.0\text{ million}$). Simultaneously, inside the exact same schedules of investments, the $\$125.0\text{ million}$ Second-Out tranches sit at a blended mark of **$9.12\text{ cents}$ on the dollar**: `ARCC` marks its $\$17.1\text{ million}$ non-accrual tranche at **$2.92\text{ cents}$** ($\$0.5\text{ million}$ fair value, Footnote 8) and its $\$7.7\text{ million}$ cost basis in KBS preferred and common equity at **$0.0\text{ cents}$**, while `FSK` marks its $\$107.9\text{ million}$ Second-Out `SOFR + 8.0% PIK` tranche at **$10.10\text{ cents}$** ($\$10.9\text{ million}$ fair value).

For a credit pod portfolio manager or an institutional limited partner (`LP`), the central forensic question jumps off the page: **How can a senior loan paying $9.22\%$ in non-cash PIK scrips be worth $100.0\text{ cents}$ on the dollar when the tranche immediately beneath it in the same capital structure is wiped out at $2.92\text{ cents}$ to $10.10\text{ cents}$—leaving only $\$11.4\text{ million}$ of total junior value cushion left in the entire company?**

At a $9.22\%$ PIK compounding rate (and $9.14\%$ total rate on the smaller `ARCC` tranche), the $\$278.3\text{ million}$ First-Out debt pool adds **$\$25.43\text{ million}$ of new senior principal every twelve months**. Even if KBS's enterprise value of $\$286.1\text{ million}$ never drops by another dollar, the compounding First-Out PIK debt devours the remaining $\$11.4\text{ million}$ Second-Out valuation cushion in exactly **$0.4483\text{ years}$—or $5.38\text{ months}$** ($t^* = \ln(286.1 / 278.3) / \ln(1 + 25.43 / 278.3)$). In less than two fiscal quarters, the First-Out tranche's own capitalized interest crosses $100\%$ Loan-to-Value (`LTV`). Yet on the reporting date, marking that First-Out paper at $98.71\text{ cents}$ to $100.0\text{ cents}$ avoids **$\$77.28\text{ million}$** in First-Out principal write-downs across `ARCC` and `FSK` compared to the $70.94\text{c}$ blended pool recovery, while booking $\$25.43\text{ million}$ in annualized "Net Investment Income" that never arrives in cash.

### III. Forensic Deep-Dives: Inside the Six Dislocated Capital Structures

#### 1. Production Resource Group (`PRG` / `PRG III, LLC`): Marking $11.40\%$ PIK Debt at $102.98\text{ Cents}$ Atop an $11.12\text{-Cent}$ Equity Wipeout
If `KBS` demonstrates how a First-Out/Second-Out debt split shields senior marks, **Production Resource Group, L.L.C. and PRG III, LLC (`PRG`)**—a live entertainment and event-production equipment provider with **$\$511.6\text{ million}$** in combined `ARCC` and `FSK` exposure—demonstrates the next stage of the lifecycle: what happens after the junior debt has already been equitized into an affiliated restructuring stub.

Per `ARCC` Form 10-Q (Period `2026-09-30`, Acc. `0001628280-26-067383`, pp. 30–31) and `FSK` Form 10-Q (Period `2026-06-30`, Acc. `0001628280-26-053783`, pp. 18, 29, 34), `PRG` is disclosed by `FSK` as an **Affiliated Issuer** following an earlier debt-for-equity restructuring in which the lenders took over ownership of the business:
- **The Senior PIK Stack ($\$402.7\text{ million}$ Par, $\$412.2\text{ million}$ Fair Value, $102.36\text{c}$ Blended Mark):**
  - `ARCC` holds four First Lien Senior Secured term loan tranches totaling **$\$84.2\text{ million}$ par** ($\$71.6\text{M}$ at $11.40\%$ PIK, $\$2.8\text{M}$ at $11.41\%$ PIK, $\$2.7\text{M}$ at $11.47\%$ PIK, and $\$7.1\text{M}$ at $11.46\%$ PIK). Every single one of `ARCC`'s four PRG debt tranches is marked at exactly **$100.0\text{ cents}$ on the dollar** ($\$84.2\text{ million}$ fair value against $\$83.9\text{ million}$ amortized cost).
  - `FSK` holds two First Lien Senior Secured tranches: **$\$308.3\text{ million}$ par** ($\$305.9\text{ million}$ cost) accruing **$11.40\%$ PIK** (`SOFR + 6.5% PIK`), which `FSK` marks at **$\$317.5\text{ million}$ fair value ($102.98\text{ cents}$ on the dollar)**, and **$\$10.2\text{ million}$ par** ($\$9.9\text{ million}$ cost) accruing **$11.40\%$ PIK**, marked at **$\$10.5\text{ million}$ fair value ($102.94\text{ cents}$ on the dollar)**.
- **The Restructured Affiliated Equity Stub ($\$108.9\text{ million}$ Cost, $\$13.0\text{ million}$ Fair Value, $11.94\text{c}$ Blended Mark):**
  - Immediately below those above-par PIK loans, `ARCC` carries $151,988$ preferred membership units of `PRG III, LLC` with a cost basis of **$\$13.6\text{ million}$** at a fair value of just **$\$2.4\text{ million}$ ($17.65\text{ cents}$ on cost)**.
  - `FSK` carries $581,691$ shares of common stock with a cost basis of **$\$95.3\text{ million}$** at a fair value of just **$\$10.6\text{ million}$ ($11.12\text{ cents}$ on cost)**.

Look closely at the internal contradiction between `FSK`'s two marks on the same company. `FSK` is telling public shareholders that PRG's enterprise value is so severely impaired that `FSK`'s $\$95.3\text{ million}$ restructured equity stake has lost **$88.88\%$ of its cost basis** (worth only $\$10.6\text{ million}$), and that the company cannot pay cash interest on $\$402.7\text{ million}$ of senior debt, forcing $100\%$ of the $11.40\%$–$11.47\%$ interest coupon to be paid in PIK paper. Yet at the exact same moment, `FSK` values those $11.40\%$ PIK loans at **$102.98\text{ cents}$ on the dollar**—nearly three full points *above par*!

Why is a $102.98\text{c}$ mark on an $11.40\%$ PIK loan sitting atop an $\$13.0\text{ million}$ total equity buffer mathematically untenable?
1. **The $3.40\text{-Month}$ Equity Cannibalization Clock:** Across `ARCC` and `FSK`, the $\$402.7\text{ million}$ of PRG senior debt accrues **$\$45.91\text{ million}$ per year in non-cash PIK principal** ($\$9.60\text{ million}$ at `ARCC` + $\$36.31\text{ million}$ at `FSK`). The total remaining equity value cushion in the entire company across both BDCs is only **$\$13.0\text{ million}$** ($\$2.4\text{ million}$ at `ARCC` + $\$10.6\text{ million}$ at `FSK`), implying a total enterprise value of $\$425.2\text{ million}$ ($\$412.2\text{ million}$ debt FV + $\$13.0\text{ million}$ equity FV, or an $83.49\text{c}$ blended recovery on total cost). At $\$45.91\text{ million}$ per year of senior PIK compounding, the First-Out debt completely devours the $\$13.0\text{ million}$ equity cushion in **$0.2832\text{ years}$—or $3.40\text{ months}$** ($13.0 / 45.91$).
2. **The $\$42.15\text{ Million}$ Annual Cash Drain on BDC Shareholders:** Even though PRG pays zero cash interest on those $\$402.7\text{ million}$ of PIK loans, `ARCC` and `FSK` book **$\$45.91\text{ million}$ per year** of GAAP Net Investment Income from PRG. Under `IRC § 852(a)(1)` ($90\%$ mandatory RIC distribution) and each BDC's advisory contract ($20.0\%$ incentive fee at `ARCC`, $17.5\%$ at `FSK`), booking $\$45.91\text{ million}$ of non-cash PRG PIK income forces `ARCC` and `FSK` to pay out **$\$42.15\text{ million}$ per year in hard cash** to external fee recipients and dividend distributions!

#### 2. PetVet Care Centers, LLC (`PetVet`): How Can `OBDC` Mark Its Own 1L Term Loan at $87.03\text{c}$ While Marking Its 1L Revolver at $56.67\text{c}$?

### IV. The Portfolio-Level RIC Cash-Drain Identity: Why `FSK` and `ARCC` Bleed $91.75\text{ Cents}$ to $92.00\text{ Cents}$ of Hard Cash on Every $\$1.00$ of PIK

Why do external BDC investment advisers go to such extraordinary lengths to engineer First-Out PIK tranches marked at $98.43\text{c}$ to $102.98\text{c}$ rather than placing impaired borrowers on non-accrual? Because under the BDC fee and tax architecture, **PIK interest is an asymmetric wealth-transfer engine from the BDC's balance sheet to the external investment adviser**.

When a BDC books $\$1.00$ of non-cash PIK interest (or preferred PIK dividend) into GAAP Net Investment Income (`NII`), the BDC receives **$\$0.00$ in cash** from the borrower. However, two mandatory cash outflows are triggered immediately:
1. **The Adviser's Cash Incentive Fee ($\phi_{\text{inc}}$):** Under `FSK`'s advisory agreement ($\phi_{\text{inc}} = 17.5\%$) and `ARCC`'s advisory agreement ($\phi_{\text{inc}} = 20.0\%$), the external manager collects its quarterly incentive fee **in hard cash** on pre-incentive-fee NII—including $100\%$ of accrued PIK income.
2. **The Mandatory RIC Cash Dividend ($\rho_{\text{RIC}} = 90.0\%$):** Under Internal Revenue Code `26 U.S.C. § 852(a)(1)`, to maintain pass-through Regulated Investment Company (`RIC`) tax status and avoid corporate-level taxation, the BDC must distribute at least $90.0\%$ of its post-fee taxable investment company income **in cash** to shareholders:

$$\text{CashDrain}_{\text{per\_PIK}} = \phi_{\text{inc}} + \rho_{\text{RIC}} \cdot (1 - \phi_{\text{inc}})$$

Plug the exact statutory and contractual constants for `FSK` and `ARCC` into this closed-form identity:
- **For `FSK` ($\phi_{\text{inc}} = 0.175$, $\rho_{\text{RIC}} = 0.90$):**
  $$\text{CashDrain}_{\text{per\_PIK, FSK}} = 0.175 + 0.90 \times (1 - 0.175) = 0.175 + 0.7425 = \mathbf{\$0.9175\text{ of cash outflow per }\$1.00\text{ of PIK}}$$
- **For `ARCC` ($\phi_{\text{inc}} = 0.200$, $\rho_{\text{RIC}} = 0.90$):**
  $$\text{CashDrain}_{\text{per\_PIK, ARCC}} = 0.200 + 0.90 \times (1 - 0.200) = 0.200 + 0.7200 = \mathbf{\$0.9200\text{ of cash outflow per }\$1.00\text{ of PIK}}$$

Every time `FSK` or `ARCC` books $\$1.00$ of non-cash PIK interest on `KBS` or `PRG`, the fund must draw down **$91.75\text{ cents}$ (`FSK`) to $92.00\text{ cents}$ (`ARCC`) of real cash** from its revolving credit lines, principal repayments, or new share issuances to pay the manager's cash fee and the mandatory RIC dividend. Furthermore, because the $1.00$ of PIK is capitalized onto the loan's carrying value at $100.0\text{c}$, the external adviser also collects a perpetual **$1.50\%$ annual base management fee** ($\phi_{\text{base}}$) in cash on that phantom principal every subsequent year until the loan is finally written off.

In `SPEC-BDC-SECTION-36B-SOFTWARE-MISMARKING-AND-PIK-FEE-EXTRACTION-v1.0`, we derived the **Self-Funding PIK Liquidity Stability Bound**: a BDC's PIK share of Net Investment Income cannot exceed **$4.24\%$** ($\text{PIK}/\text{NII} \le 4.24\%$) without forcing the fund into net liquidity decumulation or leverage expansion to fund its cash fees and RIC payout. ### V. Milkovich Adversarial Audit: Testing the Advisers' Rule 2a-5 / ASC 820 Waterfall Defense ($\mathcal{H}_0$) Against Algebraic Reality ($\mathcal{H}_1$)

In accordance with the constitutional framework governing analytical synthesis of disclosed public records (`Milkovich v. Lorain Journal Co.`, 497 U.S. 1 (1990); `Partington v. Bugliosi`, 56 F.3d 1147 (9th Cir. 1995)), we explicitly evaluate the BDC valuation committees' and external advisers' on-the-record accounting methodology as the **Null Hypothesis ($\mathcal{H}_0$)** before testing it against closed-form balance-sheet conservation identities (**$\mathcal{H}_1$**).

#### The Null Hypothesis ($\mathcal{H}_0$ — Strict Contractual Priority & Independent Level-3 Waterfall Valuation)
In their SEC Form 10-Q Footnote disclosures on Fair Value Measurements (`ASC 820`) and Board Valuation Designee procedures under SEC Rule `2a-5` (`17 C.F.R. § 270.2a-5`), `ARCC`, `FSK`, `OBDC`, `OTF`, `BXSL`, `OCSL`, and `GBDC` state that Level-3 illiquid loans are valued quarterly using independent third-party valuation firms, discounted cash flow (`DCF`) models, and enterprise-value waterfall allocations. Under $\mathcal{H}_0$:
1. **Waterfall Priority Defense (`KBS`, `PRG`, `Pluralsight`):** When a credit facility is bifurcated into a First-Out tranche and a Second-Out tranche (or debt and restructured equity), the contractual waterfall assigns $100\%$ of enterprise value recovery to the First-Out tranche before a single dollar flows to the Second-Out stub. Therefore, if `KBS` has an estimated enterprise value of $\$286.1\text{ million}$ and First-Out debt of $\$278.3\text{ million}$, the First-Out debt is $102.8\%$ covered by enterprise value (`LTV` $= 97.27\%$) and can legitimately be marked at $98.43\text{c}$ to $100.0\text{c}$, while the $\$125.0\text{ million}$ Second-Out tranche receives only the $\$11.4\text{ million}$ residual ($9.12\text{c}$).
2. **Independent Manager Assumptions Defense (`PetVet`, `PDI`, `Sorenson`):** Under `ASC 820`, different BDC managers hold different tranche vintages, undrawn revolver fee structures, and independent market-yield inputs, explaining why `OCSL`, `ARCC`, `FSK`, and `OBDC` arrive at different fair-value marks on overlapping borrowers.

#### The Alternative Hypothesis ($\mathcal{H}_1$ — Algebraic Falsification of the Par-Marked PIK Waterfall)
While $\mathcal{H}_0$ appears superficially tidy on a static spreadsheet at $t = 0$, it collapses across four independent mathematical and statutory tests:

1. **Falsification 1 — The Sub-2-Quarter PIK Cannibalization Singularity ($5.38\text{ Months}$ at `KBS`, $3.40\text{ Months}$ at `PRG`):**
   A fair-value mark under `ASC 820` represents the price an orderly market participant would pay at the measurement date given the instrument's expected cash flows and credit risk over its remaining life. In `KBS`, the total junior valuation cushion beneath the $\$278.3\text{ million}$ First-Out debt is $\$11.4\text{ million}$ ($4.10\%$ of First-Out par). In `PRG`, the total equity cushion beneath the $\$402.7\text{ million}$ senior PIK debt is $\$13.0\text{ million}$ ($3.23\%$ of senior debt par). Because those senior tranches pay **zero cash** and compound at **$9.22\%$ PIK (`KBS`)** and **$11.40\%\text{–}11.47\%$ PIK (`PRG`)**, the senior debt balance grows by $\$25.43\text{ million}$/year in `KBS` and $\$45.91\text{ million}$/year in `PRG`. No arm's-length institutional buyer under `ASC 820` pays $100.0\text{ cents}$ to $102.98\text{ cents}$ for a non-cash-pay loan sitting at $96.9\%\text{–}97.3\%$ senior LTV whose own PIK accruals will mechanically breach $100\%$ LTV in **$3.40\text{ to } 5.38\text{ months}$** unless enterprise value miraculously accelerates faster than $11.4\%$ per annum.
2. **Falsification 2 — Credit Spread Contradiction on Distressed Non-Cash Paper:**
   In `PRG`, `FSK` marks its $\$308.3\text{ million}$ and $\$10.2\text{ million}$ $11.40\%$ PIK loans at **$102.98\text{ cents}$ and $102.94\text{ cents}$**. A debt instrument only trades at a $\sim 3\text{-point}$ premium to par if its contractual coupon significantly exceeds the market-clearing yield for its risk class and prepayment is call-protected. Yet `FSK`'s own $11.12\text{c}$ equity mark on `PRG` proves the borrower is deeply distressed after an affiliated debt-for-equity restructuring and cannot service cash interest. Valuing an uncollectible-in-cash PIK coupon at a $102.98\text{c}$ premium to par inverts credit pricing theory.
3. **Falsification 3 — Intra-1L Priority Contradiction in `PetVet` ($87.03\text{c}$ Term Loan vs. $56.67\text{c}$ Revolver at `OBDC`):**
   The waterfall priority defense ($\mathcal{H}_0$) cannot explain why `OBDC` marks its PetVet 1L Revolving Credit Facility at **$56.67\text{ cents}$** while marking its PetVet 1L Term Loan at **$87.03\text{ cents}$** (a $30.36\text{c}$ internal spread), nor why `OCSL` marks junior PetVet preferred equity at **$119.86\text{ cents}$** while marking senior 1L debt at **$89.47\text{ cents}$**. If the 1L collateral pool is impaired to $56.67\text{c}$ on the revolver, the 1L term loan cannot be worth $87.03\text{c}$; if the 1L term loan is impaired to $84.04\text{c}\text{–}89.47\text{c}$, the junior preferred equity cannot be worth $119.86\text{c}$ absent a non-ratable value transfer.
4. **Falsification 4 — Statutory Fiduciary Breach & Affiliated Transaction Constraints (`ICA §§ 36(b), 57` & `Rule 2a-5`):**
   - Under `15 U.S.C. § 80a-35(b)` (`ICA Section 36(b)`), an investment adviser owes a fiduciary duty with respect to the receipt of compensation. Structuring out-of-court LMEs to preserve a $98.71\text{c}\text{–}102.98\text{c}$ mark on First-Out PIK tranches prevents **$\$77.28\text{ million}$** of First-Out markdowns in `KBS` alone and generates **$\$71.34\text{ million}$/year** in combined `KBS` + `PRG` non-cash PIK income—extracting **$1.50\%$ base fees** on inflated carrying values and **$17.5\%\text{–}20.0\%$ cash incentive fees** on phantom income while draining $\$65.5\text{ million}$/year in real cash from the funds.
   - Under `15 U.S.C. § 80a-56` (`ICA Section 57`), when a BDC (`FSK` or `ARCC`) holds control or affiliated equity in a restructured portfolio company (`PRG`) alongside other accounts managed by the same adviser complex, discretionary amendments that convert senior debt to $11.40\%$ PIK while marking it at $102.98\text{c}$ directly implicate joint-transaction and conflict-of-interest prohibitions under `17 C.F.R. § 270.2a-5(a)(2)`.
