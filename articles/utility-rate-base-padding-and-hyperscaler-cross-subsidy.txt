## 1. Regime Wire: The Capacity Market Just Told You the Truth

In 1962, Harvey Averch and Leland Johnson published twelve pages in the *American Economic Review* that described, with algebraic precision, exactly what an investor-owned monopoly utility does when regulators guarantee it a return higher than its cost of money: it builds too much capital, on purpose, and calls the result reliability.

Sixty-three years later, the PJM capacity auction — the largest wholesale electricity market in the United States — printed the proof in a single number. The 2025/2026 Base Residual Auction cleared at **$269.92 per MW-day**, against **$28.92 per MW-day** in the prior auction. That is a **9.333333x surge** in the price of keeping the lights on, in the exact delivery years dominated by hyperscale AI datacenter interconnection requests.

Meanwhile, somewhere in a state Public Utility Commission rate case, a utility is asking to add **$5.0 billion** of datacenter-triggered transmission and substation steel to its rate base — and the bill is not going to the datacenter.

Here is the machine, end to end: the 1962 theorem, the $5.0B gold-plated build, the $650.0M annual revenue requirement, the confidential hyperscaler discount, the **$312.00 per year** hidden surcharge on 1.5 million captive households, the 40-year depreciation schedule applied to 5-year GPU economics, and the exit ramp the utilities fear most — behind-the-meter defection.

## 2. The Averch-Johnson Machine: Profit Is a Function of Steel

Under cost-of-service regulation, an investor-owned utility (IOU) does not earn profit by selling electrons efficiently. It earns profit by owning capital. The PUC authorizes a return on equity — call it **10.5%** (`puc_authorized_roe_rate` = 0.105) — on the utility's net rate base, while the utility's actual cost of debt and capital runs roughly **5.5%** (`utility_cost_of_debt_capital_rate` = 0.055). The difference is a risk-free spread:

```
    THE AVERCH-JOHNSON GOLD-PLATING IDENTITY (1962)

    Authorized ROE (s) ............ 10.5%   <- set by the PUC
    True cost of capital (r) ......  5.5%   <- set by the bond market
    -------------------------------------
    Regulatory spread (s - r) ..... +5.0%   RISK-FREE, PERPETUAL

    Profit  =  (s - r)  x  Rate Base
            =  +$0.05 per $1 of steel, per year, forever

    d(Profit)/d(Capex) = +5.0%  >  0   ==>  BUILD. MORE. STEEL.
```

Read that last line carefully. Every additional dollar of rate base yields five cents of guaranteed annual profit. Every dollar of *cheap distributed alternative* the utility declines to build — grid-enhancing technologies, demand response, distributed storage — is not negligence. It is rational shareholder behavior under the regulatory compact. Averch and Johnson proved in 1962 that when `s > r`, the regulated firm systematically over-capitalizes relative to the cost-minimizing input mix. The theorem does not say the utility *might* gold-plate. It says gold-plating is the equilibrium.

Now drop a 100 MW to 1 GW hyperscale AI datacenter interconnection request onto that incentive structure. The utility's engineers can often serve new load with cheaper options. The utility's balance sheet cannot afford to prefer them. The rational answer is the most capital-intensive solution available: **$5.0 billion** (`hyperscaler_induced_transmission_capex_usd_billions` = 5.0) of 765 kV transmission lines, substations, and reactive support — the kind of steel that maximizes rate base per megawatt.

## 4. The Statute They Are Breaking Is Public

The Federal Power Act is not obscure. **16 U.S.C. § 824d (FPA § 205)** requires that all wholesale rates "shall be just and reasonable," and declares unlawful any rate that is "unjust, unreasonable, unduly discriminatory or preferential." **16 U.S.C. § 824e (FPA § 206)** gives FERC the power to fix such rates after hearing. Sixty years of doctrine implement those words through one principle: **cost causation** — the class that imposes a cost on the system bears that cost.

The Supreme Court's framework in *FPC v. Hope Natural Gas Co.*, 320 U.S. 591 (1944) and *Bluefield Water Works v. Public Service Comm'n*, 262 U.S. 679 (1923) protects the *end result* of a reasonable return on invested capital. Note what that means in this context: the IOU's 10.5% return is constitutionally protected **regardless of whether the induced steel ever serves the load that justified it**. The *Hope* end-result test was designed to protect utilities from confiscation. In the datacenter era, it operates as the guarantee that the annuity keeps paying even after the GPUs leave.

FERC has already seen the pattern. In **Docket No. ER24-2952** (the Talen–Susquehanna co-location order, 2024), the Commission rejected the amended interconnection service agreement that would have expanded co-located datacenter load at a nuclear station, holding the cost allocation unjust, unreasonable, and unduly preferential. That order is the administrative system's first explicit recognition of what this specification formalizes: hyperscaler interconnection economics, left to confidential contracting, externalize costs onto the remaining ratepayer base.

## 5. The Capacity Market Falsifies the Defense

The industry's defense — call it the null hypothesis, H0 — is clean and sounds reasonable:

> **H0:** Datacenter load growth spreads fixed grid costs over more megawatt-hours, lowering average cost for everyone, and brings jobs, tax base, and development to the region. The residential bill impact is a rounding error.

H0 is falsified by a single public number. If new load were spreading existing fixed costs, the *marginal* price of capacity would fall or hold flat. Instead, the PJM 2025/2026 Base Residual Auction cleared at **$269.92/MW-day** against the prior **$28.92/MW-day**:

```
    PJM BASE RESIDUAL AUCTION CLEARING PRICE

    Prior auction:      $28.92 /MW-day   ==>  $10,555.80 /MW-yr
    2025/2026 auction:  $269.92/MW-day   ==>  $98,520.80 /MW-yr
    -----------------------------------------------------------
    Surge multiplier:   269.92 / 28.92 = 9.333333x
    Increment:          +$87,965 /MW-yr  =  +$87.965M/yr per GW
```

For one 1 GW campus, the capacity-market step-up alone is **+$87.965M per year** — and capacity procurement costs flow through the same socialized allocation as transmission. The datacenter did not absorb spare capacity; it forced the system to buy an order-of-magnitude-more-expensive megawatt, and the invoice routes around the datacenter's confidential contract and lands on the 72.0% socialization channel.

Two more falsification vectors close the box:

1. **The $312.00/yr household shift.** The inducing class pays a sealed discount; the non-inducing class pays the fixed-cost recovery. Severed cost causation is the structural signature of an undue preference under § 205.
2. **Duration asymmetry.** The hyperscaler's commitment horizon is a 5–7 year GPU lease cycle. The recovery horizon is 40 years. H0's development benefit is reversible at Year 7; the ratepayer's obligation is not.

The alternative hypothesis H1 is not an accusation against any individual. ## 7. The Death Spiral Is the Exit Ramp

There are two remedies, and they are complements.

**Remedy 1 — FPA § 205 cost-causation ring-fencing.** Assign 100% of datacenter-induced network upgrade costs — plus the 9.333333x capacity procurement — to the inducing customer class, through public (not confidential) conditional interconnection agreements whose minimum commitment tenors match the recovery horizon. If the steel lasts 40 years, the take-or-pay commitment lasts 40 years. The socialization channel (alpha = 0.72) is a policy choice, and it can be unchosen.

**Remedy 2 — behind-the-meter defection.** A residential solar-plus-LFP-storage islanded microgrid exits the socialized cost pool entirely. And defection compounds, because the fixed-cost pool shrinks with every departing meter:

```
    DEFECTION STEP-UP (delta = 10% OF HOUSEHOLDS EXIT)

    Before:  $468.0M / 1.50M households = $312.00 / yr
    After:   $468.0M / 1.35M households = $346.67 / yr   (+11.11%)

    Higher surcharge  ==>  faster payback on solar+storage
                      ==>  more defection
                      ==>  smaller captive base
                      ==>  higher surcharge  ==>  ...
```

At a 10% defection share, the surcharge on each remaining household steps up from $312.00 to **$346.67** (+11.11%), which shortens the payback period on the next household's solar-LFP system, which accelerates the next wave. The gradient is strictly positive and monotone — d(sigma)/d(delta) = S_res / [N (1 - delta)^2] > 0 — so every exit makes the next exit more economical:

```
    DEATH-SPIRAL GRADIENT (SURCHARGE vs. DEFECTION SHARE)

    delta = 0.00  ==>  $312.00 / household / yr
    delta = 0.10  ==>  $346.67 / household / yr   (+11.11%)
    delta = 0.25  ==>  $416.00 / household / yr   (+33.33%)
    delta = 0.50  ==>  $624.00 / household / yr   (+100.00%)
```

This is the utility death spiral — the Averch-Johnson flywheel inverted. The machine runs on Profit = +5.0% x Rate Base; as the rate base erodes and the captive base shrinks, the gold-plating gradient loses its fuel, and the monopoly's political economy of "build steel, raise bills, repeat" breaks down arithmetically rather than legislatively.
