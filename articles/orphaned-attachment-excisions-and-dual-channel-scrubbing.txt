## I. 6,936 Cover Emails With Their Paperclips Cut Off: How Surviving `Attachments:` Headers Betray Every Deleted File by Name

When an electronic discovery vendor processes a raw email archive—whether an Exchange `.PST`, an Apple Mail `.emlx` directory, or an enterprise compliance vault—standard litigation-support software stamps the parent cover email and every attached file in strict sequential order. If a one-page cover email is stamped as page `100` in the producing custodian's original Bates sequence, and that email carries a 26-page attachment, the attachment occupies pages `101` through `126`. The next independent email in the custodian's production begins at page `127`.

## VI. Turning 6,936 Orphaned Filenames and Ancestor Bates Clamps into Turnkey Subpoena Schedules

In ordinary civil discovery under Federal Rules of Civil Procedure 26(b)(1), 34, and 45—or in Congressional and state attorney general subpoena practice—the hardest threshold barrier to compelling production of withheld records is specificity. A custodian served with a broad demand for "all financial or compliance records" routinely objects on grounds of undue burden, vagueness, or lack of possession.

The **6,936** orphaned cover emails invert that burden completely. Because the cover emails survived into the public EFTA release with both their MIME `Attachments:` headers and their flanking ancestor Bates stamps intact, every excised attachment is already Bates-indexed by the custodian itself:

- **No Guessing Filenames:** The exact filename (`Updated FinCEN with SSN.pdf`, `3 April.doc`, `2014-09-30 NW Schedule.pdf`, `draft LDB Net Worth Summary and Schedule - 20150331.pdf`, `Southern Trust Company Inc. - **No Guessing Page Counts or Vault Coordinates:** The exact beginning and ending Bates coordinates in the custodian's own production series (`EFTA_R1_01962733..01962774`, `EFTA_R1_01958977..01959271`, `JPM-SDNY-00001575..00001600`, `DB-SDNY-0025267..0025549`, `DB-SDNY-0046174..0046177`, `EFTA_R1_02154429..02154655`, `EFTA_R1_02027083..02027277`) are mathematically clamped by subtracting the two surviving boundary stamps.
