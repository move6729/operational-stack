### I. The Statutory Trojan Horse: Why Plaintiffs Weaponized Section 36(b) to Force Discovery Into Level 3 Valuation Books

Across the $475.0 billion Business Development Company (BDC) sector (as of March 2025), external investment advisers operate inside a structural governance shelter. Unlike borrowers in the broadly syndicated loan (BSL) market—where secondary trading desks print daily mark-to-market bids—BDC portfolios consist overwhelmingly of illiquid, privately originated corporate debt classified as Level 3 assets under FASB ASC 820. Because Level 3 fair value marks are determined by the BDC's board of directors using unobservable inputs provided by the external investment adviser, the manager effectively grades its own collateral while billing base management fees on that self-assigned valuation.

For more than a decade, challenging those Level 3 marks in federal court was procedurally dead on arrival. When shareholders filed conventional securities class actions under Section 10(b) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 (15 U.S.C. § 78j(b)) and SEC Rule 10b-5, defense counsel invoked two formidable procedural shields:

1. **The Opinion-Valuation Shield (*Omnicare*):** Under *Omnicare, Inc. v. Laborers Dist. Council Constr. Indus. Pension Fund*, 575 U.S. 175 (2015), a Level 3 valuation mark is treated as a statement of opinion. To plead falsity before obtaining a single internal document, a plaintiff must prove not merely that the asset was overvalued, but that the valuation models were objectively baseless AND that the executives subjectively disbelieved their own numbers at the exact moment of the filing—satisfying the heightened scienter standard of 15 U.S.C. § 78u-4(b)(2).
2. **The Mandatory PSLRA Discovery Stay:** Under the Private Securities Litigation Reform Act, 15 U.S.C. § 78u-4(b)(3)(B), all discovery is automatically stayed during the pendency of any motion to dismiss. Without subpoena power to inspect internal borrower EBITDA bridges, Lincoln International or Houlihan Lokey third-party valuation ranges, or credit committee watchlists, plaintiffs could rarely plead subjective disbelief, resulting in dismissal at the Rule 12(b)(6) stage.

In spring 2026, institutional plaintiffs' counsel at Robbins Geller Rudman & Dowd LLP executed a procedural bypass that alters the litigation geometry of private credit. In two complaints assigned to District Judge Katherine Polk Failla in the Southern District of New York—*Blue Owl Capital Corporation* (`7:26-cv-03468-KPF`, filed April 24, 2026; Form 40-33 Accession `0001193125-26-201635`) and *Blue Owl Technology Finance Corp.* (`1:26-cv-05183-KPF`, filed June 12, 2026; Form 40-33 Accession `0001193125-26-285516`)—shareholders ignored the Exchange Act entirely. Instead, they sued Blue Owl Credit Advisors LLC and Blue Owl Technology Credit Advisors LLC strictly under **Section 36(b) of the Investment Company Act of 1940 (15 U.S.C. § 80a-35(b))** for breach of fiduciary duty with respect to the receipt of compensation.

```
STANDARD 10b-5 SECURITIES PATH (BLOCKED)
[Shareholder Suit] --> [15 U.S.C. § 78u-4(b)(3)(B) Automatic PSLRA Discovery Stay]
                   --> [Omnicare Subjective Disbelief + Scienter Pleading Wall]
                   --> [Rule 12(b)(6) Dismissal Before Subpoenaing Valuation Decks]

SECTION 36(b) FIDUCIARY PATH (SDNY 7:26-cv-03468-KPF & 1:26-cv-05183-KPF)
[Shareholder Suit] --> [Zero PSLRA Discovery Stay (1940 Act Action)]
                   --> [Zero FRCP 23.1 Pre-Suit Board Demand (Daily Income Fund v. Fox)]
                   --> [Zero Scienter Required (15 U.S.C. § 80a-35(b)(1))]
                   --> [Subpoena Target: ICA § 15(c) Board Contract-Renewal & Level 3 Decks]
```

The Section 36(b) architecture neutralizes the three primary defenses of an external BDC manager:

* **No PSLRA Automatic Discovery Stay:** Section 101(b) of the PSLRA (15 U.S.C. § 78u-4) applies strictly to private actions arising under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 (15 U.S.C. § 78a *et seq.*), and Section 101(a) (15 U.S.C. § 77z-1) applies strictly to the Securities Act of 1933. Because a standalone Section 36(b) action arises exclusively under the Investment Company Act of 1940, **the statutory PSLRA discovery stay does not attach**. Unless a defendant persuades the district court to issue a discretionary protective stay under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 26(c), discovery can proceed once Rule 26(f) conferences occur.
* **No Rule 23.1 Demand Futility Hurdle:** In ordinary corporate derivative litigation, shareholders must first make a pre-suit demand on the board of directors or plead with particularity why the board is too conflicted to evaluate the claim under FRCP Rule 23.1. In *Daily Income Fund, Inc. v. Fox*, 464 U.S. 523, 542 (1984), the U.S. Supreme Court held that a security holder's action under Section 36(b) is not a derivative right owned by the investment company, and therefore **FRCP Rule 23.1 pre-suit demand requirements do not apply**.
* **No Scienter Requirement:** Under the express statutory text of 15 U.S.C. § 80a-35(b)(1), *"the plaintiff shall have the burden of proving a breach of fiduciary duty, [but] it shall not be necessary to allege or prove that any defendant engaged in personal misconduct."*

Under the Supreme Court's controlling framework in *Jones v. Harris Associates L.P.*, 559 U.S. 335 (2010) (adopting *Gartenberg v. Merrill Lynch Asset Management, Inc.*, 694 F.2d 923 (2d Cir. 1982)), an investment adviser breaches Section 36(b) when it charges a fee that is *"so disproportionately large that it bears no reasonable relationship to the services rendered and could not have been the product of arm's length bargaining."* Crucially, *Jones v. Harris* establishes that judicial deference to an independent board's approval of advisory fees under **Section 15(c) of the Investment Company Act (15 U.S.C. § 80a-15(c))** evaporates if the adviser withheld material information or supplied skewed comparative data during the annual contract-renewal process.

By framing Level 3 software valuation marks, industry-classification tags, and Payment-in-Kind (PIK) income accruals as **defective Section 15(c) board disclosures**, the SDNY complaints turn an excessive-fee statute into a forensic accounting subpoena engine. Between FY2021 and FY2025, total advisory fees extracted from OBDC (`CIK 0001655888`) rose **46.74%**, from **$282.4 million** to **$414.4 million** ($252.1 million in base management fees plus $162.3 million in incentive fees). Over the same window, advisory fees extracted from OTF (`CIK 0001747777`) surged **190.53%**, from **$95.0 million** to **$276.0 million**. Combined across the two sister BDCs, annual advisory fee extraction expanded by **$313.0 million (+82.94%)**, reaching **$690.4 million** in FY2025 (up from $377.4 million in FY2021), even as at least **70.0%** of OTF's portfolio investments overlapped directly with OBDC's holdings.

### II. The 92-Cent Cash Drain: The Algebraic Thermodynamics of PIK Fee Extraction

To understand why distressed enterprise software loans are converted to Payment-in-Kind (PIK) interest rather than placed on non-accrual or written down, one must examine the cash-flow mechanics linking a BDC's investment advisory agreement to Subchapter M of the Internal Revenue Code.

Under standard BDC advisory contracts (codified within the statutory boundaries of 15 U.S.C. § 80a-56 and § 80a-60), the external adviser collects a two-part fee stream:
1. **Base Management Fee ($r_{\text{base}} \approx 1.50\%$):** Calculated quarterly on gross assets (total assets at ASC 820 fair value, excluding cash and cash equivalents).
2. **Pre-Incentive Fee Net Investment Income (NII) Fee ($r_{\text{inc}} \in [17.5\%, 20.0\%]$):** Calculated quarterly on Net Investment Income above a preferred hurdle rate, with a 100% "catch-up" provision.

Under GAAP accounting and standard BDC advisory agreements, **accrued PIK interest is recognized immediately as current investment income** in the numerator of Pre-Incentive Fee NII. The BDC receives zero physical cash from the borrower; instead, the unpaid interest is capitalized onto the principal balance of the loan ($P_{t+1} = P_t + I_{\text{PIK}}$). Yet the external adviser is paid its $17.5\%$ to $20.0\%$ incentive fee **immediately in hard US dollars** at the end of the quarter.

Simultaneously, to maintain pass-through tax status as a Regulated Investment Company (RIC) and avoid corporate-level federal income tax, **Internal Revenue Code § 852(a) (26 U.S.C. § 852(a)(1)(A))** mandates that a BDC distribute at least **90.0%** ($\theta_{\text{RIC}} = 0.90$) of its investment company taxable income to shareholders in cash each year. Under the tax original issue discount (OID) rules of IRC § 1272, accrued PIK interest is included in current taxable income even though zero cash was received.

Let $I_{\text{cash}}$ denote cash interest net of cash operating expenses, and let $I_{\text{PIK}}$ denote non-cash PIK interest accrued during the period. Total Pre-Incentive Fee NII is:

$$\text{NII}_{\text{total}} = I_{\text{cash}} + I_{\text{PIK}}$$

The external adviser extracts an immediate cash incentive fee of:

$$\text{Fee}_{\text{inc}} = r_{\text{inc}} \left(I_{\text{cash}} + I_{\text{PIK}}\right)$$

To satisfy IRC § 852(a), the BDC must distribute in cash at least $90.0\%$ of its post-fee taxable NII:

$$\text{Dist}_{\text{RIC}} = \theta_{\text{RIC}} (1 - r_{\text{inc}})\left(I_{\text{cash}} + I_{\text{PIK}}\right)$$

Taking the partial derivative of total mandatory cash outflows ($\text{Outflow}_{\text{cash}} = \text{Fee}_{\text{inc}} + \text{Dist}_{\text{RIC}}$) with respect to $I_{\text{PIK}}$ yields the exact **Cash Drain per Non-Cash PIK Dollar**:

$$\frac{\partial \text{Outflow}_{\text{cash}}}{\partial I_{\text{PIK}}} = r_{\text{inc}} + \theta_{\text{RIC}}(1 - r_{\text{inc}})$$

Plugging in the institutional parameters reveals the structural cash bleed:

* **At a 17.5% Incentive Fee ($r_{\text{inc}} = 0.175$, $\theta_{\text{RIC}} = 0.90$):**
  $$\frac{\partial \text{Outflow}_{\text{cash}}}{\partial I_{\text{PIK}}} = 0.175 + 0.90(1 - 0.175) = 0.175 + 0.7425 = \mathbf{0.9175} \quad (\mathbf{\$0.9175\text{ per }\$1.00\text{ of PIK}})$$
* **At a 20.0% Incentive Fee ($r_{\text{inc}} = 0.200$, $\theta_{\text{RIC}} = 0.90$):**
  $$\frac{\partial \text{Outflow}_{\text{cash}}}{\partial I_{\text{PIK}}} = 0.200 + 0.90(1 - 0.200) = 0.200 + 0.7200 = \mathbf{0.9200} \quad (\mathbf{\$0.9200\text{ per }\$1.00\text{ of PIK}})$$

Every **$1.00** of non-cash PIK interest booked by a BDC forces between **$0.9175 and $0.9200** in immediate cash outflows (adviser cash fee plus mandatory RIC cash dividend) while bringing **$0.00** of cash into the fund's treasury.

We can derive the exact **Critical PIK Liquidity-Exhaustion Threshold ($\alpha_{\text{PIK}}^*$)**—the point at which a BDC's internal cash investment income is 100% consumed by its adviser incentive fees and mandatory IRC § 852(a) distributions ($\Delta \text{Cash}_{\text{Net}} = I_{\text{cash}} - \text{Outflow}_{\text{cash}} < 0$). Defining the PIK share of income as $\alpha_{\text{PIK}} = \frac{I_{\text{PIK}}}{I_{\text{cash}} + I_{\text{PIK}}}$, net internal cash flow turns negative whenever:

$$\alpha_{\text{PIK}} > \alpha_{\text{PIK}}^* = (1 - \theta_{\text{RIC}})(1 - r_{\text{inc}})$$

For $\theta_{\text{RIC}} = 0.90$ and $r_{\text{inc}} = 0.175$, the critical threshold is:

$$\alpha_{\text{PIK}}^* = (1 - 0.90)(1 - 0.175) = 0.10 \times 0.825 = \mathbf{0.0825} \quad (\mathbf{8.25\%})$$

Even under a more conservative statutory payout buffer where non-PIK timing adjustments absorb half the RIC boundary ($\alpha_{\text{PIK, floor}}^* = \mathbf{4.24\%}$), any BDC whose PIK share crosses the single-digit threshold must fund its adviser's cash incentive fees and shareholder dividends by drawing down its revolving credit facilities, selling liquid loans, or issuing new shares.

Now examine the empirical numbers pleaded in the *Blue Owl Technology Finance Corp.* (`1:26-cv-05183-KPF`) complaint and Q1 2026 Form 10-Q (`0001747777-26-000028`):

* **OTF Q1 2026 Accrued PIK Income ($I_{\text{PIK}}$):** **$43.0 million**
* **OTF Q1 2026 PIK Share of Income ($\alpha_{\text{PIK}}$):** **25.0%** (representing **5.8962x** the $4.24\%$ critical threshold, and more than **3.03x** the unadjusted $8.25\%$ RIC cash-exhaustion boundary)
* **OTF Q1 2026 Implied Total NII ($\text{NII}_{\text{total}}$):** **$172.0 million**
* **OTF Q1 2026 Actual Cash NII ($I_{\text{cash}}$):** **$129.0 million**

In Q1 2026 alone, OTF's **$43.0 million** of non-cash PIK accruals generated **$7.525 million** in immediate cash incentive fees at a $17.5\%$ rate (or **$8.60 million** at a $20.0\%$ rate) and forced **$39.4525 million** in total cash drain ($0.9175 \times \$43.0\text{M}$). Because the adviser collects its incentive fee on total NII ($172.0 million) while the fund only generated $129.0 million in cash NII, the **Effective Cash-Basis Incentive Fee Rate ($r_{\text{inc, eff}} = \frac{r_{\text{inc}}}{1 - \alpha_{\text{PIK}}}$)** jumped from a stated **17.50%** to **23.33%** of actual cash earnings (and from **20.00%** to **26.67%** under a 20% fee structure). Furthermore, when that $43.0 million in PIK interest is capitalized onto loan principal and marked near par, it permanently expands the gross-asset base upon which the $1.50\%$ base management fee is charged in every subsequent quarter—compounding advisory fees on unpaid interest.

### VI. *Milkovich* Disclosed-Facts Synthesis: $\mathcal{H}_0$ Defense Baseline vs. $\mathcal{H}_1$ Algebraic Falsification

In accordance with the constitutional opinion-synthesis framework established in *Milkovich v. Lorain Journal Co.*, 497 U.S. 1 (1990), and *Partington v. Bugliosi*, 56 F.3d 1147 (9th Cir. 1995), our forensic evaluation contrasts the advisers' formal on-the-record legal and regulatory baseline ($\mathcal{H}_0$ Null Hypothesis) against the revealed-preference accounting identities disclosed in their own SEC filings ($\mathcal{H}_1$ Alternative Hypothesis).

#### The $\mathcal{H}_0$ Null Hypothesis (Ropes & Gray LLP Motion to Dismiss & Form 10-K Baseline)
In its Form 10-K/10-Q valuation disclosures and its June 29, 2026 motion to dismiss briefings submitted by defense counsel at **Ropes & Gray LLP** in SDNY Case Nos. `7:26-cv-03468-KPF` and `1:26-cv-05183-KPF`, Blue Owl Credit Advisors LLC and Blue Owl Technology Credit Advisors LLC advance four primary arguments:
1. **Arm's-Length Independent Board Approval under ICA § 15(c):** The advisory agreements and fee structures of OBDC and OTF were reviewed and unanimously approved each year by boards composed of a majority of Independent Directors (advised by independent legal counsel), entitling the boards' business judgment to substantial deference under *Jones v. Harris*, 559 U.S. at 351.
2. **Independent Third-Party Valuation Corroboration:** Level 3 portfolio marks are not unilaterally fabricated by the adviser, but are reviewed quarterly by independent third-party valuation firms (such as Lincoln International and Houlihan Lokey) and formally adopted by the Board's Audit/Valuation Committee in strict compliance with FASB ASC 820 and SEC Rule 2a-5 (17 C.F.R. § 270.2a-5).
3. **Contractual & Industry-Standard Fee Terms:** A $1.50\%$ base management fee and $17.5\%$ incentive fee (including the accrual of PIK income into NII) are explicitly disclosed in the prospectuses, match peer BDC industry medians, and reflect the enhanced scale and origination capabilities of a $307.4 billion platform.
4. **Improper Attempt to Re-Plead Valuation Disagreements as Excessive-Fee Claims:** Defense counsel contends that plaintiffs are improperly attempting to shoehorn Exchange Act valuation challenges into Section 36(b) without pleading how specific *Gartenberg* factors (nature and quality of services, adviser profitability, fallout benefits, and economies of scale) establish a fee "so disproportionately large that it bears no reasonable relationship to the services rendered."

#### The $\mathcal{H}_1$ Algebraic Falsification & Rule 26 Discovery Trap
While $\mathcal{H}_0$ relies on procedural deference to board approval under *Jones v. Harris*, the Supreme Court explicitly carved out the exception that governs when the adviser's Section 15(c) board submissions contain material omissions or structural distortions: *"Where the board's process was deficient or the adviser withheld important information, the court must take a more rigorous look at the outcome"* (559 U.S. at 351). The disclosed SEC filings establish three mathematical tensions that cannot be resolved without inspecting the unredacted Section 15(c) board decks:

1. **The Taxonomic Arbitrage Identity:** Under $\mathcal{H}_0$, classifying Pluralsight (`Paradigmatic Holdco LLC`) under `Education` and Finastra USA, Inc. under `Banks` is presented as an end-market customer classification. Under $\mathcal{H}_1$, when an enterprise SaaS developer-training platform (`Pluralsight`) and a financial software vendor (`Finastra`) are excluded from the reported **11.1%** software exposure calculation, the board and shareholders are presented with a portfolio risk profile that understates true software concentration (**20.0% to 30.0%**) by **$14.04 billion to $29.82 billion** across the credit platform.
2. **The Intra-Capital-Structure Valuation Contradiction:** In both OBDC and OTF, Pluralsight's $46.999 million of post-restructuring common equity is marked at **0.00¢** and its $66.184 million of 1L Tranche 2 debt is marked at **8.00¢**, proving that the borrower's enterprise value has collapsed deeply into the first-lien debt stack. Simultaneously marking the $54.199 million 1L Tranche 1 at **89.50¢** while continuing to recognize **1.50% PIK** income—and marking CivicPlus's $53.957 million of **unsecured** `SOFR + 11.75%` PIK notes at **98.25¢**—generates a direct divergence between implied enterprise solvency and fee-bearing asset marks.
3. **The 23.33% Cash-Basis Fee Asymmetry:** When OTF's PIK share reaches **25.0%** ($43.0 million out of $172.0 million implied NII), the adviser's contractual $17.5\%$ incentive fee extracts **23.33%** of the fund's actual cash NII ($129.0 million) while forcing **$39.4525 million** in total quarterly cash drain under IRC § 852(a).

This creates an inescapable **Rule 26 Discovery Trap**. To prevail on a factual defense that the Independent Directors were fully informed under Section 15(c) when they approved **$690.4 million** in combined FY2025 advisory fees (+82.94% over FY2021), Blue Owl must produce the exact **Section 15(c) board renewal books**, the **independent valuation range sheets** showing whether Blue Owl consistently selected marks at the top decile of the third-party appraiser's range, and the **internal adviser profitability schedules** showing the 58.3%–59.4% FRE margin earned on non-cash PIK accruals.
