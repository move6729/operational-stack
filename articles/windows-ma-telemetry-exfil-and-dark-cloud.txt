## V. The Systems Engineering Hypothesis ($\mathcal{H}_1$): The Williamson M&A Boundary Bypass, Non-Cash Cartel Settlement Equilibrium, and the Four Governing Equations

### 1. Shannon Egress Velocity

Shannon (1948) fixes the physics: the time to move a corpus $D$ through a chunked channel with chunk size $c$ and sustained rate $R$ is

$$t_{\text{exfil}} = \frac{\lceil D/c \rceil \cdot c}{R}, \quad c = 0x249F00 = 2{,}400{,}000 \text{ B}$$

For a representative enterprise corpus $D = 1.0$ GiB: $\lceil 1{,}073{,}741{,}824 / 2{,}400{,}000 \rceil = 448$ chunks, and at a conservative $R = 1.25$ MB/s: $t = 448 \times 2.4\,\text{MB} / 1.25\,\text{MB/s} = 860.16\,\text{s} = 14.336$ minutes. The pipeline is sized for bulk document and database egress, not metering. A "diagnostic" channel that can move a gigabyte in a quarter hour on a routine residential connection is a bulk exfiltration channel whose idle time is spent on diagnostics.

### 2. Stigler/Spence Export Concealment Ratio

Define the PE Export Concealment Ratio $\Omega(b) = 1 - E_{\text{authentic}}(b) / S_{\text{internal}}(b)$: the complement of the ratio of PE exports that truthfully describe the binary's collection/C2 function ($E_{\text{authentic}}$) to its internal PDB symbols ($S_{\text{internal}}$). Stigler (1961) and Spence (1973) establish that search-cost walls and information asymmetry are manufactured by suppressing the observable signal. Across all three acquisition-cluster binaries — `SenseIR.exe` (`67` decoy `zlib` exports, $E_{\text{authentic}} = 0$ over $S_{\text{internal}} = 2{,}917$ unique internal C++ symbols and `21,712` `S_PUB32` records), `MsSense.exe` ($0 / 9{,}326$ internal symbols, `28,370` `S_PUB32`), and `SenseCncProxy.exe` ($0 / 4{,}427$ `S_PUB32`) — the measured ratio is $\Omega = 1.0000$ uniformly. Under $\mathcal{H}_0$, authentic entry points are exported ($\Omega < 1$). The observed $\Omega = 1.0000$ falsifies $\mathcal{H}_0$ at the binary level.

### 3. Markov Volatile Opt-Out

Model the per-cycle telemetry state as a two-state Markov chain $\{A = \text{active}, O = \text{idle/disabled}\}$ governed by `diagtrack.dll`'s RAM-only `AddDisabledTelemetryProviderUntilNextUpdate` (`RVA 0x151BB4`–`0x151C19`) versus `MsSense.exe`'s persistent `SwapOverrideSettings` (`RVA 0x1B8F80`). With transition matrix $P = ((0.95, 0.05), (0.15, 0.85))$ ($p = 0.05$, $q = 0.15$), the per-cycle stationary distribution already sits at $\pi_{\text{active}} = q/(p+q) = 0.15/(0.05+0.15) = 0.7500$. And because the disabled-provider tree at `[rbx+0x1A8]` lives solely in volatile RAM while override maps persist, every service restart or Windows Update clears $O \to A$ with probability $1$, driving the multi-reboot limit $\lim_{k \to \infty} \pi_{\text{active}}^{\text{reboot}} = 1.0000$. Akerlof (1970) and Grossman–Stiglitz (1980) supply the market reading: when the observable quality signal (the opt-out) is volatile by construction, the market for privacy cannot clear at any price.
