## 1. The Pitch: A Country as a Term Sheet

For a decade, the most fashionable idea in venture capital has been that starting a country is a product category. Balaji Srinivasan's *The Network State* (2022) gave the genre its manifesto: crowdfund a community, buy territory, negotiate recognition, exit. Pronomos Capital gave it a fund. And Honduras gave it something no pitch deck could ever manufacture on its own: a sovereign coastline.

The lineage matters, because the idea did not arrive as liberation. It arrived as a sequence of downgrades. The charter-city literature promised that poor countries could import good institutions by leasing territory to institution entrepreneurs. The network-state literature promised that individuals could exit bad states by incorporating new ones. What actually got built on Roatán was neither: it was a private government with a public coastline, whose investors held the ballots, whose residents held leases, and whose host nation held the bag.

The vehicle was the ZEDE — *Zonas de Empleo y Desarrollo Económico* — a regime created by a 2011 constitutional reform and a 2013 organic law that authorized semi-autonomous zones on Honduran soil with their own private governance, private courts, and investor "stability" guarantees. The flagship node was Próspera ZEDE, developed by Honduras Próspera Inc. and bankrolled by a venture syndicate that deployed, on the locked baseline of this analysis, $120.0 million of physical capital.

The stability agreement is the load-bearing instrument of the whole construction. In the claimant's own public theory, the 2013 regime carried a long-horizon stability commitment — a fifty-year horizon is the number its advocates invoke. Read plainly, that is a legislature that did not own the sovereignty it conveyed purporting to bind every future legislature that does. A stability clause of that kind is not a contract term. It is a perpetual lease on the democratic process, signed by a tenant.

The exit rhetoric deserves the same reading. The network-state pitch sells "exit not voice" as liberation — the right to leave a bad state and join a better one. But the ZEDE term sheet priced exit in only one direction. Residents could exit the zone; capital could never exit the invoice. And when the host state exercised its own form of exit — repealing the statute by a unanimous vote — the syndicate converted that democratic exit into a $10,775.0 million taxable event. Exit for the investor is a right. Exit for the republic is a bill.

Read the pitch materials and the ideology sounds libertarian. Read the governance documents and it reads like something much older. The ZEDE was not a republic with investors in it. It was a cap table with residents in it.

## 6. The Boundary: Nemo Dat, Article 46, and the Jury Floor

The Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties, Article 46, is the boundary. Paragraph 1:

> "A State may not invoke the fact that its consent to express consent to be bound by a treaty has been expressed in violation of a provision of its internal law regarding competence to conclude treaties as invalidating its consent unless that violation was manifest and concerned a rule of its internal law of fundamental importance."

Paragraph 2 defines the manifest prong:

> "A violation is manifest if it would be objectively evident to any State conducting itself in the matter in accordance with normal practice and in good faith."

Both prongs are satisfied, and the evidentiary vector is entirely public:

1. **Fundamental.** The ZEDE grant purported to alienate incidents of sovereignty — territorial jurisdiction, the democratic franchise, the right of citizens to their own courts and juries — that any constitutional order reserves to its constituent power. The unanimous 128-0 repeal is the strongest objective evidence available anywhere that the polity counted the rule as fundamental.
2. **Manifest.** The constitutionality of the ZEDE regime was contested inside Honduras from its inception: the precursor model-city statute was struck down by the Honduran Supreme Court in 2012, before a reconstituted chamber permitted the regime to proceed. Any counterparty conducting normal diligence could see the defect from the constitutional text alone.
3. **Nemo dat.** The maxim applies in full: *nemo dat quod non habet* — no one can give what they do not have. A transient legislature cannot sell the republic, and a treaty chapter cannot launder a void grant into a vested right.

Then there is the jury floor. The constitutional right to trial by jury is the floor beneath which no republic may sell its citizens:

> "In Suits at common law, where the value in controversy shall exceed twenty dollars, the right of trial by jury shall be preserved…" (U.S. Const. Amend. VII; cf. Art. III judicial power.)

The ZEDE's private arbitration stack stripped that floor from everyone inside the zone, and a forum that exists only to enforce the strip cannot be the forum that finally adjudicates it. Constitutional jury sovereignty is binary: it is 1.0, or the republic is gone.
