## 2. Hub and Spokes: The Anatomy of a Sherman Act § 1 Violation Without a Single Phone Call

Section 1 of the Sherman Antitrust Act of 1890 (`15 U.S.C. § 1`) is 62 words of the bluntest statutory language Congress ever wrote:

> "Every contract, combination in the form of trust or otherwise, or conspiracy, in restraint of trade or commerce among the several States, or with foreign nations, is declared to be illegal."

A hub-and-spoke conspiracy is the modern shape this old statute was built to catch. Competing firms (the spokes) each sign a vertical contract with a common vendor (the hub). Taken one contract at a time, each vertical deal looks unremarkable — a landlord licensing software. Taken together, the hub becomes the conduit through which competitors coordinate with each other: the rim agreement. The DOJ's RealPage complaint pleads exactly this — that the shared algorithm consumes each landlord's non-public, competitively sensitive daily lease-renewal, move-out, and concession data, and returns rent recommendations conditioned on the *pooled cohort's* private state.

```

======================================================================================

   [SPOKE]             [SPOKE]             [SPOKE]             [SPOKE]

   (competes with      (competes with      (competes with      (competes with
    B, C, D)            A, C, D)            A, B, D)            A, B, C)

        (mu = +16.0% over the competitive market-clearing rent R = $2,000.00)

```

Three mechanism features convert "advisory software" into an enforced horizontal agreement:

1. **Private-data pooling.** No landlord pricing on its own data could reproduce the recommendation. The output is a function of the pooled cohort state — the informational signature of a combination.
2. **Auto-accept discipline.** When acceptance is the norm, deviation is a visible defection. The algorithm re-prices the cohort daily, so any undercutting is detected and absorbed within one pricing cycle: a repeated-game punishment structure that sustains collusion without a single explicit communication.
3. **Output withholding as the coordination device.** The recommendation encodes effective supply, not just price. Units held deliberately vacant are the cartel's production quota — and Sherman Act Section 1 reaches output-restriction agreements with the same per se force as classic price-fixing.

## 3. −0.25: Why Inelastic Shelter Demand Turns 4% Withholding Into a 16% Markup

Shelter is the one purchase a household cannot defer. A family can wait two years on a car, five on a renovation; it cannot wait one month on a roof. Empirical shelter demand elasticity is therefore brutally inelastic, and the specification locks it at **ε = −0.25**.

Under a constant-elasticity demand curve, the collusive markup follows from the withheld supply in one line of algebra:

$$\mu_{\text{cartel}} = -\frac{v_{\text{cartel}} - v_{\text{comp}}}{\varepsilon_{\text{shelter}}} = -\frac{0.065 - 0.025}{-0.25} = -\frac{0.040}{-0.25} = 0.16$$

Read that result carefully, because it is the entire business model:

* The competitive market clears at a vacancy rate of **v = 0.025** (2.5% of units frictionally empty between tenants).
* The cartel's engineered artificial-vacancy equilibrium sits at **v = 0.065** (6.5% empty — 4.0 percentage points of housing supply deliberately withheld from families who need it).
* Because demand elasticity is **−0.25**, every single withheld percentage point of supply returns **four** percentage points of price. The 4.0-point withholding returns a **+16.0% markup**: the competitive rent of **$2,000.00** per unit per month becomes a collusive rent of **$2,320.00**.

$$R_{\text{cartel}} = R_{\text{comp}} \times (1 + \mu_{\text{cartel}}) = 2,000.00 \times 1.16 = \$2,320.00$$

The algorithm never needs to "raise rents" in the theatrical sense. It only needs to coordinate the cohort's willingness to leave apartments empty — inelasticity does the rest, automatically, every month, in every market where the pooled cohort holds share.

## 4. The $219.20 Wedge: An NOI Identity That Pays More for Renting Fewer Apartments

The naive objection is obvious: if the cartel leaves more units empty, doesn't it lose revenue? The artificial-vacancy NOI identity says no — and the arithmetic is short enough to verify on a napkin.

```
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                 THE ARTIFICIAL-VACANCY NOI IDENTITY (PER DOOR, PER MONTH)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   REGIME                  | VACANCY v | OCCUPANCY o | RENT R      | E[Rev] = R x o
   ------------------------|-----------|-------------|-------------|---------------
   Competitive market      |   0.025   |    0.975    | $2,000.00   |  $1,950.00
   Algorithmic cartel      |   0.065   |    0.935    | $2,320.00   |  $2,169.20
   ------------------------|-----------|-------------|-------------|---------------
   PURE RENT-EXTRACTION WEDGE (Delta_NOI)                             +$219.20
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
```

**Competitive baseline.** Occupancy is the complement of vacancy: o = 1 − 0.025 = **0.975**. Expected monthly revenue per door:

$$\mathbb{E}[\text{Rev}_{\text{comp}}] = 2,000.00 \times 0.975 = \$1,950.00$$

**Cartel regime.** The collusive rent is $2,320.00 and occupancy falls to o = 1 − 0.065 = **0.935**. Expected monthly revenue per door:

$$\mathbb{E}[\text{Rev}_{\text{cartel}}] = 2,320.00 \times 0.935 = \$2,169.20$$

**The wedge.** The net collusive NOI gain per unit per month:

$$\Delta\text{NOI}_{\text{collusive}} = 2,169.20 - 1,950.00 = +\$219.20$$

The cartel rents *fewer* apartments and collects *more* money — **+$219.20 per door per month** — because the 16.0% markup on the 93.5% occupied share more than pays for the 6.5% deliberately vacant share. Three structural facts make this wedge legally radioactive:

1. **It is pure rent extraction.** No unit is improved, no service is added, no transaction cost is reduced. The entire gain comes from coordinating scarcity against inelastic demand — the textbook definition of an antitrust injury.
2. **The vacancy is an engineered input, not a demand failure.** The empty units are the cartel's output quota, held empty on purpose, at a jointly sustained level no independent landlord would tolerate.
3. **Intentional vacancy carries a hidden capex dividend.** Empty units accrue reduced physical wear-and-tear, cutting turnover and maintenance outlays exactly where the NOI-keyed securitization trust is most sensitive. The cartel saves money twice: once on the tenant's rent, once on the tenant's absence.

## 5. From Foreclosure Auction to Rated Bond: REO Securitization and the $48.0 Million Maintenance Strip

The pricing cartel is only Vector A. Vector B runs through the capital markets.

After the 2008 foreclosure crisis, institutional sponsors bought tens of thousands of foreclosed single-family homes (REO — real estate owned) in bulk at auction, assembled them into scattered-site rental portfolios, and financed them through single-family rental securitization trusts whose bond tranches are rated directly off portfolio net operating income. Follow the incentive gradient that structure creates:

* Every dollar of **deferred habitability maintenance** avoided flows into reported NOI.
* Every dollar of **junk fees** collected from tenants flows into reported NOI.
* Reported NOI flows into the rated bond cashflow, and the bond cashflow flows into the sponsor's cost of capital.

Maintenance stripping is therefore not an operational accident of managing scattered houses across forty metros. It is the *equilibrium behavior* of an NOI-keyed capital structure — and the federal docket has now priced it.

In *FTC v. Invitation Homes Inc.*, Case No. 1:24-cv-04252 (N.D. Ga.), the Federal Trade Commission alleged that the largest single-family REO operator charged tenants undisclosed junk fees and withheld or unreasonably delayed habitability maintenance while continuing to collect full rent. The matter resolved for a **$48.0 million** ($48,000,000.00) junk-fee and maintenance-withholding settlement. That number is the empirical calibration point for the entire securitization thesis: the conduct the NOI incentive gradient predicts is the conduct a federal enforcement action resolved for eight figures.

There is a second, quieter docket. In *City of Redondo Beach ex rel. Deckard Technologies v. Invitation Homes Inc.* (9th Cir.), a qui tam relator — a whistleblower suing on behalf of a municipality — pursued False Claims Act exposure over **unpermitted renovations** of REO homes: work executed without the municipal building permits that local code requires. Every unpermitted renovation becomes a per-claim statutory exposure, and the pairing of a city plaintiff with a private relator means the enforcement does not depend on any federal agency's priorities. The structural counter-primitive is the **municipal receivership clawback**: a municipality places a code-violating REO home into receivership, cures the habitability defect at public expense, and imposes a super-priority lien on the property — recapturing the stripped maintenance dollars directly out of the securitization trust's own collateral pool, one parcel at a time.

## 6. "Non-Binding Recommendations": The H0 Defense and Its One-Line Algebraic Falsification

State the defense at its strongest, as the defendants' public posture does. Under *Milkovich v. Lorain Journal Co.*, 497 U.S. 1 (1990), and *Partington v. Bugliosi*, 56 F.3d 1147 (9th Cir. 1995), honest analysis must fairly state the subject's own on-the-record position before falsifying it:

* **H0 (the null hypothesis):** Revenue-management software merely provides non-binding supply-and-demand decision support. Every landlord may accept or reject any recommendation at will, so no "contract, combination... or conspiracy" exists under `15 U.S.C. § 1`. And single-family REITs merely professionalize a historically fragmented scattered-site rental market with institutional standards and scale efficiency.

H0 sounds reasonable. It is also falsified by a single revealed-preference identity.

```
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                    H0 FALSIFICATION BY REVEALED PREFERENCE (ONE LINE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  IF H0 were true (recommendations non-binding):
      cost of deviation = 0
      => any landlord facing inelastic demand at v = 0.065 profitably undercuts
         the cohort by renting its vacant 4.0 pct at a marginally lower price
      => competitive arbitrage drives observed vacancy back to v_comp = 0.025
         within one leasing cycle

  OBSERVED (sustained, cohort-wide):
      v_cartel - v_comp = 0.065 - 0.025 = 0.040 > 0   (does NOT converge)

  => Only an enforced rim agreement -- private-data pooling + auto-accept
     discipline -- holds rational profit-maximizers at a jointly loss-making
     vacancy level.  H0 is falsified.  H1 stands.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
```

If the software were genuinely advisory, deviation would be free — and free deviation against inelastic demand is an arbitrage no rational landlord leaves on the table. The sustained 4.0-percentage-point divergence across the *entire pooled cohort* is incompatible with independent pricing. The governing identity's residual closes exactly (0.16 × (−0.25) + 0.040 = 0), confirming the 16.0% markup is fully attributable to coordinated withholding and to nothing else.

On the REO side, H0's "professionalization" claim is falsified by the $48.0 million federal settlement itself: professionalized rental supply does not resolve a federal junk-fee and maintenance-withholding action.

## 7. $7,891.20 Per Door Per Year: The Mandatory Treble Liquidation

Section 4 of the Clayton Act (`15 U.S.C. § 15`) does not say a court *may* award enhanced damages. It says any person injured in business or property by reason of anything forbidden in the antitrust laws "shall recover threefold the damages by him sustained, and the cost of suit, including a reasonable attorney's fee." Treble damages are mandatory. The per-unit arithmetic:

```
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

   Annual single damages:        219.20 x 12          = $2,630.40
   Mandatory treble (x3):        2,630.40 x 3         = $7,891.20
   Plus:                         cost of suit + reasonable attorney's fee
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
```

$$L_{\text{treble}} = \text{round}(219.2 \times 12 \times 3,\ 4) = \$7,891.20 \text{ per unit per year}$$

Every tenant who paid the collusive rent holds a statutory treble claim of **$7,891.20 per unit-year**, plus costs and fees. And here is the discovery trap that makes the claim self-proving: the overcharge is recorded in the cartel's own books — the algorithm's daily recommendation logs and the pooled telemetry that generated them. Any truth-defense ("our rents were independently set") requires the defendants to produce the exact unredacted pricing ledger in civil discovery, which is the very record that documents the pooling. The cartel's compliance archive is the plaintiff's damages model.

Scale the identity and the liquidation surface becomes visible: at $7,891.20 per unit-year, a pooled cohort of one hundred thousand doors carries **$789.12 million** of annual treble exposure — before costs, before fees, before the state *parens patriae* multipliers, and before the municipal receivership liens recapturing the stripped maintenance dollars parcel by parcel.
