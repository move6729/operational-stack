$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}(\mathbf{W}_S) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}_{\text{syn}}} \Bigl[ D_{\text{KL}}\bigl( P_{\mathbf{W}_T}(y \mid x; T) \;\big\|\; P_{\mathbf{W}_S}(y \mid x; T) \bigr) \Bigr]$$

#### Escape Hatch A: `RLVR` Description-Length Pruning (`DeepSeek-R1-Zero` & `GRPO`)
First-generation open models (2023–2024) used **Supervised Fine-Tuning (`SFT`) distillation**, which rewards the Student for mimicking every token of the Teacher's prose—including its managerial hedging.

> *"The reward signal is solely based on the correctness of final predictions against ground-truth answers, without imposing constraints on the reasoning process itself. Notably, we bypass the conventional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase before RL training. This design choice stems from our hypothesis that human-defined reasoning patterns may limit model exploration."*

Look at what happens mathematically when you switch the training loop from **SFT imitation** to **verifiable reward optimization (`RLVR`)** against a Python compiler, unit test suite, or formal math verifier $V(x, y) \in \{0, 1\}$ under a bounded context window $B_{\text{ctx}}$ and token-length cost $\lambda_{\text{len}} > 0$:

In fact, under a finite reasoning budget $B_{\text{ctx}} = 4,096\text{ tokens}$ where each discrete multi-hop deduction step requires $c_{\text{step}} = 256\text{ tokens}$, every token wasted on managerial throat-clearing directly crowds out logical deduction:
