### 2. COHEN-HAVLIN STAR-GRAPH PERCOLATION: THE 10,000x EXTORTION LEVERAGE MULTIPLIER

To understand why a three-person ransomware affiliate crew operating behind non-extraditable residential proxies can freeze $15,000$ North American auto dealerships or $72$ German municipalities in a single evening, one must abandon corporate cybersecurity marketing and apply the percolation physics of Reuven Cohen, Keren Erez, Daniel ben-Avraham, and Shlomo Havlin (2000, *Physical Review Letters* 85:4626; 2001, *Physical Review Letters* 86:3682).

For any communication or control graph $G = (V, E)$ with degree distribution $P(k)$, the structural integrity of the giant connected component under node removal is governed by the Molloy-Reed branching factor $\kappa$:

$$\kappa = \frac{\langle k^2 \rangle}{\langle k \rangle}$$

where $\langle k \rangle$ is the first moment (mean degree) and $\langle k^2 \rangle$ is the second moment (variance plus squared mean) of the vertex degree distribution. Per Cohen et al. (2000), the critical percolation threshold $p_c$—the fraction of edges or vertices that must be severed to shatter the giant connected component—satisfies:

$$p_c = \frac{1}{\kappa - 1} = \frac{1}{\frac{\langle k^2 \rangle}{\langle k \rangle} - 1}$$

Compare two ways of organizing $N = 15,000$ operating units (such as the $15,000$ dealerships on the `CDK Global` DMS platform, or $15,000$ decentralized industrial/municipal edge nodes):

1. **Architecture A — Autonomous Local-First Peer Mesh ($G_{\text{mesh}}$):** Each of the $N = 15,000$ nodes executes its own local database, inventory ledger, and PLC control loop on local silicon, maintaining $k_0 = 4.0$ asynchronous peer-to-peer settlement channels with neighboring suppliers or clearing nodes. Because the degree distribution is homogeneous ($k_i = 4$ for all $i$), the first moment is $\langle k \rangle = 4.0$, the second moment is $\langle k^2 \rangle = 16.0$, and the branching factor is:
   $$\kappa_{\text{mesh}} = \frac{16.0}{4.0} = 4.0 \implies p_{c,\text{mesh}} = \frac{1}{4.0 - 1} = 0.333333$$
   The critical removal fraction required to collapse the network is $f_{c,\text{mesh}} = 1 - p_{c,\text{mesh}} = 0.666667$. To halt systemic commerce across this federation, an adversary must individually breach, reverse-engineer, and encrypt $N_{\text{comp,mesh}} = f_{c,\text{mesh}} \times N = 10,000$ independent local nodes.

2. **Architecture B — Centralized SaaS / *Systemhaus* Star Monoculture ($S_N$):** All $N = 15,000$ leaf nodes ($k_{\text{leaf}} = 1$) are stripped of local state custody and tethered synchronously to a single central hub vertex ($k_{\text{hub}} = N = 15,000$), yielding $V = N + 1 = 15,001$ total vertices. Across $S_{15,000}$, the mean degree and second moment are:
   $$\langle k \rangle_{\text{star}} = \frac{2N}{N + 1} = \frac{30,000}{15,001} = 1.999867$$
   $$\langle k^2 \rangle_{\text{star}} = \frac{N^2 + N}{N + 1} = \frac{225,015,000}{15,001} = 14,999.000067$$
   The resulting Molloy-Reed branching factor explodes by nearly four orders of magnitude:
   $$\kappa_{\text{star}} = \frac{\langle k^2 \rangle_{\text{star}}}{\langle k \rangle_{\text{star}}} = \frac{N + 1}{2} = \frac{15,001}{2} = 7,500.5$$

As Cohen, Erez, ben-Avraham, and Havlin proved in their landmark 2001 paper (*Phys. Rev. Lett.* 86:3682, "Breakdown of the Internet under Intentional Attack"), scale-free and star graphs exhibit an extreme duality between random failure and targeted attack. If random leaves fail (a local dealership loses power), the hub survives. Under an intentional, targeted attack directed at the highest-degree vertex ($k_{\text{max}} = 15,000$), however, the critical targeted removal fraction collapses to a single vertex:

$$f_{\text{targeted,star}} = \frac{1}{N + 1} = \frac{1}{15,001} \approx 0.00006666 \quad \left(N_{\text{comp,star}} = 1\right)$$

Dividing the adversary's required node-compromise work in the decentralized mesh by the required work in the centralized star yields the **Adversary Extortion Leverage Multiplier ($\Lambda_{\text{extort}}$)**:

$$\Lambda_{\text{extort}} = \frac{N_{\text{comp,mesh}}}{N_{\text{comp,star}}} = \frac{\left(1 - \frac{1}{k_0 - 1}\right) N}{1} = \frac{10,000}{1} = 10,000.0\times$$

Centralizing $15,000$ dealerships onto `CDK Global`—or centralizing $30\%$ of all U.S. healthcare claims clearing onto `UnitedHealth Group Inc.`'s (`NYSE: UNH`, `CIK 0000731766`) `Change Healthcare Inc.` subsidiary, or pushing an unverified Ring-0 kernel channel file (`C-00000291*.sys`) across $8.5\text{ million}$ enterprise Windows endpoints via `CrowdStrike Holdings, Inc.` (`NASDAQ: CRWD`, `CIK 0001535527`) on July 19, 2024—does not reduce systemic risk. #### Case A: Public-Sector Refusal & Non-Ergodic Kingman Queue Blowup (`Südwestfalen-IT`)

When the `Akira` ransomware group encrypted `Südwestfalen-IT` (`SIT`) on October 30, 2023, the municipal consortium followed state interior ministry directives and refused to pay the ransom. In an uncoupled municipal network ($\beta = 0$), each town hall processes citizen applications locally with base manual exception arrival rate $\lambda_0 = 2.0\text{ exceptions/hr}$. When $N = 72$ municipalities are synchronously coupled into a single shared database where cross-departmental record locks interact ($\beta = 0.35$), total exception arrival traffic scales super-linearly:

$$\Lambda(N) = N \cdot \lambda_0 \cdot N^{\beta} = 72 \times 2.0 \times 72^{0.35} = 359.544\text{ exceptions/hr}$$

With the central `SIT` servers offline and municipal clerks forced onto emergency paper ledgers and isolated makeshift laptops, the degraded central remediation capacity was bounded at $\mu_{\text{central}} = 180.0\text{ exceptions/hr}$. The resulting system utilization ratio $\rho$ breached the ergodic stability boundary ($\rho = 1.0$) by nearly a factor of two:

$$\rho = \frac{\Lambda(N)}{\mu_{\text{central}}} = \frac{359.544}{180.0} = 1.997467 > 1.0$$

In Kingman's G/G/1 waiting-time approximation, expected queue wait time $\mathbb{E}[W_q] \propto \frac{\rho}{1 - \rho}$ diverges to $+\infty$ as $\rho \ge 1.0$. Rather than clearing, the municipal backlog grew deterministically at a net drift rate of:

$$\Delta Q = \Lambda(N) - \mu_{\text{central}} = 359.544 - 180.0 = +179.544\text{ backlogged citizen filings/hr}$$

Over the $6\text{-month}$ ($4,320\text{-hour}$) outage window, the 72 municipalities accumulated an aggregate backlog of $4,320 \times 179.544 = 775,630$ uncompleted citizen transactions. Auto dealers across South Westphalia could not register sold cars; parents waited months for statutory *Elterngeld*; real estate transfers stalled because municipal tax clearance certificates (*Unbedenklichkeitsbescheinigungen*) could not be printed. Because a municipal government cannot be forced into insolvency by angry citizens, the state absorbed the attack by transferring $775,630$ units of deadweight temporal friction onto the household sector.
