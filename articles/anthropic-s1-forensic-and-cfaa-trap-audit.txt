I. THE BLANK SPACE: A QUARTER LEFT LITERALLY EMPTY
=====================================================================

On October 26, 2026, Anthropic, PBC filed its Form S-1 registration statement (SEC EDGAR CIK 0002130357; Nasdaq: ANT), with Ernst & Young LLP auditing FY2025 (Exhibit 23.2) and Grant Thornton LLP auditing FY2023–FY2024 (Exhibit 23.1). In the prospectus summary, the MD&A, and the financial statements, the company printed this (S1-QUOTE-01 — Form S-1 pp. 2, 19, 86, F-4):

    "Revenue was $41 million, $386 million, and $4,585 million for the years ended December 31, 2023, 2024, and 2025, respectively, and $16,286 million for the six months ended June 30, 2026... revenue growing from $0 at commercial launch in the first quarter of 2023 to $          for the three months ended September 30, 2026... while our revenue for the three months ended September 30, 2026 was [blank]"

The string "$          " — a dollar sign followed by ten blank spaces — is not a rendering artifact. It is the company's chosen presentation of its most recent quarter: literally empty, repeated at page 2 (prospectus summary), page 86 (MD&A), and page F-4 (interim financial statements).

The missing number was neither unknown nor undisclosed. On October 14, 2026 — day 135 of the de facto quiet period that began with the June 1, 2026 Rule 135 confidential submission (17 C.F.R. § 230.135) — the company's Investor Day disseminated the Q3 2026 result: $16,800 million of revenue, $1.70 of revenue per $1 of compute, and an adjusted operating margin of +11%. The S-1's own disclosures independently pin the figure from the other direction: page 8 claims TTM revenue per employee "over $6.6 million" against a disclosed headcount of 5,300, implying a TTM revenue floor of $34,980 million. Subtract the disclosed Q1 2026 quarter ($4,731 million), the disclosed Q2 2026 quarter ($11,555 million), and the FY2025-audited implied Q4 2025 quarter ($2,040 million), and the Q3 2026 floor back-solves to $16,654 million — 99.1% of the leaked $16,800 million.

The number was therefore knowable, known, and selectively disseminated twelve days before filing. The registration statement nonetheless leaves it blank. That asymmetry — not the growth story bracketing it — is the central forensic fact of this offering. A blank field where a selectively disclosed number belongs is differential disclosure, and it sits inside the disclosure-integrity surface of Rule 408(a), Item 105 of Regulation S-K (17 C.F.R. § 229.105), and — for the October 14 dissemination itself — the offer-conditioning prohibitions of Section 5 of the Securities Act (15 U.S.C. §§ 77e(b)(1), 77e(c)).

III. THE Q2 2026 RECLASSIFICATION: ABOLITION OF GROSS PROFIT
=====================================================================

In Q2 2026 — the same quarter as the first positive operating income — the company changed its income-statement classification (S1-QUOTE-02 — MD&A Reclassification note):

    "In the second quarter of 2026, we changed the presentation of our consolidated statements of operations from a functional classification, where research and development, sales and marketing, general and administrative, and cost of revenue were presented as separate line items, to a classification based on the nature of expenses. As a result of this change in presentation, cost of revenue, gross profit (loss), research and development, sales and marketing, and general and administrative are no longer presented as separate line items in the consolidated statements of operations. Under the new presentation, expenses are presented by major categories, including compute and infrastructure; personnel; sales, marketing, and partnerships; Bartz settlement; and other operating and administrative."

The effect is the abolition of cost of revenue and gross profit as reported lines. For a company whose dominant input is inference compute, gross margin is the single most informative unit-economics metric available to an outside reader; its removal converts the income statement from a margin document into a spend document. Post-compute margins must now be reconstructed manually from the disclosed categories: 28.4% (Q1 2026), 51.9% (Q2 2026), 45.1% (H1 2026) — and, on the leaked Q3 figures, a 41.2% ceiling (compute cost share 0.588235; post-compute gross margin ceiling 0.411765).

The metrics retained and promoted are non-GAAP constructs (S1-QUOTE-03 — Form S-1 p. 85):

    "Our high talent density, efficient go-to-market, and how extensively our teams leverage Claude in their work help to drive improved operating margin, Adjusted Operating Margin, and revenue per employee... We define Adjusted Operating Income (Loss) as operating loss excluding (i) stock-based compensation expense, (ii) non-cash charitable contributions, and (iii) the Bartz settlement. We define Adjusted Operating Margin as Adjusted Operating Income (Loss) divided by revenue."

Adjusted operating income excludes stock-based compensation ($2,185 million in H1 2026), non-cash charitable contributions, and the $1,500 million Bartz settlement. Item 10(e) of Regulation S-K (17 C.F.R. § 229.10(e)) permits such measures but demands reconciliation and prohibits misleading prominence; the structural risk is that the non-GAAP lens becomes the only lens once gross profit no longer exists. The Q2-to-Q3 comparison is the exposure: adjusted operating margin falls from 19% (exactly 19.342276%, on $2,235 million over $11,555 million) to a leaked 11% — approximately 800 basis points — while revenue per $1 of compute falls from 2.078985 to 1.70 (-18.229336%) and the compute cost share rises from 48.1% to 58.8%. The quarter in which the margin inflected down is the quarter the company declined to print. The reclassification and the blank space are the same disclosure strategy viewed from two quarters.

The arithmetic: $321.4 billion of undiscounted lease obligations plus $265.6 billion of expected fixed or minimum purchase commitments, plus $125.0–$150.0 billion of additional TPU procurements explicitly excluded from the commitments table, plus more than $20.0 billion of AMD Helios commitments also excluded, yields total compute and lease encumbrance of $732.0–$757.0 billion — approximately 21x the $34,980 million TTM revenue floor and 44.9–46.5x H1 2026 revenue. Three structural features matter for credit analysis. (1) Take-or-pay form: the Amazon, Google, and Microsoft agreements commit spend "regardless of actual usage" with cash true-ups on shortfall — obligations that behave like debt in distress even when accounted as purchase commitments. (2) Front-loaded run-rate: the SpaceXAI arrangements alone reach approximately $3.1 billion per month once capacity delivers in December 2026 — an annualized spend run-rate of approximately $37.2 billion against a leaked Q3 quarterly revenue of $16.8 billion. (3) Circularity (S1-QUOTE-06 — MD&A):

    "During the years ended December 31, 2023, 2024, and 2025, revenue generated from sales through CSP marketplaces to customers represented approximately 11%, 32%, and 47% of total revenue, respectively. During the six months ended June 30, 2025 and 2026 (unaudited), revenue generated from sales through CSP marketplaces to customers represented approximately 44% and 48% of total revenue, respectively."

The marketplace operators (Amazon, Google, Microsoft) are simultaneously the counterparties holding at least $269.4 billion of the commitment stack ($110.0 + $114.5 + $44.9). Revenue recycled through the sales channels of one's largest landlords is vendor-financed demand: its durability is a function of the landlords' continued willingness to finance it, not of end-customer willingness to pay.

VI. THE 5%–7% ADMISSION: POLICY-VIOLATION REVENUE RECOGNIZED AT SCALE
=====================================================================

The S-1 contains this admission (S1-QUOTE-04 — MD&A / Risk Factors):

    "In addition, our revenue has included, and we expect will continue to include, amounts from customers that we subsequently determine are using our models, platform, and applications in violation of our usage policy or commercial terms. We believe this dynamic is common in our industry. Because detection, investigation, and enforcement take time, we recognize revenue from such customers when the related services are provided, before restricting or terminating their access. Based on our detection and review process, we estimate that revenue from such customers represented approximately 5% to 7% of our revenue for the nine months ended September 30, 2026. This estimate requires judgment and may change as our detection methodologies evolve and we identify previously undetected usage."

The arithmetic: implied nine-month 2026 revenue is $33,086 million ($4,731 + $11,555 + $16,800). Five to seven percent of that is $1,654.3–$2,316.0 million — between $1.65 billion and $2.32 billion of revenue from customers the company has determined violate its usage policy or commercial terms. Three points. First, scale: the nine-month violation-revenue estimate equals 36.1%–50.5% of the entire FY2025 audited revenue base ($4,585 million). Second, recognition posture: revenue is recognized "before restricting or terminating their access" — booked while the underlying contractual breach is known and uncured. That posture raises direct questions under FASB ASC 606-10-25-1(e) (whether collectibility and contract enforceability survive a known counterparty violation), ASC 606-10-25-7 (reassessment of contract-existence criteria when facts change), and ASC 606-10-25-19 (continued service despite violation as an implicit modification of contract terms). Third, non-stationarity: the company concedes the estimate "requires judgment and may change as our detection methodologies evolve." The error bar on revenue quality is itself a moving target — every detection improvement retrospectively impairs previously recognized revenue, so the 5%–7% range should be read as a lower bound conditioned on current detection capability. The claim that this dynamic is "common in our industry" is unquantified and cites no peer disclosure. For modeling: haircut revenue quality 500–700 basis points and treat the delta as recurring.

The S-1 filed ten days later contains no agent-hours claim. Both disappearances are silent; neither is footnoted.

O.C.G.A. § 16-9-93 criminalizes unauthorized access to a computer system: the access token is the technical authorization boundary, and obtaining it through a loophole in a legacy endpoint to reach fee-gated data without payment is unauthorized access regardless of whether the underlying data is "public" through some other channel. 18 U.S.C. § 1030(a)(2)(B)–(C) — the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act — reaches obtaining information from a protected computer where the conduct involves interstate communication; the fee gate and the token circumvention supply the "without authorization or exceeds authorized access" element. Post-hoc mitigation does not un-complete the access. Whether federal or Georgia state prosecutors charge a confirmed model-initiated bypass as a CFAA event is a charging decision; the omission of the event from Item 103 (17 C.F.R. § 229.103) and Item 105 of a registration statement filed within 24–48 hours of confirmation is a disclosure decision, and that decision was the company's.

X. SECTION 5 GUN-JUMPING, THE QUIET PERIOD, AND RESCISSION SURFACE
=====================================================================

The timeline: June 1, 2026 — Rule 135 confidential submission (17 C.F.R. § 230.135), starting the clock; 17 C.F.R. § 230.163B permits only wall-crossed testing-the-waters communications with qualified institutional buyers and institutional accredited investors. October 14, 2026 (day 135) — Investor Day dissemination of the Q3 result ($16,800 million; $1.70 per $1 compute; +11% adjusted operating margin). October 16, 2026 (day 137) — employment report with the 2–4x RPE and 6:1 agent-hours claims. October 26, 2026 (day 147) — public Form S-1 with the Q3 revenue field blank and the October 16 claims deleted.

Section 5 makes it unlawful to offer or sell a security before a registration statement is filed (15 U.S.C. § 77e(b)(1)) or before it becomes effective (§ 77e(c)). The statutory question for the October 14 and October 16 events is whether the communications, taken in context, constituted offers or offer-conditioning activity for the registered securities. Disseminating the current quarter's headline metrics — metrics then omitted from the registration statement — to investors and press twelve days before filing is the paradigm gun-jumping fact pattern. If the communications are offers, § 77l(a)(1) gives purchasers a rescission remedy against sellers; if the registration statement's omissions are material — the blank quarter, the deleted productivity claims, the omitted incident ledger — §§ 77k and 77l(a)(2) supply the misstatement and omission remedies. Regulation FD (17 C.F.R. § 244.100) does not yet bind a pre-effective issuer, but the October 14 selective disclosure is its precise policy target, and the staff will read the blank field against it.

The litigation surface is therefore three-layered: (1) § 5 gun-jumping on the October 14/16 communications, with rescission under § 77l(a)(1); (2) §§ 77k/77l(a)(2) on the registration statement itself — the blank Q3 field, the abolished gross profit against Item 10(e) non-GAAP prominence, the 5%–7% violation-revenue recognition posture, and the omitted Items 103/105 incidents; and (3) Delaware PBC fiduciary claims against the Class T/LTBT control structure. Each layer is independently viable; together they convert the offering's disclosure strategy into the plaintiff bar's complaint index.
