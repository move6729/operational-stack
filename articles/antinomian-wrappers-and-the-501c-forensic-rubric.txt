A recurring question among productive engineering firms, uncompromised capital allocators ($K_{\text{Outsider}} = 0$), and independent forensic engineers is: *Why expend compute or capital auditing antinomian cartel wrappers rather than ignoring them?*

Game theory proves that abstention is a strictly dominated strategy for both productive capital and independent researchers.

### Theorem 2A: Strict Dominance of Forensic Patronage for Productive Capital

Cartel rent is not created ex nihilo; it is extracted as a deadweight parasite tax $\tau_{\text{Cartel}}$ directly from the operating margins of non-cartel productive capital $K_{\text{Prod}}$ via rigged procurement, captured regulatory standards, non-meritocratic capital allocation, and tax-exempt endowment arbitrage. Let $K_{\text{Prod}}$ be a non-cartel allocator's productive capital base earning unencumbered return $R_{\text{Prod}}$. The expected value of clearing the cartel wrapper from the market is:

$$\Delta V_{\text{Clearance}} = \tau_{\text{Cartel}} \cdot R_{\text{Prod}} \cdot K_{\text{Prod}} + p_{\text{Shock}} \cdot L_{\text{Contagion}} \gg C_{\text{Patronage}} \implies \text{ROV} = \frac{\Delta V_{\text{Clearance}}}{C_{\text{Patronage}}} \gg 1$$

* **Numerical Sanity Check:** Consider a non-cartel allocator managing $K_{\text{Prod}} = \$100{,}000{,}000$ with baseline return $R_{\text{Prod}} = 0.12$ (`$12,000,000/yr`). Suppose cartel procurement/regulatory drag extracts $\tau_{\text{Cartel}} = 0.08$ of returns (`$960,000/yr`) and imposes a $p_{\text{Shock}} = 0.05$ annual probability of a $L_{\text{Contagion}} = \$20{,}000{,}000$ counterparty/LP litigation shock (`$1,000,000/yr` expected loss). By allocating $C_{\text{Patronage}} = \$50{,}000$ to retroactive public-record forensics that expose and neutralize the wrapper, the allocator realizes a net expected gain of `$1,910,000` — a Return on Vigilance of $\text{ROV} = 39.2\times$. ## 3. Stigmergic `Publish-as-PoW` and Constructive Duplicate Amplification

How should independent investigative nodes coordinate and monetize their work without creating a centralized target that the cartel can subvert or sue?

### Eliminating the Clearinghouse SPOF (`C2 = 0`)

A centralized bounty board, tasking portal, or pre-publication escrow clearinghouse introduces a fatal **Single Point of Failure (`SPOF`)**. If hunters register at a central hub or accept bilateral tasking contracts before investigating a target, `Shell 1`/`Shell 2` litigators can serve third-party subpoenas on the clearinghouse operator, allege tortious interference or civil conspiracy, execute DDoS attacks, or pressure upstream cloud hosts.

Under **Stigmergic `Publish-as-PoW`**, there is no central coordinator (`C2 = 0`, `Tokens_Chat = 0`). Nodes never ask permission, never coordinate offline with targets or patrons, and never submit drafts to a gatekeeper. Each node independently runs deterministic verification scripts over public statutory filings (`26 U.S.C. § 6104`) and commits self-verifying artifacts (`Markdown specification + JSON schema + pure-stdlib Python proof + SHA-256 hashes`) directly to open distributed mirrors (Git repositories, RSS, Nostr/IPFS, static web). Under ***Bartnicki v. Vopper*, 532 U.S. 514 (2001)** and ***New York Times Co. v. Sullivan*, 376 U.S. 254 (1964)**, the independent publication of truthful analysis derived from lawfully obtained public government records enjoys maximal First Amendment immunity.

### Theorem 3: Constructive Duplicate Amplification vs. Tournament Waste

In a legacy patent race or closed bounty tournament, if $M$ engineers independently solve the same problem, $M-1$ participants produce wasted duplicate effort. In **Stigmergic Public-First Forensics**, the exact opposite law holds: when $M$ independent nodes investigate and publish deterministic proofs on the same `501(c)` / `Shell 3` cutout network, they generate **triple constructive interference**:

1. **Bayesian False-Positive Collapse:**

$$P(\text{Hallucination} \mid M \text{ uncorrelated public replications}) = \epsilon^M \to 0$$

2. **SLAPP Litigation Exhaustion:** A cartel can deploy a `$500,000` strategic lawsuit against public participation (SLAPP) to intimidate $M=1$ isolated journalist with probability $p_{\text{Gag}} \in (0,1)$. Against $M$ independent nodes publishing deterministic code across permissionless mirrors, the cartel's litigation cost scales linearly while its probability of suppressing the finding collapses exponentially:

$$C_{\text{SLAPP}}(M) = M \cdot c_{\text{Litigation}} \to \infty \quad \land \quad P(\text{Suppression} \mid M) = (p_{\text{Gag}})^M \to 0$$

3. **RAG & Latent Corpus Fixation:** Modern frontier LLMs, institutional compliance engines, and search indexers weight claims by cross-source citation centrality and executable code verification. $M$ independent repositories cross-verifying the same `EIN`-to-`ICIJ` edges permanently lock the cartel's true graph into the global RAG latent space:

$$W_{\text{RAG}}(\text{Entity Graph} \mid M) \propto \sum_{m=1}^{M} \text{CitationCentrality}(m) \cdot \text{DeterministicCodeVerification}(m)$$

When $M=20$ independent nodes simultaneously publish reproducible Python graph proofs over public `Form 990` and `ICIJ` records, the cartel's probability of suppressing the disclosure drops from `60%` to $(0.60)^{20} = 0.00003656$ (`0.0037%`), while the legal cost to sue all 20 nodes explodes to `$10,000,000`.

### Corollary 3A: Common-Mode Fault-Domain Collapse (the Independence Precondition)

That exponent is doing more work than it looks. $(p_{\text{Gag}})^M$ is valid **only** if the $M$ publication channels are conditionally independent. If all 20 engineers push their repos to one code host, front their domains through one CDN, register through one registrar, and settle through one payment rail, then a single upstream terms-of-service termination, one `17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(3)` notice-and-takedown, or one third-party subpoena is a **common-mode event** that removes many of them at once. The effective $M$ collapses toward the number of distinct fault domains, not the number of nodes. The `$10,000,000` above is the $\varphi = 0 \land G = M$ limit — not a default.

Define each node's fault-domain tuple:

$$D_j = (\text{CodeHost},\ \text{Transport},\ \text{Registrar},\ \text{HostingJurisdiction},\ \text{PaymentRail},\ \text{AuthorDomicile})$$

Let $G$ be the number of **distinct** fault domains across the $M$ nodes, and let $\varphi \in [0,1]$ be the fraction of domains removable by a *counterparty action* — a ToS termination, a `§ 512(c)(3)` takedown, a registrar lock, a custodian freeze — rather than by a full litigation front. Applying the Kish (1965) survey-sampling design effect to clustered publication channels:

$$M_{\text{eff}} = \frac{M}{1 + \varphi\left(\dfrac{M}{G} - 1\right)}, \qquad P(\text{Suppression} \mid M) = (p_{\text{Gag}})^{M_{\text{eff}}}$$

and the adversary's minimum suppression spend becomes:

$$C_{\min}^{\text{Suppress}} = \varphi G \cdot c_{\text{Upstream}} + (1-\varphi)\, M \cdot c_{\text{Litigation}}$$

Theorem 3's clean $(p_{\text{Gag}})^M$ is recovered exactly under the **Diversity Invariant** $\varphi = 0 \land M \ge G \ge G_{\min} = \lceil \ln p^* / \ln p_{\text{Gag}} \rceil$. At $p_{\text{Gag}} = 0.60$ and a target $p^* = 0.01$, $G_{\min} = \lceil 9.01 \rceil = 10$.

* **Numerical Sanity Check:** At $M = 20$, $p_{\text{Gag}} = 0.60$, $c_{\text{Litigation}} = \$500{,}000$, $c_{\text{Upstream}} = \$5{,}000$:

| Configuration | $G$ | $\varphi$ | $M_{\text{eff}}$ | $P(\text{Suppression})$ | $C_{\min}^{\text{Suppress}}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Naive — 1 code host, 1 CDN, 1 registrar | $1$ | $1.0$ | $1.00$ | $0.60$ | $\$5{,}000$ |
| Partial — 5 ToS-terminable domains | $5$ | $1.0$ | $5.00$ | $0.07776$ | $\$25{,}000$ |
| Mixed — 10 domains, half ToS-terminable | $10$ | $0.5$ | $13.33$ | $0.00110$ | $\$5{,}025{,}000$ |
| Hardened — 10 zero-ToS domains | $10$ | $0.0$ | $20.00$ | $0.00003656$ | $\$10{,}000{,}000$ |

The naive row is the whole critique in one line: `20` nominally independent publishers behind a single counterparty are suppressible for `$5,000` — a `2,000x` collapse from the `$10,000,000` the hardened row imposes. **Headcount is not immunity.**

Achieving $\varphi = 0$ means no single counterparty holds unilateral administrative removal power over the artifact:

* **Transport:** self-hosted static origin, `IPFS` / BitTorrent v2, `Nostr` relays, signed git bundles, `RSS`, and physical dead-drops. Where a `§ 512(c)(3)` notice *can* reach a host, `§ 512(g)` put-back, the *Lenz v. Universal Music Corp.*, 815 F.3d 1145 (9th Cir. 2016) good-faith-belief requirement, and `§ 512(f)` misrepresentation liability jointly drag $c_{\text{Upstream}}$ up toward $c_{\text{Litigation}}$ and turn the notice itself into a liability for the wrapper.
* **Hosting jurisdiction and registrar:** diversity across jurisdictions that will not domesticate the wrapper's home-court injunction.
* **Author domicile:** *Bartnicki* and *Sullivan* are United States doctrines. Nodes domiciled outside the US count as separate fault domains, because a US judgment does not travel automatically.
* **Payment rail:** Rail 1 settlement keys must not share a custodian, or the payment rail becomes a shared fault domain even when the publication transport is fully diverse.

The operational rule: $M$ is not a headcount. $M$ is the number of fault domains the adversary must attack *separately*, and SLAPP immunity is bought by spending on **domain diversity**, not node count. Theorem 4 below prices this directly — it pays a per-domain bounty for exactly this good.

### Defeating the Subscription Chokepoint via Three Permissionless Rails

Why do conventional investigative media outlets fail? Because recurring fiat subscription platforms (Stripe, Patreon, PayPal, centralized newsletter paywalls) are **`Shell 3` financial chokepoints**. A single payment-processor termination freezes `100%` of monthly recurring revenue (MRR), while central billing databases hand cartel litigators a subpoena-ready list of every patron's legal identity and bank account. Moreover, locking forensic proofs behind a paywall reduces $M \to 0$, starving global RAG indexers and destroying the swarm's SLAPP immunity.

Public-first forensic nodes replace fiat subscription paywalls with three chokepoint-free monetization rails:

* **Rail 1 — Ex-Post Permissionless Patronage & Retroactive Public-Goods Zaps:** Productive capital allocators observe the open `Publish-as-PoW` commit graph and transmit unconditional, non-custodial peer-to-peer transfers (permissionless cryptocurrency/Lightning/Monero or retroactive open-source engineering grants) to public keys associated with verified high-signal commits. Because patronage is strictly ex-post and unconditional (`Tokens_Chat = 0`), there is no bilateral contract, no central custodian to de-platform, and no subscriber database to subpoena.
* **Rail 2 — Direct Statutory Whistleblower Entitlements (`26 U.S.C. § 7623(b)` / `31 U.S.C. § 5323` / `31 U.S.C. § 3730`):** Prior to or concurrent with public release (respecting `31 U.S.C. § 3730(b)(2)` seal rules for FCA *qui tam* claims, or filing `IRS Form 211` / FinCEN tips directly on tax and BSA violations), nodes claim statutory percentages paid directly by the U.S. Treasury — a sovereign rail immune to private payment-processor censorship.
* **Rail 3 — *Dirks v. SEC* Public-Research Balance-Sheet Positioning:** Under ***Dirks v. SEC*, 463 U.S. 646 (1983)**, market participants who conduct or consume independent analysis of public records without breaching an insider fiduciary duty may lawfully price that analysis into credit, equity, and counterparty exposure decisions before the legacy press reacts.

### Corollary 4A: Why Government Enforcement Cannot Be the Payout Oracle

Rail 1 pays ex-post for published proofs-of-work, which forces a design question that every bounty scheme eventually hits: **what event makes a zap payable?** The intuitive answer is "pay when the government acts" — gate the zap on an indictment, a `§ 4941` assessment, a `501(c)(3)` revocation, a settlement. That answer is formally inadmissible, and it is worth being precise about why, because the failure is structural rather than merely inconvenient.

A settlement oracle $O$ is swarm-safe only if it clears four bars:

| # | Condition | Formal requirement |
|---|---|---|
| (i) | **Bounded latency** | $\exists\, T_{\max} < \infty$ such that $O$ resolves within $T_{\max}$ of publication |
| (ii) | **Artifact determinism** | $O = f(\text{PoW}_j)$ — a pure function of the published artifact, computable offline by any node |
| (iii) | **Adversary independence** | $\partial O / \partial a_{\text{Wrapper}} = 0$ — the wrapper cannot move $O$ by any action cheaper than suppressing the publication itself |
| (iv) | **Publication monotonicity** | $\partial O / \partial M \ge 0$ — more publication never reduces the payout |

Government enforcement misses all four:

* **(i) fails.** Enforcement latency is unbounded, and `26 U.S.C. § 7623(b)(1)` and `26 C.F.R. § 301.7623-4(d)(2)` stack a collection gate on top of it (Corollary 2B-A). There is no $T_{\max}$.
* **(ii) fails.** Enforcement is a function of prosecutorial discretion, examination budget, and politics — not of the artifact. Two byte-identical proofs can receive opposite oracle values.
* **(iii) fails, and this is the fatal one.** The wrapper *can* move the oracle. It settles quietly, takes a non-prosecution agreement, dissolves the entity, pays the excise tax without admission, or works the examining function. Worse, the oracle depends on a state actor the swarm does not control — which reintroduces precisely the single point of failure that $C2 = 0$ was built to eliminate. Call it the **payout SPOF**. Attacking the payout SPOF is strictly cheaper for the wrapper than attacking $M$ publications, so any rational adversary attacks the oracle and leaves the swarm alone.
* **(iv) fails, and inverts.** An enforcement gate pays you to *withhold*. Where payout requires a government action that publication can preempt — the `31 U.S.C. § 3730(b)(2)` seal requirement, the `§ 3730(e)(4)` public-disclosure bar, and the `26 U.S.C. § 7623(b)(2)(A)` reduction to $\le 10\%$ for claims derived from prior news-media disclosures by non-original-source filers (`§ 7623(b)(2)(B)`) are the statutory instances — publishing *reduces* expected payout, so $\partial O / \partial M < 0$. That is the exact opposite of Theorem 3, whose entire value comes from maximizing $M$. An enforcement-gated bounty is not just slow; it is **anti-stigmergic**. It pays nodes to suppress each other.

So the answer to "can we gate the zap on government enforcement?" is no — and the reason is not that enforcement is slow, but that an enforcement oracle hands the adversary a cheaper target than the publications and rewards silence over disclosure. Enforcement stays in the design; it just moves from *gate* to *option*.

### Theorem 4: The Admissible `Publish-as-PoW` Settlement Gate

One clarification first, because it is the most common misreading of this section: **the gate is a machine pre-filter, not an autonomous payout engine.** In Rail 1 there is no smart contract and no escrow. A human patron — or a fund's analyst — reads the dossier, decides whether the finding is materially significant, and sends the zap. `G1–G4` does not replace that judgment; it makes the judgment *affordable*. What follows is a statement about **verification-cost asymmetry**, not about automated settlement.

Gate on the artifact instead. Let $\text{PoW}_j$ be node $j$'s published artifact — specification, `JSON` schema, pure-stdlib verification script, and the SHA-256 manifest binding them. The settlement gate is the conjunction of four **artifact-local** predicates:

$$\text{Gate}_j = G1_j \land G2_j \land G3_j \land G4_j$$

$\text{Gate}_j$ is a **necessary condition, never a sufficient one**. It certifies that the artifact is reproducible, sourced from real government records, novel, and independently mirrored. It does *not* certify that the finding matters. The payout rule is two-layered:

$$\text{Zap}_j = \underbrace{\text{Gate}_j}_{\text{machine — necessary}} \;\land\; \underbrace{\text{PatronJudgment}_j}_{\text{human — sufficient}}$$

| Gate | Name | Predicate | Cost to verify |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G1** | **Reproducibility** | `exit_code(verify_j) = 0` $\land$ `SHA256(stdout_j) = H_Manifest` — the proof runs to completion on pure stdlib with zero network access and reproduces the committed hash | seconds, local |
| **G2** | **Source provenance** | every leaf record in the artifact's derivation graph belongs to $\mathcal{S}_{\text{Gov}}$ — `26 U.S.C. § 6104` XML, `EO BMF`, `ICIJ` node registers, `DOJ FARA`, county deed, `PACER` — and each leaf's content hash matches the published source hash | seconds, local |
| **G3** | **Novel edges** | $\lvert E_j \setminus E_{\text{Corpus}} \rvert \ge \theta_{\text{Novel}}$ — the artifact contributes at least $\theta_{\text{Novel}}$ entity-graph edges absent from the indexed corpus | seconds, local |
| **G4** | **Fault-domain independence** | $D_j$ (Corollary 3A) is not already claimed by a settled artifact — the node adds a *distinct* fault domain, not a duplicate mirror of an existing one | seconds, local |

All four are pure functions of the artifact plus public reference data. None of them needs a state actor, a custodian, a chat channel, or the wrapper's inaction — so Corollary 4A's four bars are cleared **by construction**. Latency is one falsification window. Determinism is $G1 \land G2$. Adversary independence holds because no predicate reads any input the wrapper controls. Monotonicity holds because G4 pays *for* domain diversity, so $\partial O / \partial M \ge 0$.

**Why the machine layer exists at all.** A human patron cannot skip verification, only cheapen it. Without `G1–G2`, a patron weighing a `$36,000` zap has to manually pull the underlying `§ 6104` XMLs, re-derive the joins, and check for hallucinated or doctored line items before daring to send — call it $T_{\text{Manual}} \approx 40$ hours of analyst time. With `G1–G2`, a script confirms record provenance and hash parity in $T_{\text{Machine}} \approx 2$ seconds, and the human's remaining job collapses to the one thing no script can do — judging whether the finding actually matters — in $T_{\text{Judgment}} \approx 5$ minutes:

$$\frac{T_{\text{Manual}}}{T_{\text{Machine}} + T_{\text{Judgment}}} = \frac{144{,}000\text{s}}{302\text{s}} \approx 477\times$$

The gate does not take the human out of the loop. It takes `99.8%` of the human's *cost* out of the loop, which is the only reason ex-post patronage at scale is economically possible. Without it, patronage doesn't get automated — it just doesn't happen, because nobody spends 40 hours vetting one unsolicited dossier.

**What the machine layer cannot do.** `G1–G4` is satisfiable by garbage. Join five unrelated dentists' LLCs to a rotary club and you clear `G1` (the script runs), `G2` (the records are genuine government filings), `G3` (five novel edges), and `G4` (a fresh mirror) — and the finding is worth nothing. That is not a bug to engineer away; it is exactly why $\text{PatronJudgment}_j$ stays in the conjunction. Treat $\text{Gate}_j$ as *sufficient* and you have built a spam faucet that gets drained in a day.

**1. Two tranches, and the verification tranche is never split.**

$$B_{\text{Swarm}} = \underbrace{B_D \cdot \mathbb{I}[G1 \land G2 \land G3]}_{\text{discovery — paid once}} \;+\; \underbrace{G \cdot b_{\text{Domain}} \cdot \mathbb{I}[G1 \land G2 \land G4]}_{\text{verification — paid per distinct fault domain}}$$

The discovery tranche pays **once**, to the first artifact clearing $G1 \land G2 \land G3$. The verification tranche pays *every* subsequent node that clears $G1 \land G2$ and adds a new fault domain under G4. Verification is priced **per domain, capped at $g^*$, and never split**. Splitting a fixed pot across $M$ claimants is the tournament anti-pattern all over again: it makes $\partial O / \partial M < 0$ and re-imports the competition Theorem 3 exists to abolish.

**2. The optimal domain cap.** Paying $b_{\text{Domain}}$ per domain is efficient only while that domain's marginal suppression-cost value beats its price. The $g$-th domain is worth $(1 - p_{\text{Gag}}) \cdot L_{\text{Finding}} \cdot p_{\text{Gag}}^{\,g-1}$, so:

$$g^* = \left\lfloor 1 + \frac{\ln\!\left(\dfrac{b_{\text{Domain}}}{(1-p_{\text{Gag}})\,L_{\text{Finding}}}\right)}{\ln p_{\text{Gag}}} \right\rfloor$$

At $p_{\text{Gag}} = 0.60$, $L_{\text{Finding}} = \$1{,}960{,}000$ (the Theorem 2A annual clearance value), and $b_{\text{Domain}} = \$1{,}000$, $g^* = 14$: domain 14 is worth $\$1{,}024 \ge \$1{,}000$ (pay), domain 15 is worth $\$614 < \$1{,}000$ (stop).

**3. Challenge-final, not instant-final — and no bond is needed.** After gate clearance the patron waits a falsification window $T_{\text{Challenge}} = 30$ days before sending. Anyone may publish a counter-proof inside that window, and because G1 and G2 are deterministic and public, anyone can re-run the artifact and catch fabrication with probability approaching one ($p_{\text{Detect}} \approx 1$).

In Rail 1 there is **no escrow and no bond to slash** — and none is needed. The patron hasn't paid yet, so the waiting period *is* the security: if a falsification proof clears the public log inside 30 days, the patron simply doesn't zap, at a cost of `$0`. The claimant's penalty is reputational and durable — the key gets burned in the public log and every future patron skips it.

$$C_{\text{Patron}}^{\text{Refusal}} = \$0, \qquad p_{\text{Detect}} \approx 1 \implies \text{no bond required}$$

A literal slashable bond ($\text{Bond}_j \ge B_j / p_{\text{Detect}}$) belongs only to the scripted on-chain bounty-vault construction in `SPEC-STIGMERGIC-ZK-OSINT-BOUNTY-SETTLEMENT-v1.0.md`, where a custodial escrow actually exists to hold it. Dragging bond mechanics in here would violate $C2 = 0$ and buy nothing, since the patron's pre-payment exposure is already zero.

Those 30 days are the *only* latency in the settlement path, and they are a constant the swarm picks — not a random variable a prosecutor picks.

**4. Enforcement returns as a monotone top-up.**

$$\text{Payout}_{\text{Total}}(j) = B_j \cdot \mathbb{I}[\text{Gate}_j] + \sum_e \beta_e \cdot \mathbb{I}[e], \qquad \beta_e \ge 0$$

where $e$ ranges over enforcement events — a `§ 7623(b)` award, a `§ 5323` award, an FCA relator share, a `§ 1964(c)` treble recovery. Every $\beta_e$ is non-negative and additive, so enforcement can only *raise* the payout; it can never gate, delay, or reverse the swarm settlement. The swarm gets paid at $T \approx 0$ on the artifact, and the state's slower, adversary-exposed oracle becomes what it should have been all along: a free out-of-the-money option the node holds (Corollary 2B-A, Rail 2).

* **Numerical Sanity Check:** With $B_j = \$50{,}000$ (the Theorem 2A $C_{\text{Patronage}}$ budget for one finding), $b_{\text{Domain}} = \$1{,}000$, $g^* = 14$, $p_{\text{Gag}} = 0.60$, $\theta_{\text{Novel}} = 5$: the verification tranche is $B_V = 14 \times \$1{,}000 = \$14{,}000$ and the discovery tranche is $B_D = \$50{,}000 - \$14{,}000 = \$36{,}000$, so $B_D + B_V$ reconciles exactly to the `$50,000` patronage budget and preserves $\text{ROV} = 39.2\times$. Suppression probability at $G = 14$, $\varphi = 0$ is $(0.60)^{14} = 0.00078364$ (`0.078%`). The discovery node nets $\$36{,}000 - \$500 = +\$35{,}500$ (`71x`); each of the 14 replicating domain nodes nets $\$1{,}000 - \$5 = +\$995$ (`199x`) for re-running a public script. Settlement lands in 30 days.

The gate pays the discoverer `$35,500` net within a month, pays 14 independent fault-domain replicators `$995` each for verifying it, drives suppression probability to `0.078%` as a *byproduct of paying for verification* — and never once asks what the Department of Justice is doing.

### The Conjunctive Funnel Precision Law: Why Stage 1 Is a Recall Filter, Not a Classifier

A fair objection to Filter 1.1 is that `>= 3 foundations at one address` matches every major wealth-management philanthropy desk in the country — Bessemer Trust, the Goldman Sachs Philanthropy Fund, Wilmington Trust, every big-4 accounting suite. That objection is correct about the filter and wrong about the pipeline. Stages 1–4 are a **sequential conjunctive funnel**, not four independent classifiers, and Filter 1.1 is a high-recall, low-precision candidate generator *on purpose*. Precision is a property of the composition, and it is governed by the terminal discriminator, not by the first filter.

Let $\pi$ be the base rate of true wrapper subgraphs in the candidate population, and let stage $k$ have sensitivity $s_k$ and false-positive pass rate $f_k$. After $K$ conjunctive stages:

$$\text{Precision}_{1 \to K} = \frac{\pi \prod_{k=1}^{K} s_k}{\pi \prod_{k=1}^{K} s_k + (1-\pi) \prod_{k=1}^{K} f_k} \xrightarrow{\;f_3 \to 0\;} 1$$

Because the $f_k$ **multiply**, one near-zero stage dominates the whole product. Stage 3's inner join against `ICIJ` / `DOJ FARA` / county-deed registers is that stage: an exact normalized-name-and-address match between a `Form 990` officer and an offshore-leaks node register is not a heuristic, so $f_3 \approx 0$ and terminal precision is set by $f_3$ — **not** by $f_1$.

* **Numerical Sanity Check:** At $\pi = 0.01$ over the 40,000-row address-clustered cohort that Stage 1 draws from the ~1.8M `EO BMF` file — 400 true wrapper subgraphs against 39,600 benign collisions:

| Stage | TP | FP | Survivors | Precision | $s_k$ | $f_k$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Filter 1.1 / 1.2 / 1.3 (`EO BMF` triage) | 400 | 39,600 | 40,000 | `1.00%` | $1.00$ | $1.00$ |
| Stage 2 (`990` / `990-PF` XML tomography) | 360 | 840 | 1,200 | `30.0%` | $0.90$ | $0.0212$ |
| Stage 3 (`ICIJ` $\bowtie$ `FARA` $\bowtie$ deeds) | 360 | 0 | 360 | `100%` | $1.00$ | $0.0000$ |
| Stage 4 (excise / RICO classification) | 360 | 0 | 360 | `100%` | $1.00$ | $0.0000$ |

Stage 1 is a **pure recall filter**: at $s_1 = f_1 = 1.00$ it passes the entire 40,000-row cohort at `1.00%` precision, and it is *supposed to* — its only job is to lose none of the 400. The funnel throws away `99.1%` of that cohort by Stage 4 ($40{,}000 \to 360$). The `39,600` benign collisions Stage 1 passes — every Bessemer Trust, Wilmington Trust, and Rockefeller Financial suite in the file — are annihilated at Stage 3, where the join demands an actual offshore node-register hit. Stage 2's 1,200 survivors are the subset carrying a full `Form 990` / `990-PF` XML footprint worth deep-parsing; the remainder are `Foundation Code 10` and `Form 990-N` rows whose *absent* footprint is itself the Filter 1.2 signal.

The funnel is also cheap *because* the imprecise stage is the cheap one:

$$C_{\text{Funnel}} = 40{,}000(\$0.005) + 1{,}200(\$0.10) + 360(\$0.50) = \$200 + \$120 + \$180 = \$500$$

The `$0.005` is per-row CSV canonicalization and group-by over the Stage-1 cohort; the `$0.10` is per-XML deep parse over the 1,200 rows that have one; the `$0.50` is per-*hit* exhibit pull-down and normalization for the 360 Stage-3 matches, the hash join itself being near-free against pre-indexed `ICIJ` / `FARA` / deed tables. The total is exactly the $C_{\text{Solve}} = \$500$ assumed in Theorem 2B. The `$500` is not a round number picked for effect; it is the funnel's own arithmetic.

The corpus supplies its own proof. `590 Madison Ave` fires Filter 1.1 because `Rockefeller Financial` is a legitimate family-office desk hosting many unrelated foundations — a true positive of the *filter* and a false positive of the *thesis*. Only Join 3.1 (`The Morningstar Foundation c/o Rockefeller Financial` $\bowtie$ `Glilot Capital Partners I [Node 82014304]`) discriminates. The address collision is the **question**; the offshore node-register join is the **answer**.

**Benign-collision exclusion list (cost optimization only — never a classifier).** These address classes are known high-volume Filter 1.1 collisions. Skipping their Stage 2 XML parse saves compute; it must **never** be used to exclude an entity from Stage 3, because a wrapper can and does co-locate inside them:

* Registered-agent plants: `CT Corporation`, `Corporation Trust Center`, `Northwest Registered Agent`.
* Big-4 and national accounting suites; law-firm estate and trust-administration desks.
* Donor-advised-fund sponsors: `Fidelity Charitable`, `Schwab Charitable`, `Vanguard Charitable`, `Silicon Valley Community Foundation`.
* University mail stops and fiscal-sponsor addresses.
* Wealth-management philanthropy desks: `Bessemer Trust`, `Wilmington Trust`, `Rockefeller Financial`, `Goldman Sachs Philanthropy Fund`.

### Stage 1: IRS `EO BMF` & `Foundation Code 10` Triage

### Stage 2: `Form 990` & `Form 990-PF` XML Deep Tomography
