## 1. The Regime Wire: Disruption as a Procurement Costume

Every capital cycle dresses its rent extraction in the vocabulary of its era's most admired industry. The venture-backed defense sector — the "American Dynamism" cohort of neo-prime startups — arrived wearing two costumes at once: the anti-monopoly disruptor, storming the legacy prime oligopoly with software-defined speed, and the patriotic risk-bearer, funding that storm with private venture capital instead of taxpayer appropriations.

Strip the costumes and one structure remains. A drone whose bill of materials costs $18,000.00 (`boutique_vc_drone_bom_cost_usd` = 18000.0) sells to the government for $200,000.00 a unit (`boutique_vc_drone_unit_price_usd` = 200000.0). That is a 91.0% gross margin (`boutique_vc_gross_margin_ratio` = 0.91), and it is not produced by engineering. It is produced by procurement routing — by the choice of statute the transaction travels through. And when the platform meets a real electromagnetic fight, the physics completes the inversion: the boutique drone finishes its mission 8.0% of the time, the $500 commodity drone finishes 75.0% of the time, and the cost of one successful effect separates by a factor of 3,750.0.

This is the story of a margin manufactured by law and deleted by physics — and of the statutory clawback that manufactures itself in the same instant.

The two costumes matter because each blocks a different audit. The disruptor costume blocks the monopoly question — nobody investigates a startup for being small — and the risk-bearer costume blocks the subsidy question, because private capital is presumed to be disciplining the price. Between the two blocked questions sits one unexamined number: $200,000.00 for $18,000.00 of hardware. This audit examines exactly that number, in three passes — the routing that protects it, the physics that deletes it, and the statute that trebles it.

## 2. The Wrapper Stack: How OTA + FAR Part 12 Erases the Audit

The Truth in Negotiations Act (TINA, 10 U.S.C. §§ 3701–3708) exists for exactly this situation: it forces contractors to hand the government certified cost-or-pricing data before a negotiated price locks, so the buyer can see the seller's numbers. The venture-defense wrapper routes around TINA in three statutory hops.

Hop one: the prototype Other Transaction Authority. 10 U.S.C. § 4022 lets DoD award prototype agreements outside the Federal Acquisition Regulation — sold to startups as speed, functioning in practice as the on-ramp. Hop two: the follow-on production OTA, which the statute permits the government to award to the same recipient, sole-source, once the prototype "successfully completes." Hop three: the FAR Part 12 commercial-item designation, under which 10 U.S.C. § 3451 exempts the buy from certified cost-or-pricing data entirely, with Cost Accounting Standards exiting alongside it.

```
  [Venture-Backed Neo-Prime Wrapper]
              |
              v
  [10 U.S.C. 4022 Prototype OTA]  <-- "speed", "non-traditional entrant"
              |
              v
  [Sole-Source Follow-On Production OTA]  <-- no competition required
              |
              v
  [FAR Part 12 "Commercial Item" Designation]  <-- 10 U.S.C. 3451
              |
              +--X-->  TINA certified cost-or-pricing data: EXEMPT
              +--X-->  Cost Accounting Standards: EXEMPT
              |
              v
  [$200,000.00 unit price on an $18,000.00 bill of materials]
              |
              v
  [91.0% gross margin, zero government audit surface]
```

The routing is rarely visible in a single document. Prototype OTAs flow through innovation units and OTA consortiums, and the consortium layer interposes an intermediary between the government and the performing contractor. DoD Office of Inspector General audits of OTA consortiums have flagged, repeatedly, the same shape: follow-on production awards where the government holds neither certified cost data nor market comparables — the two inputs the statute and the regulation each presume, and each dispense with. The audit trail that would discipline the price is the audit trail the routing was selected to avoid.

```
  Hop 1                 Hop 2                      Hop 3
  Prototype OTA   --->  Follow-On Production   -->  FAR Part 12
  (10 U.S.C. 4022)      OTA, sole-source            "Commercial Item"
  "speed"               "successful prototype"      (10 U.S.C. 3451)
                                                        |
                                                        v
                                      TINA + CAS: exempt by designation
```

The load-bearing element is hop three. TINA's commercial-item exemption assumes the price was discovered by an actual commercial market — arm's-length comparables that discipline the number. A sole-source follow-on production OTA with no comparable commercial sales has no market price. It has a cost-plus price wearing a commercial exemption. DoD Office of Inspector General audits of OTA consortiums have flagged precisely this gap repeatedly: follow-on production awards where the government holds neither certified cost data nor market comparables. The designation performs the job the market was supposed to do, minus the market.

## 3. Ninety-One Percent: The Unit Economics of a Defense SaaS Multiple

Run the arithmetic once, slowly.

```
  Unit price (sole-source OTA)          P_vc  = $200,000.00
  Bill of materials                     C_bom =  $18,000.00
  -----------------------------------------------------------
  Gross profit per unit                        $182,000.00
  Gross margin        (P_vc - C_bom)/P_vc      0.91  = 91.0%
  Implied markup      (P_vc - C_bom)/C_bom     10.1111 = 1,011.1%
```

Note what the margin is not. It is not a learning-curve artifact, not a low-rate-initial-production penalty, not non-recurring engineering being amortized. Those compress toward cost as volume rises. This structure requires the price to stay pinned at 11.1x bill of materials while volume is precisely what the sole-source routing guarantees no competitor can contest. The margin is the product; the procurement route is the factory.

A 1,011.1% markup over bill of materials is not a hardware number; it is a valuation number. Venture mathematics prices growth and recurring margin, and the wrapper's pitch is that defense revenue can carry software-like gross margin on government lock-in. The 91.0% margin is the artifact that lets a drone company be valued like a SaaS company.

Meanwhile the same $200,000.00 buys 400 commodity attrition airframes at $500.00 each (`unit_acquisition_cost_ratio` = 400.0). One boutique unit is four hundred bare-metal FPVs, before a single one of them has to survive anything.

## 4. The Eight-Percent Problem: Contested EW Deletes the Datalink Drone

The boutique platform's operating concept depends on the electromagnetic environment behaving like a California test range: GNSS available, datalink clean, cloud reach-back intact. High-density electronic warfare — GNSS spoofing stacked on broadband RF jamming — removes all three at once. A cloud- and datalink-dependent airframe does not degrade gracefully under that regime; it collapses. The locked contested-EW mission completion probability for the boutique platform is 0.08 (`contested_ew_boutique_survival_prob` = 0.08): eight missions in a hundred deliver their effect.

The commodity counter-architecture is built from the opposite premise. A $500.00 bare-metal or fiber-optic FPV (`commodity_bare_metal_fpv_unit_cost_usd` = 500.0) carries RF-immune guidance — a fiber spool or onboard edge autonomy — and has no datink to jam, no constellation to spoof, no cloud to reach. Its contested-EW completion probability is 0.75 (`fiber_optic_or_edge_fpv_survival_prob` = 0.75). Attrition is the design environment, not the exception to it.

The survival asymmetry is physics, not brand preference. Spoofing corrupts the constellation the boutique airframe navigates by; jamming severs the datalink it is steered over; reach-back latency breaks the cloud loop it is managed through. Remove all three and what remains is a $200,000.00 airframe with no guidance authority. A fiber-optic spool cannot be jammed, because it radiates nothing; edge autonomy cannot be spoofed, because it listens to nothing external. RF immunity is purchased here with copper and glass at $500.00, not with software at $200,000.00.

## 5. The 3,750x Inversion: Cost Per Successful Effect

Cost per successful effect is the only unit economics that survives contact with attrition: the price divided by the probability the price lands.

```

  E4:  CPE_fpv = C_fpv / p_fpv =     500.0 / 0.75 = $666.666667 per effect

  Cost per successful effect, contested EW (USD, log-scale feel):

  Boutique VC drone   |########################################| $2,500,000.00
  Commodity FPV       |#| $666.666667

  E5:  Lambda = CPE_vc / CPE_fpv
             = (P_vc * p_fpv) / (C_fpv * p_vc)

Three thousand seven hundred fifty. It exceeds 1,000x for any boutique survival probability below 0.30. Cost-per-effect parity at any shared survival probability requires the boutique unit price to fall to $500.00 or less — a 99.75% price collapse. The parity survival probability at the locked price, p_parity = 200,000.0 / 666.666667 = 300.0, is a number greater than one: no reliability program ever flown can close the gap, because probabilities cap at 1.0 and the gap is 300 wide. At $200,000.00, the boutique drone cannot engineer its way out of the inversion. It can only be bought by someone who is not paying with effect.

## 6. The Risk-Transfer Defense (H0) — and the Identity That Falsifies It (H1)

The sector's on-the-record defense is ready, and it is the null hypothesis this audit tests. H0, stated publicly by venture-defense entrants and their investors: fixed-price FAR Part 12 and OTA procurement transfers R&D and production risk off taxpayers and onto private venture capital; the premium over cost is the market price of that risk transfer; the 91.0% margin is the reward for bearing failure risk the government would otherwise carry.

H0 has one falsification condition, and it is an accounting identity. Risk transfer requires a price discovered under risk — a competitive market where the seller can fail and the number can clear or not. A sole-source follow-on production OTA with zero commercial market comparables has no discovered price. Its "fixed price" is a cost-plus number wearing a commercial exemption, and the premium accrues precisely because competition and certified cost data were routed around, not because risk was priced. The identity makes the gap measurable:

```
  Implied markup under H0's "market price":  (200,000.0 - 18,000.0) / 18,000.0
                                           = 1,011.1%
  Allowable defense profit cap (pi_cap):       15.0%
  Ratio of implied markup to cap:              67.4x
```

H0 predicts margin commensurate with risk borne in a market. The observed structure shows margin commensurate with audit absence. That is the falsification, stated as synthesis from disclosed records — the bill of materials, the OTA terms, the sole-source justification, and the commercial-item determination — not as accusation. Where the comparables the designation asserts actually exist, H0 survives; the entire dispute reduces to producing them.

Framing matters for the same reason the numbers do. Nothing in this audit asserts that any specific contractor committed a statutory violation; the clawback figures are boundary computations — what the statute trebles if and when the comparables fail to appear. The H0 -> H1 turn is synthesis from disclosed records, the protected core of forensic analysis: the bill of materials says one thing, the price says another, and the exemption asserts a market that the sole-source routing structurally excludes. Whoever produces the documents resolves the equation. That is the entire dispute, and it is resolvable with paper.

## 7. The Clawback Arithmetic: TINA Defective Pricing and FCA Treble

The same price structure that manufactures the margin manufactures the liability, in the same arithmetic.

```
  Compliant ceiling price:   C_bom * (1 + pi_cap) = 18,000.0 * 1.15 = $20,700.00

  E6:  TINA defective-pricing overcharge per unit
       D_TINA = P_vc - C_bom * (1 + pi_cap)
              = 200,000.0 - 20,700.0
              = $179,300.00        (tina_defective_pricing_overcharge_per_unit_usd)

  E7:  False Claims Act treble disgorgement per unit (31 U.S.C. 3729)
       D_FCA  = 3 * D_TINA
              = 3 * 179,300.0
              = $537,900.00        (fca_treble_disgorgement_per_unit_usd)

  D_FCA / P_vc = 537,900.0 / 200,000.0 = 2.6895
```

```
  Clawback waterfall, per unit (USD):

  Unit price                          $200,000.00
  - Compliant ceiling (BOM + 15.0%)   - $20,700.00
  = Defective-pricing spread            $179,300.00    (E6, TINA)
  x 3  (31 U.S.C. 3729 treble)
  = Disgorgement exposure               $537,900.00    (E7, FCA)
  = 2.6895x the unit price itself
```

Read the last line twice: the treble exposure per unit is 2.6895 times the unit's entire price. Where a FAR Part 12 commercial-item certification is supported by no commercial market comparables, the certification itself is the contested factual predicate, and 31 U.S.C. § 3729 trebles the spread. The wrapper priced its margin at 91.0%; the statute prices the unwind at 268.95% of revenue per unit. And the discovery dynamics run one direction: any truth-defense of the commercial designation requires producing, in civil discovery, the exact comparables the designation asserts exist. The ledger is the defense, or the ledger is the evidence. There is no third door.

The governing system has two regimes, separated by a single price:

```
  Regime      P_vc (USD)    m_vc     CPE_vc (USD)    Lambda       D_FCA (USD)
  ---------   -----------   ------   -------------   ----------   -----------
  Compliant      20,700.0   0.1304      258,750.0    388.125x           0.0
  Defection     200,000.0   0.91      2,500,000.0    3,750.0x     537,900.0
```

Statutory correction — forcing the price back to the compliant ceiling of $18,000.00 plus 15.0% — compresses the inversion from 3,750.0x to 388.125x and zeroes the clawback. It does not fix the physics: at even a fully lawful price, the boutique architecture delivers effects at nearly four hundred times commodity cost under jamming. The statute corrects the margin. Only the architecture corrects the inversion.
