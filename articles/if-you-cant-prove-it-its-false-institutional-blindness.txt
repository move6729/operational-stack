### I. The 1-Bit Rounding Error: Why "If You Can't Prove It 100%, It's False" Breaks Probability Theory

Watch how a modern institutional review panel, compliance desk, or corporate governance committee reacts when confronted with an uncomfortable structural anomaly—a balance sheet whose cash flow contradicts its reported earnings, a discovery production with silent pagination jumps, or a multi-billion-dollar derivatives strategy whose stated volume exceeds the entire open interest of the underlying exchange.

That sentence sounds sober, cautious, and methodical. In reality, it is a catastrophic mathematical bug. It replaces continuous probability with a **1-bit binary step function** that systematically rounds high-probability warnings down to zero.

To see the bug on a pocket calculator, suppose an investigator is auditing a complex financial or legal system and uncovers $k = 5$ genuinely independent pieces of circumstantial evidence ($E_1, E_2, E_3, E_4, E_5$). None of these five clues is a Hollywood "smoking gun." Taken completely in isolation against an equal prior, each individual clue points toward the underlying anomaly with only a **75% probability**—meaning each clue has a 25% false-positive rate and a **per-signal likelihood ratio of $\Lambda_i = \frac{0.75}{0.25} = 3.0$**.

They inspect Clue 1 ($0.75 < 0.95$) and round it to `0` ("unproven, could be a coincidence"). They inspect Clue 2 ($0.75 < 0.95$) and round it to `0` ("inconclusive"). They repeat this truncation across all five clues:
   $$\text{Step}_{\tau=0.95}(0.75) + \text{Step}_{\tau=0.95}(0.75) + \text{Step}_{\tau=0.95}(0.75) + \text{Step}_{\tau=0.95}(0.75) + \text{Step}_{\tau=0.95}(0.75) = 0 + 0 + 0 + 0 + 0 = 0.0$$
