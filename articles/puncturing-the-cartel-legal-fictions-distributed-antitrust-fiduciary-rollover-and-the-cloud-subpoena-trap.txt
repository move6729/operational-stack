## 2. Fiction #1 — The "Passive Investor" Hallucination: How *Texas v. BlackRock* and the DOJ Just Stripped the 1914 Clayton Act § 7 Shield ($\text{MHHI} = 4,800\text{ pts}$ and Vanguard's February 2026 Capitulation)

For four decades, the foundational defensive mantra of the asset-management oligopoly has been a single four-word phrase lifted from the third paragraph of Section 7 of the Clayton Act of 1914 (`15 U.S.C. § 18`): *"solely for investment."*

Section 7 prohibits any corporation from acquiring the whole or any part of the stock of two or more competing corporations where the effect *"may be substantially to lessen competition, or to tend to create a monopoly."* To prevent the statute from outlawing ordinary retail or institutional savings, Congress included a narrow safe harbor:

$$\text{15 U.S.C. § 18 (Para. 3):} \quad \text{"This section shall not apply to persons purchasing such stock solely for investment and not using the same by voting or otherwise to bring about, or in attempting to bring about, the substantial lessening of competition."}$$

Notice the conjunctive statutory structure. The exemption has two mandatory prongs:
1. The stock must be purchased *"solely for investment"*; **and**
2. The holder must **not** use that stock *"by voting or otherwise to bring about, or in attempting to bring about, the substantial lessening of competition."*

How did a safe harbor written for genuinely silent minority bondholders and passive savers get stretched to cover three firms casting 22% of the votes at every competing coal producer, oil major, airline, bank, and pharmaceutical company in the United States? By pretending that "index investing" is synonymous with "passive ownership"—even when the index fund managers join international climate and governance syndicates, summon competitor CEOs to bilateral "stewardship engagements," threaten to vote out board directors who expand production capacity, and enforce industry-wide supply discipline.

### The Modified Herfindahl-Hirschman Index ($\text{MHHI}$) Proof of Horizontal Cartelization

In standard antitrust enforcement under the 2023 DOJ/FTC Merger Guidelines, a market's concentration is measured by the Herfindahl-Hirschman Index ($\text{HHI} = \sum_{j=1}^N s_j^2 \times 10,000$), where any market above **$1,800\text{ points}$** is deemed "highly concentrated" and any transaction that increases concentration by **$\Delta\text{HHI} \ge 100\text{ points}$** is presumed to illegally lessen competition.

Traditional $\text{HHI}$ assumes that competing firms have completely separate owners who want to steal market share from one another. When the exact same institutional syndicates own 22% of **every** competitor in an industry, however, aggressive price competition or output expansion by Firm A hurts the common owners' equity stakes in Firms B, C, D, and E. As formalized by Bresnahan & Salop (1986) and O'Brien & Salop (2000), the true competitive distortion is captured by the **Modified Herfindahl-Hirschman Index ($\text{MHHI}$)**:

$$\text{MHHI} = \underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^{N} s_j^2}_{\text{HHI}_{\text{Base}}} + \underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^{N} \sum_{k \neq j} s_j s_k \left( \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{M} \gamma_{ij} \beta_{ik}}{\sum_{i=1}^{M} \gamma_{ij} \beta_{ij}} \right)}_{\Delta\text{HHI}_{\text{CO}} \text{ (Common-Ownership Density)}}$$

Take a benchmark 5-firm domestic energy or industrial sector ($N = 5$ symmetric competitors, each holding $s_j = 20\%$ market share) where BlackRock, Vanguard, and State Street hold a combined horizontal ownership share of $\beta_{\text{Big3}} = 0.2200$ ($22.00\%$) across all five rivals, while the remaining $78.00\%$ is held by dispersed retail and non-overlapping holders producing a common-ownership control internalization weight of $\lambda_{\text{CO}} = 0.3500$:

* **Baseline Market Concentration ($\text{HHI}_{\text{Base}}$):**
  $$\text{HHI}_{\text{Base}} = 5 \times (20)^2 = 2,000.0\text{ points}$$
* **Common-Ownership Concentration Surge ($\Delta\text{HHI}_{\text{CO}}$):**
  $$\Delta\text{HHI}_{\text{CO}} = \lambda_{\text{CO}} \cdot \left(10,000 - \text{HHI}_{\text{Base}}\right) = 0.3500 \times (10,000 - 2,000.0) = 2,800.0\text{ points}$$
* **Total Effective Market Concentration ($\text{MHHI}_{\text{Total}}$):**
  $$\text{MHHI}_{\text{Total}} = 2,000.0 + 2,800.0 = 4,800.0\text{ points}$$

A $\Delta\text{HHI}_{\text{CO}}$ of **$2,800.0\text{ points}$** is **28.0 times ($28.0\times$)** the DOJ/FTC's $100.0\text{-point}$ presumption threshold for an illegal merger. Economically, common ownership at $\beta_{\text{Big3}} = 22.00\%$ transforms a five-competitor market ($\text{HHI} = 2,000$) into the functional equivalent of a near-duopoly ($\text{MHHI} = 4,800$) without a single formal merger ever being filed under the Hart-Scott-Rodino Act.
