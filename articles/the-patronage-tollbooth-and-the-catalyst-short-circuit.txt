## 1. The Queue Is a Product

Ask any planning department why your accessory dwelling unit permit has sat in "plan check" for fourteen months and you will hear about staffing shortages, legacy software, and unprecedented demand. None of it is the real explanation.

The real explanation is that the delay is inventory. A permit counter that processes applications in the statutory sixty days has nothing to sell. A permit counter that processes the same applications in twelve months has created a scarce resource—speed—and every scarce resource attracts a market. The `$500/hr` permit expediter, ideally a former deputy planning director, does not possess magic. He possesses a queue position. He sells you delivery at statutory speed from a queue moving at one-sixth of statutory speed, and the political machine that staffs his old desk collects the relationship equity.

This is the patronage tollbooth: administrative delay, deliberately maintained, monetized twice. The first monetization is the favor economy—the bypass sold to connected developers and institutional players. The second is quieter and shows up directly on the municipal balance sheet: non-refundable application fees retained on the files that die in queue. When an agency clears only `4.80%` of a cohort—the `95.20%` attrition rate measured across multi-stage compliance gauntlets—it keeps `100%` of the upfront tolls on the dead files while paying the cost of issuance on almost nothing.

A 1,000-file cohort at a `$15,000.00` utility/impact deposit, `$2,000.00` marginal issuance cost, `95.20%` attrition, and a `$4,000.00` per-file federal litigation reserve produces **`$14,864,000.00`** in net tollbooth revenue. No denial letters, no appealable record, no judicial review. Attrition is the highest-margin line item the agency has.
