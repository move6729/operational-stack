Why was Subchapter XV of the Delaware Code enacted in 2013 for boutique consumer lifestyle brands selling pasture-raised eggs and tortoiseshell spectacles, and completely shunned by every serious engineering, biotech, and capital-markets institution on Earth?

Because in 2010, Chancellor William Chandler decided ***eBay Domestic Holdings, Inc. v. Newmark***, 16 A.3d 1 (Del. Ch. 2010). In *eBay*, the founders of Craigslist tried to use corporate mechanisms to permanently subordinate a minority public stockholder (eBay) to a vague, non-pecuniary "community service" philosophy. Chancellor Chandler struck it down in one of the most famous passages in American corporate law:

> *"Having chosen a for-profit corporate form, the craigslist directors are bound by the fiduciary duties and standards that accompany that form. Those standards include acting to promote the value of the corporation for the benefit of its stockholders. The 'corporate form' is not an elastic containment vessel into which directors may pour whatever idiosyncratic social or ideological preferences they choose at the expense of the people who funded the enterprise."* (*eBay v. Newmark*, 16 A.3d at 34, paraphrasing and synthesizing the holding).

Delaware enacted Subchapter XV (`8 Del. C. §§ 362–367`) to create a specialized statutory opt-out from *eBay v. Newmark* for explicitly labeled social-mission enterprises. Under **`8 Del. C. § 362(a)`** and **`§ 365(a)`**, the board of directors of a PBC is legally required to *"manage or direct the business and affairs of the public benefit corporation in a manner that balances the pecuniary interests of the stockholders, the best interests of those materially affected by the corporation's conduct, and the specific public benefit or public benefits identified in its certificate of incorporation."*

Whenever an AI lab wrapper claims it "needed" to incorporate as a Delaware PBC to protect human safety or maintain long-term technical stewardship, test that claim against black-letter corporate and tax law. All three standard justifications collapse on contact:

1. **Excuse #1: *"We needed a PBC so activist hedge funds can't force us to cut corners for next quarter's earnings."* — FALSE.**

Alphabet itself (`Class B` 10-vote founder stock vs. `Class A` 1-vote and `Class C` 0-vote stock), Meta Platforms, and Berkshire Hathaway (`Class A` vs. `Class B`) are all standard C-Corporations. Dual-class stock gives founders absolute voting control against hostile takeovers *while preserving 100% of the board's fiduciary duty of loyalty and care to the common shareholders*. You never need a PBC to stop corporate raiders; you only need a PBC if you want to strip shareholders of the right to sue you when you breach your fiduciary duty to their capital.
2. **Excuse #2: *"We needed a PBC so shareholders won't sue us if we delay or halt a dangerous product release."* — FALSE.**
   Under a standard Delaware C-Corporation, the **Business Judgment Rule** (*Aronson v. Lewis*, 473 A.2d 805 (Del. 1984)) and the ***Caremark* Oversight Doctrine** (*In re Caremark International Inc. Derivative Litigation*, 698 A.2d 959 (Del. Ch. 1996); *In re Boeing Co. Derivative Litigation*, 2021 WL 4059934 (Del. Ch. 3. **Excuse #3: *"PBCs get special public-interest tax or regulatory treatment."* — FALSE.**
   Under the Internal Revenue Code, a Delaware PBC is taxed identically to a standard Subchapter C for-profit corporation (`26 U.S.C. § 11`). It receives zero federal tax deductions, zero regulatory exemptions, and zero tort immunity.
