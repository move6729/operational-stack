## I. THE S-1 THAT READS AS A CREDITOR'S CONFESSION

On October 26, 2026, Anthropic, PBC filed a Form S-1 registration statement with the Securities and Exchange Commission (EDGAR CIK 0002130357). A registration statement is supposed to be a selling document — the polished front of the house shown to prospective public investors. Anthropic's S-1 is something rarer and more useful: a confession, rendered under signature, of how an insolvent cloud-rentier wrapper used one hyperscaler's TPUs, balance sheet, and marketplace distribution to subordinate that same hyperscaler's recovery behind a $732.0–$757.0 billion compute-and-lease encumbrance pile — and then committed to wire $3.1 billion a month to Elon Musk's SpaceX.AI LLC, the direct rival of Google's Gemini franchise.

The balance sheet alone should end the conversation. For the six months ended June 30, 2026, Anthropic reported a fair value loss of -$72,388 million and a net loss of -$80,748 million, against just $16,286 million of H1 2026 revenue — a loss roughly five times its half-year top line. Stockholders' deficit stood at -$127,619 million; accumulated deficit at -$132,902 million (Consolidated Balance Sheets, pp. F-3–F-4). Against that negative-equity base, the S-1's own commitment disclosures (Note 14, p. F-59; MD&A Contractual Obligations, p. 171) stack up to $732.0–$757.0 billion of undiscounted compute and lease encumbrance — 16.6x to 17.2x the annualized nine-month 2026 implied revenue of $33,086 million.

This article proceeds in two ledgers, because Anthropic kept two. Ledger One is what Anthropic induced Google to commit between March and August 2026: $114.5 billion of take-or-pay cloud spend through July 2033 plus $125.0–$150.0 billion of additional custom TPU compute for 2028–2031 — a total Google exposure of $239.5–$264.5 billion. ## II. LEDGER ONE: WHAT GOOGLE WAS INDUCED TO SIGN ($239.5–$264.5 BILLION)

Start with the structure of the Google commitment, because the maturity schedule is the subordination trap's skeleton.

First, the take-or-pay: $114.5 billion between April 2026 and July 2033, "regardless of actual usage," with a cash true-up payable to Google if actual spend falls short. This is not a cloud contract; it is a seven-year, seven-figure-per-day credit facility extended by Alphabet's balance sheet to a counterparty that, as of the S-1's own interim balance sheet, has negative stockholders' equity of $127.6 billion. A take-or-pay true-up is only as good as the counterparty's ability to pay it, and the S-1 concedes that ability does not exist without continuous external financing.

Second, the back-ended TPU tranche: an additional $125.0–$150.0 billion of custom TPU compute to be delivered 2028–2031 — which the S-1 itself flags as "not included in the contractual commitments table above." That exclusion is doing enormous work. It means the headline commitments table that analysts and index committees will quote systematically understates Anthropic's obligations by up to $150 billion, and it means Google's largest recovery tranche sits entirely in the future — 2028 through 2031 — behind every claim Anthropic incurs between now and then.

Combined, Google's exposure is $239.5 billion (low case) to $264.5 billion (high case). No single customer in the history of cloud computing has ever extracted a commitment of this scale from one hyperscaler. For Alphabet shareholders, the question is not whether this is a great commercial win. The question is: what did Anthropic represent about its own capital structure, its compute economics, and its counterparty pipeline while it was negotiating the May–August 2026 amendments that expanded this exposure — and what was it simultaneously signing with Elon Musk?

## III. LEDGER TWO: THE $281.7 BILLION FRONT-LOAD WIRED BEHIND GOOGLE'S BACK

The S-1's own timeline answers that question with brutal precision. "Between May and October 2026" — the same window in which Anthropic was amending the Google agreement upward (May–August 2026) — Anthropic "entered into and amended several agreements with SpaceX.AI LLC (formerly known as X.AI LLC)... a wholly owned subsidiary of Space Exploration Technologies Corp." Do the arithmetic. $3.1 billion per month is $37.2 billion per year. Anthropic's nine-month 2026 implied revenue is $33,086 million — an annualized run-rate of roughly $44.1 billion. The SpaceX.AI rent alone therefore consumes approximately 84% of Anthropic's annualized revenue before a single dollar of Broadcom rent, AWS, Azure, electricity, payroll, or debt service is paid. No compute business on Earth carries that structure and survives on operating cash flow. The S-1 knows this; that is why the company is registering shares.

Then the Broadcom layer: five-year master equipment leases with rent payments of $161.2 billion excluding interest. Add the SpaceX.AI commitments and the Broadcom leases and you get a $281.7 billion front-loaded drain — a number that, by itself, already exceeds the entire low-case Google exposure of $239.5 billion.

Now name the beneficiary that matters. SpaceX.AI is the rebranded X.AI — Elon Musk's frontier-model vehicle, the direct commercial rival of Google DeepMind's Gemini. Read plainly, the S-1 discloses that Anthropic took Google's TPUs, Google's take-or-pay guarantee, and Google's marketplace distribution, and used the balance-sheet credibility and compute headroom those instruments created to commit $3.1 billion a month — $37.2 billion a year — to Gemini's rival.
