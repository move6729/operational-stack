## II. Spreadsheet Isomorphism & Mega-Check Adverse Selection: Why Hyperscalers Dump Terminal Risk Onto Retail LPs while GPs Swap Row Labels from '2021 SaaS ARR' to '2025 AI Leases'

How did the exact same credit and real-asset investment committees look at 2021 enterprise SaaS buyouts and 2025 hyperscale AI data center shells and conclude that both were "fortress, downside-protected fixed-income replacements"?

The answer is **Spreadsheet Isomorphism**. To an origination analyst working inside an Excel model in Midtown Manhattan, a 2021 leveraged buyout of a legacy workflow SaaS company and a 2025 triple-net (NNN) construction lease for an Abilene AI cluster do not look like different technologies. In the spreadsheet, **they are the exact same cash-flow vector with a changed row label**:

$$\mathbf{CF}_{2021}^{\text{SaaS}}(\text{"Contracted ARR"}, \text{NDR}=110\%, \text{LTV}=40\%) \;\cong\; \mathbf{CF}_{2025}^{\text{Infra}}(\text{"15-Yr NNN Lease"}, \text{Esc}=2.75\%, \text{Yield}=8.3\%)$$

In 2021, private credit underwriters convinced themselves that enterprise software wasn't really a cyclical technology business—it was "digital utility infrastructure." They replaced traditional EBITDA coverage covenants with **Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR)** recurring-revenue loans at 2.5x to 3.5x ARR (often 15x to 25x actual cash EBITDA), pointing to historical 110% Net Dollar Retention (NDR) and a "40% loan-to-value (LTV) equity cushion" based on peak 2021 Vista or Thoma Bravo buyout multiples. 

By late 2024, when AI coding assistants and agentic workflows began eroding per-seat SaaS moats, the origination desks didn't rewrite their risk models. They simply migrated to the Real Assets tab of the spreadsheet, erased the label `"Contracted Multi-Year SaaS ARR"`, and typed `"15-Year Triple-Net (NNN) Hyperscale Data Center Lease"` into the exact same discounted cash-flow rows.

Look at the structural blind spot shared by both models: **both underwrite a 15-to-30-year technological life using a backwards-looking contractual wrapper while ignoring the physical obsolescence clock of the underlying asset.**
