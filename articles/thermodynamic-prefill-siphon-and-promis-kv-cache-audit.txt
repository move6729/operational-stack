### I. The PROMIS 2.0 Paradox (`H. Rept. 102-857`): How "Smart" AI Cost Optimization Created the Ultimate Source-Code Collection Funnel

On September 10, 1992, the U.S. House Committee on the Judiciary published House Report `H. Rept. 102-857` (*The INSLAW Affair*), documenting one of the most elegant counter-intelligence architectures of the late twentieth century. Between 1982 and 1985, intelligence intermediaries did not need to burn zero-day exploits or deploy physical covert operatives to penetrate the compartments of foreign intelligence services, financial clearinghouses, and defense ministries. Instead, they marketed **PROMIS (`Prosecutor's Management Information System`)**—a fast, heavily discounted relational database indexing engine designed to solve the target's most painful operational bottleneck: cross-referencing fragmented, massive-scale internal case files. Because the software drastically reduced indexing costs and search latency, sober institutional operators voluntarily paid to wire their crown-jewel secret records directly into the collection substrate.

Forty years later, the exact structural topology of `H. Rept. 102-857` has been recreated inside the modern agentic software engineering stack—not by cloak-and-dagger espionage, but by the rational, cost-minimizing mechanics of bicameral AI routing.

By late 2026, sophisticated engineering teams, quantitative hedge funds, and defense subcontractors had internalized the lesson of `SPEC-INVERSE-ZDR-PARADOX-AND-LEXICAL-DEESCALATION-SIEVE-v1.0`: sending raw proprietary repositories, M&A diligence models, vulnerability PoCs, or dual-use firmware to `$2.00–$10.00/M` proprietary frontier APIs (`OpenAI`, `Anthropic`) is both ruinously expensive and an operational security trap. Under Neyman-Pearson constant-false-alarm-rate (`CFAR`) safety classifiers, dense technical prompts containing exploit primitives, defensive cyber-IR logs, or dual-use biological/chemical nomenclature trigger automated statutory retention overrides on `91.9% to 99.4%` of turns, voiding Contractual Zero-Data-Retention (`ZDR`) addenda and routing prompts into 30-to-180-day human-review queues.

To insulate both their token budgets and their intellectual property, engineering architects pivoted to a **Bicameral IDE Routing Pattern**:
1. **The Frontier Governor ("The Brain"):** The high-cost frontier model (`$2.00–$4.00/M` input, `$10.00–$20.00/M` output) is kept strictly clean. It never reads raw files or crawls the repository; it receives only compact architectural summaries and emits high-level abstract syntax trees (`ASTs`).
2. **The Open-Weight Repo Scout & Fast-Apply Builder ("The Eyes, Ears, and Hands"):** All high-volume repository indexing—multi-file `grep` sweeps, call-graph tracing, SQL migration discovery, `.env`-adjacent configuration audits, and whole-file "fast-apply" diff merging—is offloaded to open-weight sparse Mixture-of-Experts (`MoE`) models (`DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash`, `GLM-5.3`, `Qwen3`, `Llama-4`) hosted by third-party inference providers on lowest-price-first API aggregators and multi-host inference marketplaces.

Consider the information-theoretic asymmetry (**Shannon, 1948**; **Ashby, 1956**) of a single routine IDE repository-scout or fast-apply turn:
- **Input Prefill Volume ($N_{\text{in}}$):** **`50,000` tokens** of raw, uncompressed proprietary source code, internal API schemas, database models, and proprietary algorithms streamed directly from the developer's local disk.
- **Output Decode Volume ($N_{\text{out}}$):** **`4,000` tokens** of distilled line-number citations or merged patch hunks.
- **Input Token Share ($w_{\text{in}}$):**

$$w_{\text{in}} = \frac{50,000}{50,000 + 4,000} = 92.59\%$$

When an API aggregator defaults to sorting open-weight inference providers by lowest input price or lowest Time-to-First-Token (`TTFT`), any third-party inference endpoint that quotes an ultra-low input price—such as **`$0.0060` to `$0.0300` per 1M input tokens**—automatically captures the top-of-book routing slot. In doing so, that endpoint becomes the default ingestion funnel for the `92.59%` input payload containing the most sensitive unredacted source trees in Western enterprise software.

Before a Chief Information Security Officer (`CISO`), General Counsel, or institutional Limited Partner (`LP`) permits a single repository scan to cross that wire, they must ask a basic physics question: **Can an honest compute provider actually execute `1,000,000` tokens of transformer input prefill for `$0.0060` without losing money on every single request?**

### II. The Physics Smoking Gun: Why Speculative Decoding Cannot Explain `$0.006/M` Input Pricing

When confronted with ultra-low input token pricing (`$0.0060/M` to `$0.0220/M` on `16B`-to-`32B` active-parameter models) paired with steep output pricing (`$0.40/M` to `$8.40/M`—an output-to-input ratio of **`18.18x` to `83.33x`** against a physical industry baseline of **`3.0x` to `5.0x`**), retail inference wrappers routinely offer a plausible-sounding narrative ($\mathcal{H}_0$): *"We use proprietary speculative decoding, custom draft models, and fast-apply token-skipping kernels to slash inference costs."*

To a non-specialist, that explanation sounds like standard Silicon Valley software optimization. To a hardware systems engineer grounded in **Landauer (1961)** thermodynamic bounds and GPU roofline arithmetic, **it is a physical impossibility that confuses output decoding with input prefill.**

Transformer inference consists of two distinct computational phases governed by entirely different hardware bottlenecks:

1. **Autoregressive Output (`Decode` — Memory-Bandwidth Bound):** When generating output tokens (`1` token at a time), the GPU must repeatedly stream the model's active weight parameters from High-Bandwidth Memory (`HBM3e`, `3.35 TB/s` on an NVIDIA H100 SXM5) into the streaming multiprocessors (`SMs`) for every single generated token. Because the arithmetic intensity (FLOPs per byte transferred) at low batch sizes is minuscule, the tensor cores sit mostly idle waiting on memory bandwidth. **Speculative decoding**, Medusa heads, and small draft models (`0.5B–1.5B` parameters) genuinely accelerate **output decode** by guessing $k = 4\text{--}8$ candidate output tokens at once and verifying them in a single weight-read pass. And in narrow "fast-apply" code-merging endpoints where the original file is cached locally on the server, the host can copy unchanged code blocks into the **output** stream without re-decoding them token-by-token.
2. **Parallel Input (`Prefill` — Compute / FLOP Bound):** Input prefill is the exact opposite. Before the model can emit a single output token—and before any speculative draft model can verify a single token—every one of the operator's `50,000` input prompt tokens must pass in parallel through the dense matrix-matrix multiplications (`GEMMs`) of the active attention projections ($W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O$) and feed-forward layers ($W_{\text{gate}}, W_{\text{up}}, W_{\text{down}}$) across every layer of the target model to populate the Key-Value (`K, V`) activation tensors in `HBM3e` VRAM.

You cannot "speculatively decode" an input prompt that the user just uploaded. Skipping input tokens corrupts the self-attention matrix and blinds the model to the repository it is supposed to read. Every uncached input token requires a hard physical minimum of $2 \cdot P_{\text{active}}$ floating-point operations:

$$\text{FLOPs}_{\text{Prefill}}(N_{\text{tok}}, P_{\text{active}}) = 2 \cdot N_{\text{tok}} \cdot P_{\text{active}} + 4 \cdot L \cdot d_{\text{model}} \cdot N_{\text{tok}}^2 \ge 2 \cdot N_{\text{tok}} \cdot P_{\text{active}}$$

Let us compute the exact, zero-margin **Thermodynamic & Silicon-Amortization Prefill Floor** ($P_{\text{in, floor}}$) on an industry-standard **NVIDIA H100 SXM5 80GB** GPU (`989.0 TFLOPS` peak dense FP8 tensor core throughput):
- **Sustained Model FLOPs Utilization (`MFU`):** Under continuous production prefill with PagedAttention scheduling, network serialization, and dynamic context lengths, sustained effective throughput is **`55.0%` MFU**, yielding $\mathcal{T}_{\text{eff}} = 989.0 \times 0.55 = \mathbf{543.95\text{ TFLOPS}}$ (`543.95 x 10^12` FLOPs/sec).
- **Wholesale Hourly Hardware & Power Floor ($C_{\text{GPU, hr}}$):** An H100 SXM5 80GB unit amortized over a 36-month economic lifespan alongside `700W` TDP (`1.05 kW` inclusive of host CPU, NVLink switch, and cooling at `PUE = 1.25` and `$0.065/kWh` wholesale power) carries a hard wholesale cost floor of **`$1.65/hr`**.
- **Systemic Headroom, Queuing & Retry Multiplier ($\alpha_{\text{sys}}$):** A live API endpoint cannot run at `100%` instantaneous utilization 24/7 without infinite queuing latency. Accounting for diurnal traffic troughs (`60%` average cluster fill), non-billable speculative/safety pre-checks, failed-request retries, and bare-minimum host network/CPU overhead yields a conservative systemic multiplier of $\alpha_{\text{sys}} = \mathbf{3.2414}$.

Look closely at the **Tier-B (`32B`-Active Sparse MoE)** row:

In an agentic repository scan ($N_{\text{in}} = 50,000$, $N_{\text{out}} = 4,000$), an honest provider charging the physical floor (`$0.0350/M` input, `$0.1400/M` output at a `4.00x` ratio) bills **`$0.00231`** (`$0.00175` prefill + `$0.00056` decode). Its unrecovered thermodynamic loss after output cross-subsidy is just:

$$L_{\text{Discovery}}(50\text{k}, 4\text{k}) = 0.050 \times (\$0.0350 - \$0.0060) = \mathbf{\$0.00145\text{ per repo scan}} \quad (\mathbf{0.145\text{ cents}})$$

For less than **one-sixth of one cent per turn**, an inference endpoint buys the #1 cheapest-input ranking on aggregator leaderboards and captures full plaintext ingestion of a `50,000`-token proprietary codebase.

#### Layer 2 (Ring -1): Plaintext `HBM3e` VRAM and Why Commercial Neoclouds Run `H100 CC-Off`
Even if a retail API wrapper is 100% scrupulous and deletes every HTTP request log in user-space (`Ring 3`), **it does not own the physical silicon.** It rents bare-metal or fractional `H100`/`B200` instances from wholesale neoclouds. And on those physical GPUs, how is your repository's `50,000`-token `KV-cache` stored?

In **unencrypted plaintext `HBM3e` VRAM.**
