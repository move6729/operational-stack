## 1. The Frontier Lobbying Fantasy Meets Statutory Reality

Every time a Chinese or open-source laboratory drops a frontier-grade sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) or reasoning-distilled model under an MIT or Apache 2.0 license, the same reflex echoes through Washington lobbying corridors: *if foreign labs used capability distillation from US frontier APIs to train low-cost open weights, why doesn't the US government simply ban those models inside the United States?*

The answer is twofold. First, outlawing the domestic download, possession, or local execution of published open-weight models is **flatly illegal under current United States statutory and constitutional law**. Second, even if Congress tore up the US Code to make it legal, banning open-weight models domestically would function as a **58.31x to 100.00x asymmetric regulatory self-sanction** across **every business and every physical or digital product in the United States**. Outside the United States, any manufacturer, hospital, logistics fleet, automaker, or software firm can run open-weight intelligence for the bare physical cost of **electricity plus hardware depreciation** ($\$0.4875$ to $\$0.8361$ per million tokens, with $\$0.00$ license rent). Inside the United States under a domestic ban, every company in every industry would be forced by law to pay a **$98.28\%$ to $99.00\%$ monopoly rent surcharge** ($\$48.75$ per million tokens) to three closed API vendors—bankrupting American unit margins across all goods and services, locking US academic labs out of white-box gradient research, and triggering an immediate **human-capital brain drain** of engineering talent to foreign jurisdictions.

To understand why Washington is boxed in, we have to examine the black-letter statutory firewall, the unit-margin math of downstream token consumption, and the offshoring threshold for AI engineering talent.

## 2. Why an Executive Ban Is Dead on Arrival: IEEPA, the EAR, and the First Amendment

When the White House or the Commerce Department restricts foreign technology—whether Huawei telecommunications hardware or ByteDance's hosted TikTok infrastructure—it relies on two primary statutory engines: the **International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA, `50 U.S.C. § 1701 et seq.`)** and the **Export Administration Regulations (EAR, `15 C.F.R. Parts 730–774`)**.

Neither statute can touch a published open-weight neural network downloaded and executed on domestic silicon.

### 2.1 The IEEPA Berman Amendment (`50 U.S.C. § 1702(b)(3)`)

In 1988—and expanded explicitly for digital media in the Free Trade in Ideas Act of 1994—Congress enacted the Berman Amendment to IEEPA, codified at `50 U.S.C. § 1702(b)(3)`. The statute strips the President of emergency authority over informational materials in unambiguous terms:

> "The authority granted to the President by this section does not include the authority to regulate or prohibit, directly or indirectly— ... (3) the importation from any country, or the exportation to any country, whether commercial or otherwise, regardless of format or medium of transmission, of any information or informational materials, including but not limited to, publications, films, posters, phonograph records, photographs, microfilms, microfiche, tapes, compact disks, CD ROMs, artworks, and news wire feeds." — `50 U.S.C. § 1702(b)(3)`

Notice the statutory lock: **"regardless of format or medium of transmission."** A `safetensors` or `GGUF` file containing 671 billion floating-point parameters published on a public repository is pure mathematical information. Moreover, while the second sentence of `50 U.S.C. § 1702(b)(3)` historically carved out *exports* controlled under sections `4604` and `4605` of Title 50, that exception applies only to *exports* (never to *importation* or domestic use), and sections `4604` and `4605` were formally repealed by the Export Control Reform Act of 2018 (`Pub. L. 115-232, div. A, title XVII, § 1766(a)`). Any Executive Order invoking IEEPA to ban American companies from downloading or running open-weight models is *ultra vires* on arrival.

### 2.2 The EAR Published Software Exemption (`15 C.F.R. §§ 734.3(b)(3), 734.7`)

What about the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS)? Under `15 C.F.R. § 734.3(b)(3)`, published software and technology are statutorily excluded from the scope of the Export Administration Regulations:

> "(b) The following are not subject to the EAR: ... (3) Information and 'software' that: (i) Are published, as described in § 734.7; (ii) Arise during, or result from, fundamental research, as described in § 734.8..." — `15 C.F.R. § 734.3(b)(3)`

And `15 C.F.R. § 734.7(a)(4)` defines "published" explicitly to cover unrestricted internet uploads:

> "...unclassified 'technology' or 'software' is 'published,' and is thus not 'technology' or 'software' subject to the EAR, when it has been made available to the public without restrictions upon its further dissemination such as through any of the following: ... (4) Public dissemination (i.e., unlimited distribution) in any form (e.g., not necessarily in published form), including posting on the Internet on sites available to the public..." — `15 C.F.R. § 734.7(a)(4)`

Aside from narrow ECCN `5D002` encryption object code (`15 C.F.R. § 734.7(b)`), once weights are posted to the open internet under a permissive license, BIS jurisdiction evaporates.

### 2.3 First Amendment Scientific Expression (*Bernstein v. U.S. Dep't of Justice*)

Even if regulators attempted to rewrite the EAR to classify published floating-point weights as restricted items, they hit the First Amendment wall established in *Bernstein v. U.S. Dep't of Justice*, 176 F.3d 1132 (9th Cir. 1999) (affirming 974 F. Supp. 1288 (N.D. Cal. 1997); reh'g en banc granted and panel opinion withdrawn after BIS relaxed encryption rules, 192 F.3d 1308 (9th Cir. 1999)):

> "We find that the EAR regulations (1) operate as a prepublication licensing scheme that burdens scientific expression, (2) vest boundless discretion in government officials, and (3) lack adequate procedural safeguards. Consequently, we hold that the challenged regulations constitute a prior restraint on speech that offends the First Amendment." — *Bernstein*, 176 F.3d at 1134–35

Model weights are the serialized coefficients of a mathematical equation. Outlawing the domestic possession or local execution of published mathematical matrices is a prior restraint on scientific and algorithmic speech.

## 3. The Myth of "Distillation Taint": Why API Terms of Service Don't Follow the Weights

Recognizing that trade sanctions cannot touch open weights, incumbent frontier labs have floated a second theory: *what if a foreign model was trained via knowledge distillation on synthetic reasoning traces extracted from a US frontier API?* In that case, OpenAI's Terms of Use explicitly state that end-users may not *"Use Output to develop models that compete with OpenAI"* or *"Automatically or programmatically extract data or Output."* Does that contractual breach "taint" the resulting open-weight model so that US businesses cannot legally run it?

Under federal copyright law, the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA), and basic contract privity, the answer is **no**:

1. **No Copyright in Mathematical Procedures (`17 U.S.C. §§ 102(b), 103(b)`):** Raw AI outputs lack human authorship and are not copyrightable in the first place. Even if a lab claimed a thin compilation copyright over curated outputs (`17 U.S.C. § 103(b)`), knowledge distillation does not copy verbatim prose into the student model. It updates an architecturally distinct matrix $\mathbf{W}_S$ via gradient descent over probability distributions $\mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}(\mathbf{W}_S) = \mathbb{E}_{x}[D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\mathbf{W}_T} \| P_{\mathbf{W}_S})]$. Section `102(b)` of the Copyright Act is categorical:
   > "In no case does copyright protection for an original work of authorship extend to any idea, procedure, process, system, method of operation, concept, principle, or discovery, regardless of the form in which it is described, explained, illustrated, or embodied in such work." — `17 U.S.C. § 102(b)`
   A trained weight matrix is a mathematical system and method of operation, not a derivative literary work.
2. **No CFAA Liability Under *Van Buren* (`18 U.S.C. § 1030(e)(6)`):** In *Van Buren v. United States*, 593 U.S. 374, 390 (2021), the Supreme Court held that the CFAA's prohibition on "exceed[ing] authorized access" (`18 U.S.C. § 1030(e)(6)`) is a strictly technical "gates-up-or-down inquiry":
   > "The term 'exceeds authorized access' is defined to mean 'to access a computer with authorization and to use such access to obtain or alter information in the computer that the accesser is not entitled so to obtain or alter.' §1030(e)(6)." — *Van Buren*, 593 U.S. at 381 (2021)
   A user with a valid, paid API key who violates a contractual *purpose* clause ("do not train a competing model") commits at most a private breach of contract—never a federal computer intrusion crime under the CFAA.
3. **Contract Covenants Do Not Run With Floating-Point Tensors:** Because a Terms of Service breach is a purely bilateral (`in personam`) contract claim between the API vendor and the scraper, it creates zero `in rem` legal taint on the published weights. An American startup, hospital, or manufacturer that downloads an MIT-licensed model from Hugging Face is not in contractual privity with OpenAI and bears zero derivative liability.

## 4. The Macroeconomic Suicide Pact: A 58.31x to 100.00x Compute Tax on US Industry

Even if we perform a thought experiment where Congress repeals the Berman Amendment and overrides the First Amendment, look at what a domestic open-weight ban actually does to the US economy.

### 4.1 The Capex Compression Asymmetry ($\Gamma_{\text{distill}}$)

In the `DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report` (`arXiv:2412.19437v2, Table 1`), the authors documented the exact compute expenditure required to train a 671B-parameter sparse-MoE frontier model:

> "Training Costs Pre-Training Context Extension Post-Training Total in H800 GPU Hours 2664K 119K 5K 2788K in USD $5.328M $0.238M $0.01M $5.576M Table 1: Training costs of DeepSeek-V3, assuming the rental price of H800 is $2 per GPU hour." — `DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report, arXiv:2412.19437v2, Table 1`

Against a $\$500\text{M}$ to $\$1,000\text{M}$ closed US frontier cluster run, the **Capex Compression Ratio** ($\Gamma_{\text{distill}}$) is:

$$\Gamma_{\text{distill}} = \frac{C_{\text{teacher}}}{C_{\text{student}}} \in \left[\frac{\$500\text{M}}{\$5.576\text{M}}, \; \frac{\$1,000\text{M}}{\$5.576\text{M}}\right] = [89.67\times, \; 179.34\times]$$

Once someone spends $\$5.576\text{M}$ and gives the resulting weights away under an MIT license, that training expenditure becomes a permanent deflationary gift to global industry.

### 4.2 The Blended Inference Price Gap ($\Phi_{\text{API}}$)

Using the canonical blended token price equation from `SPEC-NEOCLOUD-GPU-DEBT-AND-INFERENCE-DEFLATION-v1.0`:

$$\bar{P}(\rho) = \frac{p_{\text{in}} + \rho \, p_{\text{out}}}{1 + \rho}$$

at an agentic reasoning output-to-input ratio of $\rho = 3.0$:

- **Closed US Frontier Reasoning API (`openai/o1` at $\$15.00/\text{M}$ in, $\$60.00/\text{M}$ out):**
  $$\bar{P}_{\text{closed}}(3) = \frac{15.00 + 3(60.00)}{4} = \$48.7500/\text{M tokens}$$
- **Open-Weight Sparse-MoE Standard Market API (`DeepSeek-V3` at $\$0.2574/\text{M}$ in, $\$1.0290/\text{M}$ out):**
  $$\bar{P}_{\text{open,V3}}(3) = \frac{0.2574 + 3(1.0290)}{4} = \$0.8361/\text{M tokens}$$
- **Open-Weight Off-Peak / Local Self-Hosted Equivalent ($\$0.1500/\text{M}$ in, $\$0.6000/\text{M}$ out):**
  $$\bar{P}_{\text{open,off}}(3) = \frac{0.1500 + 3(0.6000)}{4} = \$0.4875/\text{M tokens}$$

That is an **Inference Cost Asymmetry Factor** ($\Phi_{\text{API}}$) of **$58.31\times$** at standard market rates and **$100.00\times$** at off-peak/self-hosted rates.

Look at what that price gap actually means in physical terms:

$$\bar{P}_{\text{ex-USA}}(\rho) = C_{\text{power}} + C_{\text{silicon}} + \underbrace{C_{\text{license}}}_{\$0.00} \quad \text{vs.} \quad \bar{P}_{\text{USA,ban}}(\rho) = C_{\text{power}} + C_{\text{silicon}} + R_{\text{cartel}}$$

Outside the United States, once open weights are released under an MIT license, machine intelligence is priced like a commodity utility: **you pay for electricity plus amortized hardware, and zero software rent**. Inside the United States under a domestic open-weight ban, the **Cartel Rent Share** ($\theta_{\text{rent}} = 1 - 1/\Phi_{\text{API}}$) is **$98.28\%$ to $99.00\%$** of the total bill. You are paying 1% for actual physics (power + silicon) and 99% in compulsory state-enforced rent to a San Francisco API oligopoly.

### 4.3 Not Just "AI Labs": Why a Rent Mandate Bankrupts Every US Business and Product

A common delusion in policy circles is treating open-weight models as an issue that only affects "AI companies" or Silicon Valley software startups. In reality, machine models are now a **universal intermediate input across 100% of the economy**—exactly like electricity, steel, or microprocessors. Every company is integrating machine models into its internal operations and into the physical and digital products it sells:

- **Physical Products & Embedded Edge Hardware:** Automakers, medical device manufacturers, defense contractors, agricultural equipment builders, robotics factories, and consumer electronics brands embed local open-weight models directly onto on-device silicon (NPUs/GPUs) inside vehicles, surgical tools, CNC machines, drones, and appliances. Those embedded models run offline at the edge for **$\$0.00$ in recurring API rent**. If Washington banned domestic open weights, an American tractor, car, factory robot, or medical scanner could not legally ship with an embedded open-weight brain—it would either fail to function offline or require a permanent, metered $\$48.75/\text{M}$ cloud rent subscription, while German, Japanese, Korean, and Chinese manufacturers shipped superior untethered products running free local weights at the cost of battery power + silicon.
- **Every Main Street & Fortune 500 Business:** Hospitals adjudicating clinical charts, regional banks underwriting credit, logistics companies routing freight, law and accounting firms auditing contracts, and manufacturers running automated quality control all consume massive token volumes per unit of output.

Take a concrete unit workflow across any of those industries—generating $R_{\text{unit}} = \$12.00$ in unit revenue, incurring $C_{\text{ops}} = \$4.00$ in non-compute operating costs ($V_{\text{net}} = \$8.00$), and consuming $Q_{\text{tokens}} = 0.50\text{M}$ tokens at $\rho = 3.0$:

1. **Foreign Competitor (Germany, Japan, Singapore, China) Running Open Weights ($\bar{P}_{\text{open,V3}} = \$0.8361/\text{M}$):**
   $$\Pi_{\text{foreign}} = \$8.00 - 0.50 \times \$0.8361 = +\$7.58195/\text{unit} \approx +\$7.58/\text{unit} \quad (+63.18\%\text{ operating margin})$$
2. **US Enterprise Forced onto Closed Frontier APIs by a Domestic Ban ($\bar{P}_{\text{closed}} = \$48.75/\text{M}$):**
   $$\Pi_{\text{US}} = \$8.00 - 0.50 \times \$48.75 = -\$16.3750/\text{unit} \approx -\$16.38/\text{unit} \quad (-136.46\%\text{ operating margin})$$

For any workflow consuming between $Q_{\text{crit,US}} = 0.1641\text{M}$ tokens and $Q_{\text{crit,foreign}} = 9.5682\text{M}$ tokens per unit, a domestic open-weight ban literally makes the business model illegal to operate profitably inside the United States while handing foreign competitors a $63.18\%$ margin monopoly ($\tau_{\text{reg}} = \$23.95695/\text{unit}$). Across the macroeconomy, when $\eta_{\text{destroy}} = 75\%$ of high-volume token workflows are priced out of domestic deployment and frontier labs retain $m_{\text{net}} = 30\%$ net rent after GPU depreciation, the **Regulatory Capture Destruction Multiplier** ($\Omega_{\text{capture}}$) reaches **$13.3333\times$**: Washington would destroy $\$13.33$ of downstream US economic surplus for every $\$1.00$ of API rent it protected for three San Francisco labs.

### 4.4 The Brain-Drain Ratchet: Exporting US AI Startups and PhD Labs Offshore

There is a second, even more corrosive self-inflicted wound hidden inside a domestic open-weight ban: **human-capital flight**.

A closed black-box API (`openai/o1`) returns text strings over HTTP, but it hides the underlying weight tensors $\mathbf{W}$, internal residual-stream activations $\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)}$, and parameter gradients $\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\mathcal{L}$. You cannot perform mechanistic interpretability, post-training reinforcement learning (`GRPO` / `RLVR`), parameter-efficient fine-tuning (`LoRA`), speculative decoding alignment, or sub-byte quantization (`FP8` / `INT4` kernel engineering) through a closed text endpoint. Outlawing domestic possession or modification of frontier open weights would instantly make it impossible to conduct frontier AI systems research at MIT, Stanford, Berkeley, or CMU, while researchers in Zurich, London, Paris, Toronto, Singapore, and Hangzhou remained free to inspect and train directly on the weights.

Worse, look at the startup formation math:

- **58.31x Experimentation Compression:** On a fixed seed-stage or university lab compute budget of $B_{\text{compute}} = \$250,000/\text{yr}$ at $\rho = 3.0$, an unconstrained team running open weights executes **$299.01\text{B tokens/yr}$** ($512.82\text{B tokens/yr}$ at off-peak/self-hosted rates) with full white-box gradient access. A domestic team forced onto closed frontier APIs gets just **$5.13\text{B tokens/yr}$** and zero gradient access.
- **The Critical Brain-Drain Threshold ($Q_{\text{drain}}^*$):** Suppose a US founder values the venture-hub network advantage of San Francisco or Boston over Toronto, London, Zurich, or Singapore at $\Delta V_{\text{hub}} = \$500,000/\text{yr}$, faces an annualized corporate re-domiciliation and relocation cost of $r \, C_{\text{reloc}} = \$100,000/\text{yr}$, and avoids $C_{\text{compliance}} = \$120,000/\text{yr}$ in domestic open-weight compliance audits by moving offshore (a net hub hurdle of $\$480,000/\text{yr}$). Against the blended price spread of $\bar{P}_{\text{closed}}(3) - \bar{P}_{\text{open,V3}}(3) = \$47.9139/\text{M tokens}$, the **Critical Brain-Drain Token Threshold** is:
  $$Q_{\text{drain}}^* = \frac{\Delta V_{\text{hub}} + r \, C_{\text{reloc}} - C_{\text{compliance}}}{\bar{P}_{\text{closed}}(3) - \bar{P}_{\text{open,V3}}(3)} = \frac{\$480,000}{\$47.9139/\text{M}} = 10,017.97\text{M tokens/yr} \approx 10.0180\text{B tokens/yr} \quad (\approx 27.45\text{M tokens/day})$$
- **The Seed-Stage Relocation Payoff:** Any 10-engineer AI startup running agentic evaluation or synthetic data pipelines easily processes $100\text{B tokens/yr}$ ($\approx 274\text{M tokens/day}$). At $100\text{B tokens/yr}$, open-weight compute costs $\$83,610/\text{yr}$ offshore versus $\$4,875,000/\text{yr}$ (plus $\$120,000$ compliance) on closed domestic APIs. Even after giving up the $\$600,000/\text{yr}$ combined hub and relocation premium, re-domiciling the company outside the United States saves **$+\$4,311,390/\text{yr}$**. No seed startup raising a $\$4\text{M}$ round can stay in Delaware or California if staying requires handing $120\%$ of its entire equity raise to an incumbent API monopoly every twelve months.

We have seen this exact movie before. During the 1990s "Crypto Wars," when the US government classified strong encryption (`>40-bit` keys) as munitions under the Arms Export Control Act (`22 U.S.C. § 2778`), it did not stop global encryption adoption—it simply drove cryptographic R&D, security software teams, and open-standard design (including the Belgian-designed AES/Rijndael cipher) to Europe until Washington capitulated and relaxed `15 C.F.R. § 742.15` in 2000 (`65 Fed. Reg. 2492`).

And crucially, **there is no physical way for the state to plug this exit valve short of a literal lockdown**:

- **You Cannot Lock Researchers Inside the Country (*Kent v. Dulles*):** In *Kent v. Dulles*, 357 U.S. 116, 125 (1958), and *Aptheker v. Secretary of State*, 378 U.S. 500, 505–06 (1964), the Supreme Court held that the right of citizens to travel abroad is a fundamental Fifth Amendment liberty that cannot be stripped to enforce ideological or industrial controls. Short of revoking computer scientists' passports at the airport or building a national Great Firewall to block domestic engineers from `SSH`-ing into offshore GPU clusters in Zurich or Toronto, the US government cannot stop researchers and startups from moving their workloads—or themselves—across borders.
- **You Cannot Police Floating-Point Matrices on Private Silicon (`U.S. Const. amend. IV`):** A 671B-parameter model quantized to `FP8` ($671\text{ GB}$) or `INT4` ($335.5\text{ GB}$) fits on a single $0.25\text{-gram}$ microSD card smaller than a fingernail ($2,684\text{ GB/g}$ storage density) and downloads over a $10\text{ Gbps}$ fiber link in $268.4\text{ to }536.8\text{ seconds}$. Once those weights sit on an air-gapped workstation or enterprise server rack, the only way for federal agents to know *which* floating-point numbers are being multiplied inside VRAM would be mandatory, warrantless kernel-level spyware on every GPU in America—a textbook violation of the Fourth Amendment ban on general warrants.

## 5. Where the Real Regulatory Equilibrium Lands

Because Washington cannot outlaw local matrix multiplication without violating federal statutes, the First Amendment, and basic industrial arithmetic, actual US policy is settling into a strict four-part perimeter:

1. **Federal Agency & Procurement Exclusions (`NDAA § 889` Model):** Under `Pub. L. 115-232 § 889` (`FAR 52.204-25`), the government can bar *executive agencies* and *federal defense contractors* from running foreign-origin software on government networks:
   > "Section 889(a)(1)(A) of the John S. McCain National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2019 (Pub. L. 115-232) prohibits the head of an executive agency on or after August 13, 2019, from procuring or obtaining, or extending or renewing a contract to procure or obtain, any equipment, system, or service that uses covered telecommunications equipment or services as a substantial or essential component of any system..." — `FAR 52.204-25(b)(1)`
2. **Hosted Foreign Endpoint Bans (Data Egress Only):** Washington can restrict US firms from sending sensitive data to *hosted API servers physically located in China*. That rule does zero damage to a US company running the exact same open weights air-gapped on domestic AWS, Azure, or on-prem H100s.
3. **Physical Silicon & HBM Export Controls:** Because bits cannot be recalled once published, BIS enforcement doubles down on physical EUV lithography, HBM stacks, and semiconductor manufacturing equipment (`15 C.F.R. Part 744`).
4. **Private API Rate-Limiting:** Frontier labs are left to police their own API egress with KYC gates and classifier filters rather than using the Department of Justice to outlaw low-cost linear algebra.

Open-weight distillation is a one-way thermodynamic ratchet. Once frontier reasoning is compressed into open weights, any country that tries to ban its own industries from running those weights is simply sanctioning itself.
