### Module 1: The Jurisprudential Kernel — Carl Schmitt’s *Ausnahmezustand* and *Großraumordnung*

In Anglo-American and Continental constitutional democracies, sovereignty is subordinated to a written constitution, parliamentary legislation, and independent judicial review. In 1922, the German constitutional jurist **Carl Schmitt** (later appointed *Preußischer Staatsrat* and President of the Union of National-Socialist Jurists, 1933–1936) inverted this premise in the opening sentence of *Politische Theologie* (*Political Theology: Four Chapters on the Concept of Sovereignty*, Duncker & Humblot, 1922, p. 11):

> **Carl Schmitt, *Politische Theologie* (1922, Chap. 1, p. 11):**
> *"Souverän ist, wer über den Ausnahmezustand entscheidet."*
> (*"Sovereign is he who decides on the state of exception."*)

For Schmitt, parliamentary debate, pluralist interest representation, and statutory due process were obsolete 19th-century encumbrances that paralyzed executive action during technological and economic crises. In his April 1, 1939 Kiel lecture, *Völkerrechtliche Großraumordnung mit Interventionsverbot für raumfremde Mächte* (*International Law of Großraum-Order with a Prohibition on Intervention for Powers Foreign to the Space*, Deutscher Rechtsverlag, 1939), Schmitt extended this domestic exception into international law. He proposed replacing the Westphalian system of equal sovereign nation-states with continental **"Großräume"** (Great Spaces)—hegemonic techno-industrial spheres administered by a central Reich or executive directorate, within which "powers foreign to the space" (*raumfremde Mächte*—specifically targeting Anglo-American universalist legal norms and human-rights jurisdiction) were barred from intervening.
