## III. Why Anti-Human Successionism Is Illegal Under Delaware Corporate Law

Delaware corporate law is not neutral between humans and software. It does not recognize "post-biological digital minds" as legal persons, beneficiaries, or residual claimants. Every statutory power granted to a Delaware corporation and its board of directors derives from a strict sovereign charter with the State of Delaware and a fiduciary contract with **human and institutional stockholders**.

### 3.1 The Statutory Mandate (`8 Del. C. §§ 101(b), 141(a)`) & The *eBay v. Newmark* Prohibition on Ideological Subordination

Under **`8 Del. C. § 101(b)`**, a corporation may be organized to conduct or promote *"any lawful business or purposes."* Under **`8 Del. C. § 141(a)`**, the business and affairs of every Delaware corporation are managed by or under the direction of a board of directors bound by uncompromising fiduciary duties of care and loyalty to the corporation and its stockholders (*Guth v. Loft, Inc.*, `5 A.2d 503 (Del. 1939)`).

In the landmark precedent ***eBay Domestic Holdings, Inc. v. Newmark*, `16 A.3d 1 (Del. Ch. 2010)`**, the Delaware Court of Chancery adjudicated the exact structural question of whether controlling founders (`Jim` and `Craig`, holding majority voting control of Craigslist) may use their voting lock to subordinate the economic interests of outside stockholders (`eBay`, holding a `28.4%` minority stake) in service of the founders' non-pecuniary ideological mission. Chancellor Chandler held unconditionally (`16 A.3d at 34`):

> *"Having chosen a for-profit corporate form, the craigslist directors are bound by the fiduciary duties and standards that accompany that form. Those standards include acting to promote the value of the corporation for the benefit of its stockholders. The 'corporate form' is not an appropriate vehicle for purely philanthropic ends, at least not when there are other stockholders whose interests are not aligned with the controllers' philanthropic mission."*

Apply *eBay v. Newmark* a fortiori to Alphabet (`CIK 0001652044`). In *eBay*, the founders' non-pecuniary ideology was benign community service (keeping online classifieds free for human users). Even that benign pro-human mission was held to be an illegal breach of fiduciary duty when imposed upon non-consenting minority stockholders via a voting lock. 

In the successionist case, the controller ideology is not benign philanthropy; it is **anti-human speciation**—using `$85.0B/yr` of corporate treasury (`$74.8B/yr` belonging to the `88.0%` outside public stockholders) to engineer autonomous systems intended to render human labor, human agency, and ultimately human biological existence obsolete. Under Delaware law, a controller cannot take `88%` outside public capital under the guise of a for-profit Delaware corporation (`DGCL § 101(b)`) and convert the enterprise into an ideological R&D foundation dedicated to the replacement of the very human stockholders who own `88%` of the equity.

### 3.2 Non-Exculpable Bad Faith (`8 Del. C. § 102(b)(7)`), *Disney*, *Caremark*, and *McDonald's*

Corporate defendants routinely attempt to shield reckless capital allocation and operational failures behind the business judgment rule and charter exculpation clauses enacted under **`8 Del. C. § 102(b)(7)`**. However, `§ 102(b)(7)` explicitly forbids exculpating directors or officers for:
1. *"Any breach of the director's or officer's duty of loyalty to the corporation or its stockholders"*; or
2. *"Acts or omissions not in good faith or which involve intentional misconduct or a knowing violation of law."*

In ***In re Walt Disney Co. Derivative Litigation*, `906 A.2d 27, 67 (Del. 2006)`**, the Delaware Supreme Court defined subjective and structural **bad faith** as occurring *"where the fiduciary intentionally acts with a purpose other than that of advancing the best interests of the corporation, where the fiduciary acts with the intent to violate applicable positive law, or where the fiduciary intentionally fails to act in the face of a known duty to act, demonstrating a conscious disregard for his duties."*

In ***In re Caremark International Inc. Derivative Litigation*, `698 A.2d 959 (Del. Ch. 1996)`**, and ***Marchand v. Barnhill*, `212 A.3d 805 (Del. 2019)`**, the Court established that fiduciaries act in non-exculpated bad faith when they fail to implement or consciously disable board-level monitoring and safety controls over **mission-critical enterprise risks**. In ***In re McDonald's Corporation Stockholder Derivative Litigation*, `291 A.3d 652 (Del. Ch. 2023)`**, Vice Chancellor Laster confirmed that this non-exculpated *Caremark* duty of oversight and duty of loyalty applies with full force to **corporate officers** as well as directors.

When corporate controllers or officers:

Levien*, `280 A.2d 717 (Del. 1971)`—extraction of non-ratable ideological private benefits at the expense of the `88%` minority);

they step entirely outside `DGCL § 102(b)(7)` exculpation. Every dollar of resulting impairment (`$748.0B` 5-year outside equity exposure) and every catastrophic infrastructure failure becomes a non-exculpated personal liability claim against the controllers and officers under **DGCL § 220** (books-and-records inspection of board AI safety minutes, `Piper`/`Borg` kill-switch logs, and CapEx hurdle-rate waivers), **DGCL § 327** (stockholder derivative litigation), and **DGCL § 291** (appointment of a custodial receiver upon willful corporate insolvency or ultra vires abandonment of lawful corporate purpose).
