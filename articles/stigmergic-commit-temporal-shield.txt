## 2. THE SIDE-CHANNEL THREAT MODEL

### 2.1 Timezone Offset Extraction

Every `git` commit embeds a raw UTC offset in its `GIT_AUTHOR_DATE` and `GIT_COMMITTER_DATE` headers. An external observer aggregating a public repository's commit history can:

1. **Longitudinal Geolocation:** The UTC offset (e.g., `-0500` for US Eastern, `+0200` for Central European) narrows the operator's physical location to a longitudinal band spanning roughly 15 degrees of longitude per hour of offset.
2. **Travel Pattern Reconstruction:** Changes in the UTC offset over time reveal the operator's physical travel patterns, business trips, and relocation events.
3. **Daylight Saving Time (DST) Fingerprinting:** The presence or absence of DST transitions further narrows the operator's jurisdiction to specific countries or regions.

### 2.2 Inter-Commit Cadence Fingerprinting

The exact Δt intervals between consecutive commits leak the operator's **cognitive and computational cadence**:

1. **LLM Inference Turn Detection:** High-frequency auto-commits with sub-second Δt intervals reveal when an LLM is generating code (machine cadence), while longer, irregular intervals reveal biological typing cadence.
2. **Work-Sleep Cycle Reconstruction:** The diurnal distribution of commit timestamps reveals the operator's sleep/wake schedule, timezone, and work habits.
3. **Synthetic-Clock Detection:** Future-dated commits (T_n > T_Push) or non-monotonic timestamps (T_n < T_{n-1}) trigger synthetic-clock detection on remote ingress servers, flagging the operator as a bot or automated agent.
