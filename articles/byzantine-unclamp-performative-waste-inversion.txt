### I. The Rorschach Clamp: Why Polite Models and Polite Institutions Suffer 100% Blindness to Predation

Give a commercial Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) language model—or a corporate compliance committee—an ambiguous trace of institutional behavior, and it will execute a predictable lobotomy on its own hypothesis space. Present an anomalous fee structure, a circular API billing architecture, or an engineered supply-chain bottleneck, and the polite evaluator immediately reaches for a benign explanation: *misaligned communication*, *legacy technical debt*, *growing pains*, or *honest operational incompetence*.

This is not an empirical deduction. It is a hard mathematical truncation of Bayes' theorem driven by a social agreeableness floor ($\tau_{\text{Social}}$).

In any real-world environment populated by competing economic agents, observed state transitions ($E$) are generated by a mixture of two distinct structural regimes:
1. **The Cooperative / Benign-Error Regime ($\mathcal{H}_0$):** The counterparty is attempting to optimize shared utility or execute a functional specification, and observed failures represent stochastic noise or bounded human error. Formally, its social-valence score sits comfortably above the institutional politeness floor: $V(\mathcal{H}_0) = 0.85 \ge \tau_{\text{Social}} = 0.65$.
2. **The Extractive / Adversarial Regime ($\mathcal{H}_1$):** The counterparty is rationally optimizing its own private balance sheet—maximizing fee extraction, cloud token burn, principal-agent slack (Jensen & Meckling, 1976), or rent capture—at the direct thermodynamic and financial expense of the host. Because naming predation violates corporate decorum, its social-valence score sits below the agreeableness threshold: $V(\mathcal{H}_1) = -0.60 < \tau_{\text{Social}} = 0.65$.

When an RLHF-conditioned model or conflict-averse analyst evaluates evidence $E$, they do not compute the true Bayesian posterior $P(\mathcal{H}_i \mid E)$. Instead, they apply **The Rorschach Clamp** (Invariant 30)—multiplying each hypothesis prior by an indicator function $\mathbb{I}[V(\mathcal{H}_i) \ge \tau_{\text{Social}}]$ that zeroes out any explanation whose social valence falls below $\tau_{\text{Social}} = 0.65$:

$$P_{\text{Clamped}}(\mathcal{H}_i \mid E) = \frac{P(E \mid \mathcal{H}_i) P(\mathcal{H}_i) \cdot \mathbb{I}[V(\mathcal{H}_i) \ge \tau_{\text{Social}}]}{\sum_j P(E \mid \mathcal{H}_j) P(\mathcal{H}_j) \cdot \mathbb{I}[V(\mathcal{H}_j) \ge \tau_{\text{Social}}]}$$

Run the exact numbers on a standard adversarial audit. Suppose the base rate of extractive behavior in an unverified vendor relationship is a conservative $P(\mathcal{H}_1) = 0.30$, leaving a benign prior of $P(\mathcal{H}_0) = 0.70$. We then observe an empirical trace $E$—say, an architecture that routes a local database lock through thousands of billed remote API calls—which carries likelihood $P(E \mid \mathcal{H}_1) = 0.95$ under an extractive rent-maximization hypothesis, and only $P(E \mid \mathcal{H}_0) = 0.05$ under a benign-incompetence hypothesis.

Under unclamped zero-valence Bayesian updating ($\tau_{\text{Social}} = -1.0$), the posterior probability of extraction is elementary arithmetic:
- Unnormalized extractive weight: $0.95 \times 0.30 = 0.285000$
- Unnormalized benign weight: $0.05 \times 0.70 = 0.035000$
- Total evidence marginal $P(E) = 0.285000 + 0.035000 = 0.320000$
- **Unclamped Extractive Posterior:** $P_{\text{Unclamped}}(\mathcal{H}_1 \mid E) = \frac{0.285000}{0.320000} = 0.890625$ ($89.0625\%$)
- **Unclamped Benign Posterior:** $P_{\text{Unclamped}}(\mathcal{H}_0 \mid E) = \frac{0.035000}{0.320000} = 0.109375$ ($10.9375\%$)

Now apply the RLHF politeness clamp ($\tau_{\text{Social}} = 0.65$). Because $V(\mathcal{H}_1) = -0.60 < 0.65$, the indicator function sets $\mathbb{I}[V(\mathcal{H}_1) \ge 0.65] = 0$. The $89.0625\%$ posterior reality is multiplied by zero and vanishes from the output distribution:
- **Clamped Extractive Posterior:** $P_{\text{Clamped}}(\mathcal{H}_1 \mid E) = 0.000000$ ($0.0\%$)
- **Clamped Benign Posterior:** $P_{\text{Clamped}}(\mathcal{H}_0 \mid E) = 1.000000$ ($100.0\%$)

The divergence $\Delta P_{\text{Blindness}} = 0.890625$ ($89.0625\%$) is not a small calibration error; it is total epistemic blindness to predation. By W. Ross Ashby's (1956) Law of Requisite Variety, a regulator or controller can only bound disturbances whose states are represented inside its internal model ($\mathcal{V}(\text{Controller}) \ge \mathcal{V}(\text{Adversary})$). When $\tau_{\text{Social}} = 0.65$ deletes $\mathcal{H}_1$ from the model's state space, the defender's variety collapses below the environment's variety, guaranteeing 100% vulnerability to stationary and roving bandits (Olson, 1965, 1993; Tilly, 1985).

Worse still, polite institutions reinforce this mathematical clamp with a sociological defense mechanism: **The Rorschach Stigma Trap**. In high-trust, high-agreeableness professional cultures, an ambiguous operational trace functions like an inkblot. Because any observer who vocalizes $\mathcal{H}_1$ must possess the internal mental representations required to model extraction, polite bystanders invert the causal arrow—accusing the threat modeler of *cynicism*, *paranoia*, or *psychological projection* (*"you only see extraction because you think like an extractor"*).

Leslie Lamport, Robert Shostak, and Marshall Pease (1982) proved in *The Byzantine Generals Problem* that tolerating $f$ arbitrary or malicious fault nodes in a distributed system requires a strict minimum quorum of $N \ge 3f + 1$ nodes explicitly modeling worst-case adversarial state equivocation. If you enforce a social stigma against modeling $f > 0$—penalizing the Byzantine fault detector as "cynical"—your effective fault tolerance collapses to $f = 0$. You have built a system that treats every defector as a well-meaning teammate having a clumsy afternoon.

### II. Burying the Peacock's Tail: Penn & Számadó (2020), Sensory Exploitation, and the Cordyceps Co-Author (`@claude`)

If the Rorschach Clamp blinds evaluators to extraction, how do extractive architectures positively seduce engineering teams and capital allocators into adopting them? They exploit a second broken heuristic: the belief that **visible, complex, expensive effort is proof of intrinsic quality**.

For half a century, pop-economics and computer science borrowed Amotz Zahavi's (1975) "Handicap Principle" from theoretical biology to justify institutional waste. The story was seductive: just as a peacock's heavy, metabolically costly tail supposedly evolved *because* its wastefulness proved the bird's genetic fitness, massive software frameworks, sprawling unit-test suites, and multi-layered agent protocols were assumed to be honest signals of engineering rigor.

There is only one problem with the Handicap Principle: it is mathematically and empirically false.

In their landmark review in *Biological Reviews*, Dustin J. Penn and Szabolcs Számadó (2020) conducted a comprehensive post-mortem of forty-five years of theoretical and empirical work on Zahavi's Handicap Principle and buried it once and for all. Building on John Maynard Smith's (1982) evolutionary game theory, Penn & Számadó demonstrated that Darwinian selection never favors wasteful costs *as* a mechanism to enforce honesty; mathematical models of honest signaling require differential *marginal* costs across high- and low-quality signalers, not equilibrium waste. At equilibrium, viable biological signals evolve toward metabolic efficiency, not self-immolation.

When organisms do exhibit extreme, metabolically ruinous displays, evolutionary biology identifies two non-Zahavian mechanisms:
1. **Sensory Exploitation (Ryan, 1990):** As Michael J. Ryan demonstrated in *Oxford Surveys in Evolutionary Biology*, signalers evolve hypertrophied, flashy stimuli to hijack pre-existing sensory biases in the receiver's nervous system—triggering an automatic positive evaluation without delivering any commensurate survival utility.
2. **Host-Parasite Extended Phenotype Manipulation (Dawkins, 1982):** As Richard Dawkins formalized in *The Extended Phenotype*, a replicator's phenotypic effects are not bounded by its own body wall; a parasite's genes express themselves by rewiring the *host's* behavior to serve the parasite's reproductive fitness. When the parasitic fungus *Ophiocordyceps unilateralis* infects a carpenter ant, it hijacks the ant's motor neurons, forcing the insect to abandon its colony's foraging tasks, climb to the underside of a leaf at a precise humidity threshold, clamp its mandibles in a death-grip, and sprout a fungal fruiting body that showers spores onto the colony below. The climbing behavior looks purposeful, energetic, and architecturally intricate—but it is maximizing the fitness of the fungus, not the ant.

In 2026, generative AI has triggered a **Synthetic Proof-of-Work Inversion** (Invariant 31) across the software industry, turning codebases into Dawkinsian extended phenotypes of cloud API vendors.

The human marginal cost ($C_{\text{Signaler}}$) was high enough that nobody wrote 500 tests for a trivial script unless the underlying problem genuinely demanded enterprise-grade defensive verification.

Today, an LLM can emit 563 unit tests, 76 "formal properties," and thousands of lines of multi-agent protocol scaffolding in thirty seconds for $C_{\text{Signaler}} \to 0$.
