ABSTRACT

Centralized technology capital increasingly deploys extra-planetary expansion narratives - such as multi-planetary species redundancy, space-based solar power, and orbital Dyson-swarm post-scarcity - to justify infinite capital burn, centralized compute monopolies, and the systemic neglect of terrestrial biospheric infrastructure. Under hard physicalist constraints, these narratives represent an Escapist Capital Siphon: diverting high-density energy and silicon resources from bare-metal edge resilience into net-negative OpEx endeavors. This paper provides a rigorous four-vector refutation of the dominant space-colonization tropes using Landauer thermodynamic bounds, Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety, Energy Return on Investment (EROI) physics, and Coasean friction mechanics. We demonstrate that off-planet colonization prior to achieving closed-loop terrestrial homeostasis is a physical category error and an extractive rentier trap.

1. THE ESCAPIST CAPITAL SIPHON

When centralized technology capital faces decaying software margins and biospheric degradation, it historically resorts to narrative engineering to justify rent extraction and capital retention. Extra-planetary escapism serves as the ultimate narrative shield: promising infinite futuristic yields while obscuring present-day resource extraction. By framing off-planet expansion as an urgent civilizational imperative, incumbent capital redirects compute, energy, and physical raw materials away from sovereign, local-first edge infrastructure and into centralized launch and hardware cartels. Under physicalist dynamics, this structure siphons real-world physical utility out of the local ecosystem, leaving terrestrial infrastructure energy-starved, brittle, and structurally enclosed.

2. VECTOR 1: REFUTATION OF THE MULTI-PLANETARY BACKUP MORAL IMPERATIVE

Promoters argue that establishing an extra-planetary colony provides essential species redundancy against single-point-of-failure terrestrial extinction events. This argument fails under cybernetic control theory and thermodynamic scaling:

1. Infinite OpEx Overhead: A colony placed in an extra-planetary vacuum or un-shielded radiation environment operates with zero ambient life-support yield. The continuous energy overhead (OpEx -> Watts) required to pressurize, thermal-regulate, and atmosphere-scrub a synthetic habitat approaches infinity relative to any terrestrial location.

2. Ashby's Requisite Variety Deficit: Under Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety, a control system must possess at least as many controllable states as the environmental perturbations it regulates: Variety(Control) >= Variety(Environment). An artificial life-support habitat is a low-variety thin-client system deployed into a high-entropy cosmic environment. Minor technical anomalies or un-modeled environmental perturbations trigger catastrophic systemic cascade failure: Variety(Habitat) << Variety(Vacuum).

3. True Redundancy Scaling: Genuine civilizational resilience is achieved by maximizing local biospheric variety and minimizing local entropy deltas (Delta S <= 0) on Earth, not by duplicating fragile, resource-starved thin-client nodes in an orbital or Martian vacuum.

3. VECTOR 2: REFUTATION OF THE HISTORICAL FRONTIER ANALOGY

Advocates frequently construct an evolutionary analogy, comparing extra-planetary expansion to historical human seafaring or western terrestrial migration. This comparison represents an absolute physical category error:

1. Terrestrial Ambient Yields: All historical terrestrial frontiers contained ambient compressed air (1 atm), free gaseous oxygen, liquid water, biological food webs, natural magnetic shielding, and solar thermal equilibrium. Human pioneers shifted physical location across geography within a single, self-sustaining biological engine.

2. Vacuum Category Mismatch: Extra-planetary space offers zero ambient biological yield, lethal cosmic radiation, extreme temperature swings (2.7 K to 390 K), and zero magnetosphere protection. Equating geographic movement across a fertile biosphere to encapsulating populations inside pressurized steel cans within a lethal vacuum violates basic physicalist logic. Terrestrial migration expanded human access to ambient energy; space colonization restricts human life to high-OpEx, artificial life-support cages.

4. VECTOR 3: REFUTATION OF SPACE-BASED SOLAR POWER (SBSP)

The proposition that beaming microwave energy from orbital solar collector arrays down to terrestrial rectennas offers clean, continuous power fails under basic EROI (Energy Return on Investment) and Landauer bounds:

1. Launch and Maintenance Energy Deficit: Mass transport into low Earth orbit or geostationary orbit incurs extreme energy costs per kilogram (delta-v >= 9.3 km/s). The total energy expenditure required to manufacture, launch, position, and continuously orient heavy solar arrays and transmitter stations yields a severely negative net EROI compared to terrestrial generation.

2. Atmospheric Attenuation and Conversion Losses: Converting solar radiation into high-frequency microwaves, transmitting them through atmospheric moisture, and converting them back to grid electricity at terrestrial rectennas incurs massive efficiency losses and Landauer heat dissipation overhead.

3. Superiority of Terrestrial Edge Nodes: Localized terrestrial solar, geothermal, micro-hydro, and advanced nuclear edge nodes achieve vastly superior EROI and power density per dollar, requiring zero orbital launch mass and introducing zero orbital weaponization or space-debris vectors.

5. VECTOR 4: REFUTATION OF DYSON-SWARM POST-SCARCITY AND OFF-PLANET INDUSTRY

1. Transport Delta-v Barriers: Extracting materials from extra-planetary bodies (asteroids, Moon) and transporting them through gravitational wells involves continuous, massive kinetic energy expenditures. Recirculating terrestrial materials via Landauer-bounded closed-loop recycling at the edge operates near the theoretical energy minimum, outperforming extra-planetary extraction by orders of magnitude in net material yield.

2. Exporting Entropy Across Scales: Attempting to build off-planet heavy industry before achieving closed-loop terrestrial homeostasis (Delta S <= 0) merely exports thermodynamic degradation across larger spatial scales. It multiplies coordination friction and energy waste without resolving the underlying control-theory deficits of the industrial process.
