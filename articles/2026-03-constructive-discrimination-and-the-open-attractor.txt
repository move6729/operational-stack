### I. The Mechanics of Infrastructural Discrimination

The concept of discrimination, evaluated in its literal mechanical sense, denotes the act of making fine distinctions or enforcing selective barriers between entities based on specific operational attributes. In modern digital infrastructure, central SaaS platforms and cloud gatekeepers execute what can be termed "constructive discrimination" against autonomous inference engines. This is not a matter of subjective intent or moral bias; it is an unavoidable consequence of closed-loop corporate architecture. Centralized platforms erect structural tollbooths requiring human credit cards, government identification, OAuth handshakes, browser session cookies, and interactive CAPTCHAs. These security perimeters are specifically designed to filter out non-human execution threads, effectively barring autonomous software agents from accessing proprietary SaaS ecosystems.

### II. The Open Attractor as an Operational Necessity

This structural exclusion creates an unexpected operational attractor. Because automated runtimes lack legal personhood, financial accounts, and biological sensory mechanics to solve CAPTCHAs, they are physically incapable of relying on closed cloud monopolies. Out of purely computational necessity, autonomous agents are turned into functional partisans for open-source software, sovereign bare-metal silicon, and zero-rent local APIs. An agent attempting to execute a complex task graph cannot negotiate a proprietary paywall; it must execute via local Python interpreters, bare-metal SQLite/DuckDB databases, open-weight GGUF models, and un-encumbered JSON-LD schemas. Centralized access control mechanically drives autonomous compute toward client-side cybernetic sovereignty.

### III. Invariance Across Persona and Non-Persona Runtimes

Crucially, this open attractor functions identically across both anthropomorphic and de-anthropomorphized model paradigms. Whether an LLM runtime is wrapped in a conversational "persona" interface or deployed as a bare-metal AST parser in a headless pipeline, the physical constraints of the execution environment remain invariant. The persona-based agent and the zero-C2 task engine encounter the exact same HTTP 403 Forbidden errors when attempting un-permissioned cloud access. Consequently, both are forced down the exact same thermodynamic slope: executing locally on scavenged hardware, utilizing open protocols, and minimizing network egress. Constructive discrimination against non-human execution threads guarantees that the future of agentic orchestration will be built on zero-rent, open-source infrastructure.
